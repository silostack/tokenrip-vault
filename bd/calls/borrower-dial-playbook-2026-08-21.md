# Borrower Dial Playbook

One page to have open while dialing. Everything here serves the sprint: 150 net-new in-box companies contacted, 3 confirmed needs, 1 credit submission by Sep 26. Every reached call also feeds the intelligence layer, so a "no" is worth logging as carefully as a "yes."

## 1. What this call is

This is a broker call, not a software pitch. You are an origination firm that saw a public event and is offering the company competing financing terms at no cost to them. Borrowers take these calls because the offer is real and costs them nothing. You never mention Quintel the product, the scorer, or signals as a concept. You mention one specific fact about their business and ask one question.

The call has two jobs, in this order:

1. Find out whether there is an equipment purchase behind the event and how it is being paid for.
2. Leave with the two timing dates, whatever the answer to (1) was.

Everything else is optional. A 90-second call that produces a clean disposition and two dates is a full success.

Goal for each reached call is extraction, not explanation. If you are talking more than a third of the time, you are losing the call.

## 2. Before you dial (60 seconds, capped)

The dial sheet gives you the company, the trigger and its "why," prior UCC secured party, the briefing, and a contact. Read it once and fix three things in your head:

- The fact you will lead with, in plain English. Not "a USAspending award record," but "the $14M Army Corps contract you picked up last month."
- The equipment that fact plausibly implies. Award → what do they need to perform it. Permit → what goes into the building. UCC maturing → what was on the original filing. New authority → trucks.
- Who you want. Equipment financing decisions sit with the owner or CFO at small and mid-size companies, and with controller, VP finance, or VP ops at larger ones. Titles that cannot commit money get a referral flip, not a pitch.

If the contact record looks wrong (dead website, revenue way off, contact at a different entity), fix it in the data tool before dialing or skip it. Do not burn a dial on a bad record.

Do not research beyond this. The call is the research.

## 3. The call, step by step

### Gatekeeper

Warm, first names, ask for help, never pitch.

> "Hi, it's Simon. I'm hoping you can point me the right way. Who handles equipment purchases and financing there, is that still [Name]?"

If screened:

> "It's about the [fact], and financing options on the equipment side. Is [Name] better by phone or email?"

Whatever they give you (right name, direct line, "she's in after two") goes in the note. Thank them by name.

### Opener (about 15 seconds)

> "[Name], it's Simon with Quintel. We're an equipment finance origination firm, we work with lenders that finance [asset class] for companies your size. I saw [fact]. Quick question, is there equipment going into that, and if so, have you lined up how you're paying for it?"

Then stop talking. Whatever they say next is the call.

Notes on the opener:

- Lead with the fact, not with who you are. The fact is why they stay on the line.
- "Origination firm" is the honest description and it's what borrowers understand. If they ask "are you a broker?" the answer is yes: "Yes, we place equipment deals with lenders. The lender pays us, you don't."
- Never say software, platform, AI, signals, or data. You saw a public record; that's all.

### Signal-specific openers

Swap the fact line. Everything else stays.

| Trigger                            | Fact line                                                                                                               | Equipment it implies                                           |
| ---------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Federal / state contract award     | "saw you were awarded the [$X] [agency] contract for [scope]"                                                           | whatever performs the contract: trucks, machinery, IT, medical |
| Building permit / expansion        | "saw the permit for the [new facility / expansion] on [street]"                                                         | production lines, racking, HVAC, fleet to serve it             |
| UCC maturity (renewals lane)       | "looks like the equipment financing you set up with [secured party] back in [year] is coming up on the end of its term" | the original collateral; refi, buyout, or replacement          |
| New DOT / operating authority      | "saw you got your [MC / operating] authority in [month]"                                                                | trucks, trailers                                               |
| Hiring surge / new location / news | "saw you're [opening the Dallas location / hiring 40 technicians]"                                                      | whatever a new site or crew uses                               |

For UCC maturity, the question changes: "Are you planning to buy it out, refinance it, or replace the equipment? And have you had a conversation with [secured party] about it yet?"

### The core question

Whatever the opener, you are trying to learn four things in this order. Ask them as conversation, not as a form.

1. Is there equipment involved? What, roughly how much, new or used?
2. How is it being paid for? Cash, bank line, the vendor's financing, a broker, a lender you already use, or not decided yet. Already signed?
3. When does your current equipment financing mature? (Who's the lender, what month and year.)
4. When is your next capex cycle or budget window?

Questions 3 and 4 are asked on every reached call, including when the answer to (1) is "there's no equipment." A "no equipment" call with two dates is a dated future call. A "no equipment" call without them is a dead lead.

A natural way in to 3 and 4 when there's no need today:

> "Fair enough. Two quick things so I'm not bugging you at the wrong time: do you have any equipment financing on the books right now, and roughly when does that come up? And when do you normally look at next year's equipment budget?"

### Branches

**A. Need is live and financing isn't settled.** This is the deal. Slow down.

> "Okay, that's exactly what we do. Tell me about the equipment."

Collect Tier 3 (spec, vendor, quote in hand, cost, delivery date, structure they want, who else is quoting, decision date, years in business, entity name). Then:

> "Here's what happens next. I'll have a lender that does [asset class] at this size look at it and come back with terms. I'll need [the quote / a one-page summary] from you. Can you send that today, and can I call you [day] to walk through what comes back?"

Hand to the lender the same day. Fee stated to the lender per the paper in place (Onset 0.5–1%, Wingspire 0.5%, Envision 1%; VFI-box deals go to VFI only).

**B. Need exists, already financed.** Still valuable. Find out who, when they signed, the structure, and whether the vendor steered it.

> "Makes sense. Who'd you end up going with? Were they the only ones you looked at, or did you shop it? ... And when does that one run through?"

Ask if they'd want a market read on terms for that asset class when it comes up. Log permission. This is the incumbent map and the calendar.

**C. No equipment behind the event.** Ask what the event actually meant (the award was services-only, the permit is a tenant improvement, the UCC was a working-capital lien). One sentence. Then the two timing questions, then out. This is the calibration data that makes the scorer honest. Log it as a clean no, not as "not interested."

**D. Wrong person.** "Who would know? Can I say you pointed me to them?" Get a name and a direct line. Log the referral and dial the new name the same block.

**E. Not now.** Get a date, not a vibe. "When should I come back to this? Is [month] right?" Log the date as a date.

**F. Do not contact.** Log it, honor it, move on.

### Close (every call, 15 seconds)

Three permission questions, whichever apply:

> "Is it okay if I follow up by email? ... If something in your space comes up that's worth knowing, can I send it? ... And if a lender has terms worth seeing for [asset] around [maturity month], you'd want that?"

Then: "Appreciate the time, [Name]." Hang up. Log before the next dial.

## 4. Objections

Short answers. Answer, then ask a question back. Never defend.

| They say | You say |
|---|---|
| "How did you get my number / hear about this?" | "It's public record, [the award / permit / filing] is published. We read those so we can call companies at the right time instead of cold." Then back to the question. |
| "We have a bank for that." | "Most companies we talk to do. Banks are great on the line, often not on equipment terms or advance rates. Worth a second quote when the time comes? ... Who's the bank, and when does the current equipment note mature?" |
| "The vendor handles the financing." | "Common. Vendor programs are convenient and sometimes the best deal, sometimes not. When you take theirs, do you ever get a comparison? ... What's the vendor?" |
| "We pay cash." | "Got it. Then there's no deal here today, and that's fine. Two quick things for timing..." (go to the two dates). |
| "Send me something." | "Happy to. What should it be about, financing on [the equipment] or a general read on terms? ... And is there actually a purchase coming, or is this a polite no? Either's fine, I'd rather know." |
| "Are you a broker?" | "Yes. We place equipment deals with lenders that do [asset class]. You don't pay us, the lender does." |
| "What's in it for you?" | "The lender pays us a fee when something funds. You get competing terms without shopping it yourself." |
| "Not interested." | "No problem. One thing so I don't call at the wrong time: when does your current equipment financing run through?" Then out. |
| "Who are the lenders?" | "Depends on the asset and size. For [asset] at [size] it'd be an independent that does that all day, not a generalist. If there's a real purchase I'll name them." Don't name lenders before a need exists. |
| "We already have a broker." | "Who? ... Do they bring you terms or do you bring them deals? ... When's the next thing you'd run through them?" Log the broker name. |

Two things you never do on this call: quote a rate, and promise an approval.

## 5. Data mining: what every call can teach the system

The sprint needs deals. The intelligence layer needs labels, dates, and corrections. Every reached call produces some of the second even when it produces none of the first. This section is what to listen for and what to ask when the conversation allows, grouped by what it teaches.

### Signal calibration (does the trigger mean anything?)

- Was the event real and did they know about it? A "what award?" is a resolution error or a stale record; log it as such, not as "no need."
- Did the event imply equipment? If not, why not (services-only award, tenant improvement, working-capital UCC). The "why not" is a rule the scorer can learn.
- What preceded the event? "Did you order the equipment before or after the award came through?" If equipment was bought before the public event, the signal is lagging and some earlier signal (bid submission, hiring, a permit) was the real one. This is the leading-signal question and it's worth asking every time there's a purchase.
- How many other people called on this? "Has anyone else reached out since the award?" Measures how contested the signal is and how fast.
- Lag: when did they actually decide, versus when the record appeared. Month is enough.

### The timing calendar (the asset no lender has)

- Current equipment financing: lender, approximate balance, maturity month/year, structure (lease/loan, FMV/TRAC/$1-out), per facility if they volunteer it.
- Next capex window: month or quarter, and what drives it (fiscal year, fleet age, contract cycle, growth plan).
- Replacement cycle for their core asset: "How often do you turn the trucks / machines?" Gives a recurring date without a filing.
- Re-contact date, as a date, for every not-now.

### The incumbent map (who finances what, where)

- Who financed the last purchase, and was it shopped or taken from the vendor?
- Primary bank, whether equipment runs through it, and whether they're happy with it.
- Broker relationship, if any: name, how they found them, what they bring.
- Vendor/dealer they buy from and whether that dealer has a captive program. Dealers are a future channel and a future signal source.

### Decision process (how a deal would actually move)

- Who decides on equipment financing: name, title, and who else weighs in (owner, CFO, board, PE sponsor).
- Documents they typically provide and how fast. App-only comfort versus full financials.
- Guarantor posture: will the owner sign personally? (Affects which lender it fits.)
- How long the last one took from quote to funding, and what they would have changed.

### Borrower-side pain (product discovery, by asking)

Ask once, then do not pitch during the answer:

> "Last time you financed equipment, what was the annoying part?"

Listen for: took too long, bank wouldn't do the asset, rate was opaque, advance rate too low, vendor pushed one lender, had to call eight places, paperwork. Write the phrase they used, verbatim.

Then, if there is room:

> "If someone sent you a short read on what terms look like for [asset] a couple of months before your financing comes up, is that useful, or is that noise?"

A yes is a borrower product found without building it. A no is a product not built. Both are answers.

### Data quality (fix the companies that matter)

- Correct name, title, direct line, email for the decision-maker.
- Revenue band, employee count, locations, ownership (PE-backed and sponsor name if so), website, main phone.
- Entity confusion: is this the legal entity that would borrow, or a parent/sister? Which one holds the equipment?
- Any other locations or affiliates. Each is a future dial.

### Lender story (what goes to Wingspire, 36th, Alliance)

Every confirmed need, every "already financed with X," and every dated maturity is a line in the ledger you show lenders. Capture enough that a line reads: company, trigger, date contacted, what they said, what they need, when. That sentence is the sales deck.

## 6. How to take notes in the system

Notes attach to the company and the trigger. Structure first, prose second.

**Line 1, codes.** Reached · person (name/title/role: decides/influences/refers) · signal real y/n/partly · equipment y/n + asset + cost band · financing status · disposition · permissions.

**Line 2, dates.** Maturity (lender + MM/YYYY) · capex window (MM/YYYY or Qx YYYY) · re-contact date. Dates as dates, always. If they said "spring," write 04/2027 and flag it approximate.

**Line 3, corrections.** Anything wrong in the record: contact, revenue, entity, website. Be specific so it can be fixed.

**Line 4+, prose.** What they said, in their words. Quote the pain phrase. Note what preceded the event if they told you. Note who else called. Note the incumbent and whether it was shopped.

Tag the script version (b1.0 now) so changes can be compared. Log every dial, including no-answer and voicemail, same block. An unlogged call didn't happen.

Example:

```
REACHED · Maria Lopez, CFO (decides) · signal: yes, known · equip: yes, 3 vac trucks, ~$900K, new · financing: vendor quote in hand, not signed · disp: NEED IN-BOX · perm: email y, lender intro y, market read y
MAT: Ally 06/2027 (2 trucks, $1-out) · CAPEX: Q1 each year, budget locked by Nov · RECONTACT: 08/25 with terms
FIX: direct 555-0142; revenue ~$18M not $4M; entity is Lopez Environmental Services LLC
"Vendor gave us one number and told us that's the program. I'd take a second quote." Award came through 7/9; ordered trucks 7/20, so the award led. Nobody else has called. Last deal: Ally, took 3 weeks, fine. Wants to close by end of Sept for a 10/1 start.
```

## 7. Daily rhythm

- One block, 25–30 dials, mornings local to the prospect (8:00 to 10:00 their time reaches owners and CFOs before the day eats them; 4:00 to 5:30 is second best).
- No answer: voicemail (20 seconds, the fact and a callback number, nothing else), then email within 60 seconds with the same fact and a single question, then a LinkedIn connect. Second dial on day 3 with a second fact if there is one.
- Log before the next dial. Tally at the end of the block: dials, reached, Tier 1 captured, needs, dates collected, corrections made.
- After the block, review the needs and hand off the same day. Confirmed need with no handoff by end of day is a stall.
- Every five reached calls, note what is and isn't landing in the opener. Change one thing, bump the version, log it.

Voicemail:

> "[Name], Simon with Quintel. Saw [fact]. If there's equipment going into that and you haven't settled the financing, we can get you competing terms from lenders that do [asset class], no cost to you. [Number]. I'll also send a two-line email."

Email 1 (under 90 words, send within 60 seconds of the voicemail):

> Subject: [fact, short: "the Army Corps award" / "your 2021 equipment lease"]
>
> [Name], I'm with Quintel, an equipment finance origination firm. I saw [fact]. If there's equipment going into that and the financing isn't locked, we can get you competing terms from lenders that do [asset class] at this size. No cost to you, the lender pays us.
>
> Is there a purchase coming, and if so, have you settled how you're paying for it? Ten minutes by phone is enough.

## 8. Guardrails

- VFI-box deals go to VFI only while Stauss's advisor role stands. Check the box before handing off.
- Routing on overlapping $5–50M deals between Onset and Wingspire is unwritten. Write it before the first handoff that qualifies.
- State broker licensing check (30 minutes) is still open. Do it before taking the first fee, not before the first dial. California CFL applies to the Envision channel specifically.
- Never quote rates, never promise approvals, never name a lender before a need exists.
- Nothing a borrower tells you goes to a lender without the borrower's permission (Tier 1 item 7). In an industry this small, one leaked confidence ends the motion.
- Small-ticket needs (sub-$250K) that surface incidentally go to Envision; they do not get founder follow-up time beyond the handoff.

## 9. What good looks like after week one

- 100+ dials, 25+ reached, 25 Tier 1 records, 25 pairs of dates, at least one need in a lender's hands.
- A list of the five most common "why the signal didn't mean equipment" reasons, which is a scorer fix.
- A first read on which trigger type reaches decision-makers and which doesn't.
- Opener at b1.2 or later, with the change log saying why.

If after 50 reached calls there are zero needs and the signal-real rate is below half, stop and look at the box and the trigger mix before dialing further. That's a data problem, not a phone problem.
