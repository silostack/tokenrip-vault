## Key Intelligence / What Changed

**1. A vendor-driven lessor handed us a buying case for a direct-origination product — and it's one we've never pitched.**
> "If I were to eventually go to an inside sales force that were calling on direct, it would be **to learn to leverage those vendors**… we could go after end users and cold call and see if that works. And then get those first couple deals, take those vendors and try and leverage those vendors — **we did the deal for your customer, why don't we work with you.** That type of stuff. **Going backward.**"

This inverts the pitch. For a vendor-first shop, the end-user deal isn't the revenue — it's the **credential that opens the vendor**, and a vendor is a recurring stream, not one transaction. That reframe changes the metric (vendor conversations opened, not deals funded), changes the ROI math (one vendor relationship, not one ticket), and changes what to demo (surface end users of a *target dealer's* equipment — a vendor-prospecting tool in a lead-list costume).

**So what:** the current script — "instead of ten BDOs, one good sales guy" — is a direct-origination pitch that reads as *irrelevant* to any shop without a direct sales force. Kevin only got there because he did the translation himself. **Armada is majority-vendor too** ([[bd/calls/contacts/vickie-rocco]]). Two of the recent lender calls are vendor-primary books where direct is the growth story. This is a **positioning gap in the pitch, not an ICP problem** — and it's cheap to fix.

**2. Third independent "does your data reach my end of the market?" — and third time we answered with a story instead of a number.**
> "I'm worried that we're a **middle marketplace**, so I'm wondering how much information you're going to get about contracts and things being done for **small-time customers**. Where are you pulling your information from?"
> …then, unresolved: *"I'd like to see, if we got into our industries, **how many companies would actually be in that**."*

Bill/Armada asked it as "a truck driver with a fleet of two." Michael/Providence surfaced it as a size-filter gap. Now Kevin. Alek's second/third-order-effects answer is the right *concept* — and Kevin accepted it conceptually, then immediately asked for the measurement. **We have been asked the same question three times in two weeks and have never once produced a count.** Producing it for Civista is a two-hour job that simultaneously answers Armada. Do it once, reuse it.

**3. Price is not the variable — the frame and the buyer's ticket economics are.**
$1,500/mo drew **zero** objection from a lender with a $250–500K average ticket; Kevin's only framing was "if it was cost effective, to do and try." Armada's CRO said *"I like it, I don't like the price"* at the identical number nine days earlier ([[bd/calls/notes/vickie-rocco-2026-07-22]]), and Stauss volunteered **$7–15K/mo** for the same engine ([[bd/calls/contacts/stauss-paulos]]). Three lenders, one price, three completely different reactions. **The number is not doing the work.** What Civista is actually rationing is not dollars — it's **priority and approval process**.
