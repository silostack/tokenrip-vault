2. **Providence's value sits after a qualified reply, not at the initial discovery layer.** Straight-through automation serves clean credit; Providence's edge is human underwriting for non-standard borrowers and equipment. Vendor inbound proves the scale outcome, while outbound proves the hard part: creating an inbound-quality conversation.
3. **A “find equipment” email should not displace the financing test.** The buyer who already has a machine has a short finance window. A dealer gets value only from an approved, fundable buyer, not an unqualified shopper. The personal-shopper concept is a separate hypothesis for customers without a chosen unit.
4. **The focus-group direction favors email + a short, truthful first touch + human follow-up.** Owners avoid interrupting calls, decide from subject/preview, and need a rapid path during an active purchase. Scheduled callbacks and personal/industry nurturing are a second asset: they retain timing information that Providence's CRM currently does not exploit.
