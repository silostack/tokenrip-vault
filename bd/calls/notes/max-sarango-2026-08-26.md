## Key Intelligence / What Changed

1. **Vendor reciprocity is a distinct sourcing job, not merely another filter.** **Fact:** Robert described using cold callers to find end users in a vendor's territory, then sending purchase opportunities back to the vendor: *“I can flip that over to one of my Hunter reps, and it makes me look good in front of a vendor, and we get the deal.”* The product implication is a campaign object combining vendor, equipment niche, territory, end-user universe, and financing history—not a lender-wide geo filter.
2. **Location matters because the vendor relationship has a territory.** **Fact:** Robert's examples were El Paso automotive repair and territory-specific support for an equipment rep. Geography was largely irrelevant to geo-neutral lenders; it becomes load-bearing when an originator is trying to protect or deepen a local dealer relationship. **Inference, high confidence:** the useful unit is likely vendor territory or rep territory, not state alone. Test by asking Robert for one live vendor/rep territory and building around it.
3. **Incumbent-financier history is a campaign selector and a credit-box proxy.** **Fact:** Robert wants companies in the target niche with current or historical UCCs naming lenders such as Ascentium Capital or All Equipment Finance. Those secured parties indicate both equipment-buying behavior and a borrower profile Providence believes it can finance. This is more useful than a generic “has UCC” flag.
4. **Trade shows provide two different signal classes that should not be conflated.** **Fact:** Max identified public exhibitor directories at PACK EXPO/PMMI and NAB replacement cycles; Robert hypothesized that paid attendance signals buying intent. Public exhibitor/vendor intelligence appears cheap and actionable. Named attendee data/geofencing remains **unconfirmed** and may face access, consent, and provenance constraints. Start with exhibitors, equipment taxonomy, geography, and event timing; treat attendee identification as research, not a promised feature.
5. **Small-ticket means high volume, narrow campaigns, and slower outcome labels.** **Fact:** Providence typically works sub-$1M deals, with $150–300K already moving toward middle ticket, and views UCC outreach as a long-cycle process where calls do not immediately become applications. The feedback model cannot learn only from funded outcomes; it needs intermediate labels such as bad fit, bad data, connected, future need, vendor-introduction opportunity, and application.
6. **The full-team rollout risk is adoption, not account provisioning.** **Fact:** Max explicitly refused to expose the tool to the broader team until he and Robert have a profitable working model because a poor first experience would create permanent rejection. The two-person pod is the correct test, but scaling should require measured campaign proof rather than Max's enthusiasm alone.

### So What

The core product opportunity is not “add location.” It is **campaign orchestration for vendor-led originators**: select a vendor relationship, define the vendor's territory and equipment niche, find end users showing purchase/renewal signals, and record both borrower outcomes and vendor reciprocity. That is a different workflow from ranking a lender-wide pool and should first be tested as a concierge campaign, not generalized into the product from one call.
