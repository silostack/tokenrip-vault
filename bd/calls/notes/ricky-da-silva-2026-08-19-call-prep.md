## The frame: Envision is not Wingspire in miniature

The Wingspire call uses the same discovery skeleton with inverted pricing logic. Porting the Onset fee logic here repeats the anchoring mistake—a price set in one work-content context carried into another.

| | Wingspire (mid-ticket) | Envision (small-ticket) |
|---|---|---|
| Market broker comp | 2–4% referral fee | Points in rate markup, often 4–8+ pts |
| Our fee problem | A discount that must be spent deliberately | Percentages collapse at $30k tickets; flat fee fits |
| Structural position | One broker among many, cheaper | The only broker type viable in the band—a software cost structure is the entry ticket |
| Conversation | Fee level + structural trades | Volume, box-mapping, their paper |

The same math cuts back at us: founder dials cannot feed a $500-per-deal channel. Envision works only when the automated UCC-renewals lane feeds it, which is why geography is the kill question.

## Pricing choreography: they quote first

Envision runs Vendor 360 and belongs to NAELB and NEFA, so a standard broker/referral package with a comp schedule almost certainly exists. Ricky's two LinkedIn questions—funded-only? percent of amount financed?—show he has priced broker comp before.

**The move:** *"What does your standard referral agreement pay today? Send me the package—we'll probably slot into your existing paper."*

Letting them anchor wins three ways:

1. Their number is likely above the reserve tiers. Never hand over a discount nobody asked for.
2. Their paper collapses legal friction—the Onset pattern, where the lender papered Quintel and it worked.
3. Their comp schedule is free market intelligence for the Wingspire call.

## Discovery: walk out with these six

Priority logic: kill questions first, price-setting second, path third, operations last. If only the two P0 rows get answered, pricing and the go/no-go decision can still happen from the desk. If everything except P0 gets answered, comp has been negotiated on flow that may not exist.

- **P0 — the call fails without these:** #1 (box + geography, the only answer that can zero the relationship) and #3 (handoff form + their close rate, which set the channel's economics: funded-only fee × close rate = revenue per referral).
- **P1 — sets price and pace, recoverable by email after:** #2 (their comp package—the ask matters more than the number landing live) and #5 (signer + routing, one casual question).
- **P2 — operational, negotiable at paper time:** #4 and #6.

## What the low fee buys: invoice the structure

The gap between our fee and market comp is a budget, not a leak. Spend it on things a broker never asks for:

- **Outcome feed, per referral: funded / declined / why.** The #1 ask. Frame as their self-interest: "We're cheap because we're software; the trade is you tell us what funded and what declined so our targeting for you sharpens." Trains the ranker; a Type-2-pool brick.
- **Their box as a written spec.** The one-pager—them programming our router for free.
- **Decision SLA.** A 24-hour approval is their public promise; attach it contractually to our referrals. Speed makes multi-lender routing work.
- **Non-exclusivity, explicit.** Quintel routes "when Envision is the best fit"—the same clause as the Wingspire email. Never traded for fee.
- **Skip the mid-ticket asks** (CRM integration, attribution tails). At $500 per deal, keep the machinery thin.

## Gates

- **Geographic overlap** (silent kill): if they don't fund FL, CO, and CT, what is Envision for? The answer must exist before comp is negotiated.
- **CA CFL broker-licensing check:** Envision holds CFL license #603F254; referral fees to CFL lenders can require a license on our side. A 30-minute check must happen before any paper is signed, and it must cover both handoff forms (standing closer commitment).
- **Supply reality:** outside the UCC lane, the corpus's small-ticket band was a data artifact (07-29 defects doc). Do not promise flow the feed cannot produce.

## Call order

1. Open on their world, not ours: how Envision sources today, Vendor 360 vs. direct, where new flow would land (discovery #1–2).
2. Frame the offer as the category they already buy: "We source deals with our own platform and refer them when you're the best fit—funded-only fee, your standard paper if you have one." The platform is the credibility layer for how we source, not the product being sold. Three lenders in a row engaged on per-deal fee after going cold on the platform—don't fight the pattern.
3. Comp: ask for their standard package. Reserve tiers only if pushed.
4. Structure asks: outcome feed + box spec + decline SLA, positioned as "what makes our sourcing get better for you."
5. Path: who signs; intro to the Director or sales ops when back; Ricky as receiving AE if routing allows.

**Cost-bearing next step to ask for on the call:** their box/credit one-pager, their standard broker/referral package, and an intro to the Director when back. Send a same-day recap email with a dated next step (closer commitment, 5 PM).
