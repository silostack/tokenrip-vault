1. **Lead sourcing is not enough to justify a funded-deal fee.** *Fact, from Stauss’s VFI funnel:* $2.2B term sheets sent → $800M executed → just under $300M formally approved. He reinforced it with a live $23M GPU transaction that remains exposed to a late vendor-invoice anomaly despite strong borrower credit. **Implication:** Treat sourced lead, packaged deal, lender acceptance, executed term sheet, approval, and funding as distinct events. A success fee without control over the intervening work is misaligned.

2. **The next economic test is a tight concierge workflow, not an agent platform.** *Inferred, medium confidence:* Stauss’s post-approval examples support a deal-coordination service, but only when Quintel has a complete file and a legitimate role with both parties. The cheapest disconfirming test is a single Bevel package with explicit borrower permission, maximum three lender matches, and an event ledger. If it cannot reach a booked lender/borrower introduction, do not expand into automated deal execution.

3. **Ctrl Flow is a competitor signal, not a pricing benchmark yet.** *Fact:* Stauss reports $15K per seat/month and that Ctrl Flow provides rich deal sheets while in contact with the borrower to set a call. *Unknown:* its conversion, file quality, degree of exclusivity, and whether it closes the borrower handoff. **Implication:** Quintel should compare cost per qualified, consented introduction or per accepted package—not monthly price alone.

4. **Bevel is the cleanest immediate design-partner node.** *Fact, via Stauss:* Bevel’s packages are thorough and it sent roughly $15M of deal volume across three emails in one week; NED and Bevel are cited as high-quality sources. *Inferred, medium confidence:* Bevel can produce enough clean, permissioned volume for a pilot. 5. **Scope creep has crossed the business boundary.** Stauss described CFO-resource directories, fractional staffing, repair services, vendor financing, equipment brokering, insurance, warranties, tax/accounting, ABL, working capital, geography expansion, and tokenized funding. His “crawl, walk, run” principle still applies. These routes require different trust, compliance, operations, and sales motions; none should be treated as a Quintel roadmap commitment.
