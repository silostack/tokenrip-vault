## Key Intelligence / What Changed

**1. The project is the deal unit, not the company — and it multiplies 5–10×.** *"There's probably like three or four deals here in this one project… someone's gonna be supplying the steel… who's doing the construction work, the digging… what kind of facilities are they building… who's supplying the vehicles?"* Grounded in a real closed deal: *"We did a deal with a copper mining company. There was only 15 million and there was like six or seven vendors and thousands of invoices."* At $500M–$10B project scale that becomes *"hundreds of thousands of vendors."* This reframes the excluded-oversized-company problem — Energy Transfer is not noise to filter, it is a **container of qualified targets** we currently discard. It also supplies the missing timing dimension: projects run in phases over 12–24 months, so the correct outreach is *"this is probably not slated for another six, nine, twelve months, but thought I'd get ahead of it."*

**2. The soft-collateral wedge is the deal-angle moat, stated in full for the first time.** For a well-capitalized target: *"where I'm a strategic play is for any of the really soft, less desirable assets… does it make sense for us to finance the racking that you're going to hold all your servers in, or the build-out costs, or a cooling system… if it's a really good credit profile and really terrible collateral, that's my deal."* With the economics attached: borrow at 7%, price at 7.75%, 75bps–1pt spread on a $30M deal, accepted *because* VFI securitizes (*"my securitization market's gonna love that"* — explicitly not how he'd have priced it 10 years ago). And the underground-pipe note: *"Most lenders don't like underground piping… We're one of the few in the middle market space that do it."* **This is directly generatable** — for any oversized or bond-financed target, output what the senior capital won't encumber. It converts commitment #76/#107 from a nice-to-have into the reason an out-of-box company is workable at all.
