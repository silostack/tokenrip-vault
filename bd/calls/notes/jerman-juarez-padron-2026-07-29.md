## Key Intelligence / What Changed

1. **Onset's best deals live where Quintel's data probably doesn't.** *"Where we shine for some of those bigger deals is if they're going international… Portugal, Dubai, Colombia, Dominican Republic… Mexico is a good one. Canada is a good one."* And the reason is structural, not incidental: US banks won't finance offshore collateral (repossession risk), so a USD-entity lender is the only option — and foreign borrowers want US-dollar backing to attract investors. Meanwhile *"bigger deals in the United States are a little bit harder because usually they have really good banking relationships."* Onset's edge is precisely the segment least visible in US market data. Those deals arrive via *"brokers that I've had over the years… relationships from us going to events overseas… flyers… referrals."* **So what:** the feed may be structurally mismatched to the box. If Quintel can only surface US-domiciled sub-$1M-ish signal, it lands below the $750k floor Jerman just set and outside where he wins. This is the #1 thing to resolve before Tuesday — and it is a *sourcing-capability* question, not a sales question.

2. **The committee approved the model but shrank the deployment to one seat.** *"My team recommended that I personally am the only one on it for now… more so just kind of like a tester, test trial."* Jerman pre-empted the obvious read: *"it's not like a trial, like, oh, let's see if we close one deal or see how it goes in three months"* — any funded deal pays. Two consequences: (a) integration lift goes to zero (no Edge CRM work — Alek correctly banked this), and (b) throughput collapses to one part-time closer (*"half of my day… morning… afternoon do what I'm doing now"*). Expected near-term revenue is a function of one person's spare mornings.

3. **Pricing moved in our favor without us asking, and there's a stated escalator.** Jerman floated ~0.5pt on call 1; the committee came back **0.5–1%**, size-indexed, with smaller deals skewing toward 1% *"especially if it's a quick one."* Escalation to 1–2% if volume proves out, benchmarked against top brokers at 2.5–3%. Payment terms are unusually clean for a contingency model: **funded → 5 days → wired**, and commission is knowable at formal approval.

4. **Onset owns the paper, and Quintel is being papered as a broker.** *"I do have a master lease broker agreement. I'd have to tweak it because obviously it's made for brokers. Broker classification is not cosmetic: it may carry non-circumvention, exclusivity, duty, and term language written for a different counterparty. **Attribution — the model's single point of failure — will be defined in their document unless we supply language.** The "siloed pod" mechanism from call 1 evaporated with the one-seat scope-down; nothing replaced it.

5. **Their close mechanics are strong once a deal reaches approval.** *"Once the approval is set, it's a really good 90, 95% of closing."* Post-approval deaths are client-side covenant refusals (won't maintain updated financial statements), not credit or project failures. Decision speed is genuinely fast — *"it's just myself and the credit committee"*, no board pitch, and Britain is one office over. **Buy box now explicit:** floor $250k technically, practical floor **$750k**, sweet spot **$1M–$100M**, will look at $200M–$1B on strong credit, **industry-agnostic**, credit-profile-first.
