## Key Intelligence / What Changed
1. **EF lenders anchor on the broker comp model, not SaaS.** Onset pays for essentially nothing on subscription (just Sales Navigator). Their instinct for paying an outside sourcing party is the broker deal: **pay only on funded, % of cost of property.** *"Usually it is per deal… when the deal gets funded, then you guys would get paid."* This is a recurring buyer-psychology datapoint, not an Onset quirk — worth testing against every EF lender prospect. It reframes the whole Quintel pricing question: subscription may be pushing against the grain of how this buyer *thinks about paying.*
2. **The economics of rev-share here are enormous — and that's the trap.** Broker comp is 1–2% of cost of property; on a $60M deal that's ~$600k, on a $5M deal ~$60k. Jerman floated Quintel at ~0.5pt. Even at half a point, a single funded large-ticket deal dwarfs $10k/mo × 12. The upside is real — but it's **contingent, back-loaded, and attribution-dependent**, and it converts Quintel from predictable ARR into a lead-gen contingency fee. Whether that's a business Simon wants is a founder decision, not a sales concession.
3. **Attribution is the single point of failure — and Onset can't self-attribute cleanly.** Alek correctly flagged it (company already in their CRM vs. surfaced-by-us). Jerman's own fix — **a small pod working ONLY the Quintel feed, siloed from their custom "Edge" CRM** — is the workable path, but it's unproven and must be written into any agreement. If sourcing is disputed after a deal funds, the whole model collapses to unpaid work.
4. **Custom in-house CRM ("Edge")** — not Salesforce/HubSpot. Standard connectors won't apply; integration is bespoke and likely Onset-IT-built. Don't promise a clean CRM push.
5. **Not the buyer.** Decision is Britain Johnson (VP, warm inbound) + Jeff Miller. Jerman is champion/coach only — but a well-placed one (sits beside Britain, on the exec process team, weekly Tue 1:30 forum).
