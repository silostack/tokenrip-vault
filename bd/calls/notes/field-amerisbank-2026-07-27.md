## Key Intelligence / What Changed

### 1. The first person our champion pitches is the owner of the thing we're replacing — and nobody noticed

> **Field:** *"We do have an internal marketing team that basically does a lot of emails, mailers, things like that, or web traffic — whatever comes through, and **they create lists based off of those items**."*
> …
> **Field:** *"I've got our own individual marketing team, and then a director — that's basically who I'd go to next… Basically I've got a **marketing director** and then a **sales director**."*

Alek's demo opened by describing exactly what Ameris's marketing team produces — *"instead of calling these lists based off of 'these guys clicked on our website'"* — as the inferior alternative. Fifteen minutes later Field named the marketing director as stop #1.

**So what:** the pitch as delivered reads, to that director, as *your lists are the problem*. A marketing leader hearing that in a secondhand retelling from a sales manager kills the deal in ten minutes and never tells us why. The fix is cheap and must be in writing before the meeting: **marketing keeps producing the list; Quintel ranks it and adds the event-driven names a campaign database structurally cannot generate** (a company that just won a federal contract has never clicked our email). Framed that way, the marketing director gets a better-converting list attributed to their function — they become the co-buyer instead of the blocker.

This is a **new, reusable ICP pattern**: at bank-owned shops with an internal marketing function, marketing is not a rubber stamp on the sales stack — it is the incumbent vendor. First time this configuration has appeared in the log.

### 2. Ameris's own back book is the pitch for this buyer, and it was delivered as a throwaway aside

> **Alek:** *"…if you guys are like, hey, we want to go look at our book, look at our UCC filings that were financed by Ameris, and say, okay, these guys are up for renewal, we'll reach back out to them. That sort of thing. **Or anyone else, just as an example.**"*

Two sentences, then immediately generalized away. Consider who Field is: Balboa was one of the largest independent small-ticket equipment finance companies in the US before Ameris bought it. Their back book is **tens of thousands of UCC-1 filings naming them as secured party**, on assets with known useful lives. And Field independently described their existing reactivation motion as *"maybe someone's applied here in the past — it could have been like five years ago, but no one's ever had contact with them again. That might still be in a campaign."*

They are already running reactivation — blind, off application age. We can rank it off filing date, asset type and replacement cycle.

**So what — three things at once:**
- It is **Capability 1** in the tear sheet's own dial-vs-switch split (§1a): rank and resurface *their own* flow. Positive floor, buildable, provable. Cold-surfacing net-new is the switch we're supposed to sell as upside, not stake the pilot on. This buyer is a Capability 1 buyer par excellence and got a Capability 2 demo.
- It is **the structural answer to the exclusivity question Field asked** — no other lender can be sold Ameris's own funded book. It converts the objection from a promise we have to keep into a fact about the product.
- It **does not compete with the marketing team.** It arms them.

Do this as a real cut before the directors call if there is any path to it: *"Here are N companies Ameris/Balboa financed whose equipment is at replacement age, ranked."* That is a leave-behind no competitor can produce and it is built from public filings we already ingest.

### 3. The exclusivity answer we gave has an expiry date, and two deals in the pipeline are what expires it

> **Alek:** *"It gets tailored to your buy box… As of now, **the lenders we have using this are on the larger ticket side — two to 100 million dollars in financing**."*

If either signs, the reassurance Field carries into his directors meeting becomes false, and it becomes false in a room we won't be in. Tear sheet §3 already carries a ⚠️ liability note for the identical pattern — "one company per buy box" verbally promised to both Katharine and Mike with overlapping boxes. **This is the third time we've answered exclusivity with a promise we cannot keep.**

The house answer exists and is better: two lenders looking at the same public signal don't get the same list, because the ranking runs on *your* box, *your* history, *your* funded book — and if true category exclusivity matters, it's a paid tier. It has no expiry date because it's true.
