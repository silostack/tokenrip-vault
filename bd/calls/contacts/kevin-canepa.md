# Kevin Canepa — Civista Bank / Civista Leasing & Finance

## Who / What

**Civista** — bank-owned equipment finance operation (Civista Bank, civista.bank). Kevin runs out of **New Hampshire**. *Bank ownership is inferred from the domain, not confirmed on the call — see Assumptions.* If confirmed, it changes the sales motion materially: banks bring procurement, vendor risk review, InfoSec questionnaires and budget cycles that an independent lessor does not.

**Origination model — vendor-first, and stated flatly:**
> "Our motto has always been to work with vendors, get the vendor, and then keep that customer, retain that customer and do more business with them."
> "We don't have a direct sales force." / "We don't need to. We build from within."

Their "direct" book is not outbound at all — it is repeat business from customers originally introduced by vendors, and **the vendor initiates**: *"The vendors come to us and say, can you help this customer?"* Top-of-funnel today is **trade shows and contacts**.

**Buy box (on the record):**
- **Average ticket $250K–$500K** ("somewhere around there")
- **All 50 states** — "we basically go into industries"
- **Sectors:** waste & recycling (scrap metal yards, garbage haulers, roll-off companies); propane; non-destructive test equipment; construction equipment; **specialty trucks** (service-body vans, vacuum trucks, sewer/septic trucks, camera trucks); cranes.
- Self-described as a **"middle marketplace"** lender.
- **CRM: Salesforce.**

**Kevin Canepa** — **stepped down as head of sales roughly two weeks before the call (~2026-07-10) because he "wanted to sell."** No replacement has been named. He explicitly disclaimed authority: *"I'm not the decision maker now, so I'd have to get someone to approve it."* He offered to route Quintel to "one of my other former employees" — i.e. people who reported to him and still sit inside the org.

**Why they matter:** not for the deal size — for the **pattern**. Civista is the first vendor-only shop in the thread to articulate a coherent reason to buy a direct-origination tool, and it is a reason we did not have: use direct end-user origination as a *wedge into the vendor*, not as a revenue line. That reframe potentially unlocks the entire vendor-driven segment, which Quintel's current pitch disqualifies in the first two minutes.

## Running Intelligence

**The vendor-wedge thesis (new, and the most valuable thing on the call).**
> **Kevin:** "If I were to eventually go to an inside sales force that were calling on direct, it would be to learn to leverage those vendors… my theory is that we've concentrated a lot on going to trade shows and making contacts. But something like this, if it's tailored to the companies that we're dealing with in our industries, then we could go after end users and cold call and see if that works. And then get those first couple deals, take those vendors and try and leverage those vendors — *we did the deal for your customer, why don't we work with you*. That type of stuff. **Going backward.**"

The buyer's job-to-be-done here is **not lead volume — it is vendor acquisition**. The end-user deal is the proof-of-work that earns the vendor conversation. That inverts Quintel's usual pitch: the metric isn't funded deals off the list, it's **new vendor relationships opened per surfaced end-user deal**, and each of those is worth a recurring stream, not one transaction. It also means **the equipment inference is the product** for this segment — surface end users of a *target dealer's* equipment, and you've built a vendor-prospecting tool wearing a lead-list costume.

Alek's reaction was correct and should be noted: *"I haven't heard that one yet, that's pretty smart."* It is the first time a prospect has handed us a use case for the majority of the market that we currently screen out.

**Vendor-only shops are not automatically disqualified — the pitch just doesn't reach them.**
The current script (~"replace ten BDOs with one good rep") is a *direct-origination* pitch and lands as irrelevant to a firm with no direct sales force. Kevin got past it only because he did the translation work himself. Most won't. Segment count check: Armada is majority-vendor too ([[bd/calls/contacts/vickie-rocco]]) — that is at least two of the recent lender calls where vendor financing is the primary book and direct is the growth story. **This is a positioning gap, not an ICP problem.**

**"Middle marketplace" coverage doubt — third independent surfacing.**
> "I'm worried that we're a middle marketplace, so I'm wondering how much information you're going to get about contracts and things being done for small-time customers. Where are you pulling your information from?"

Same structural doubt as Bill/Armada ("a truck driver with a fleet of two") and Michael/Providence's low-end gap. Alek answered with the second/third-order-effects story — which is the right *conceptual* answer — but Kevin immediately converted it into a **measurement request**: *"I'd like to see, if we got into our industries, how many companies would actually be in that."* Three lenders in ~two weeks have now asked, in three different phrasings, **"how much of my buy box do you actually cover?"** and we have not once answered with a number. That is now the single most repeated unmet ask in the pipeline.

**Pricing behaved differently here.** $1,500/mo drew no objection at all — Kevin's cost framing was "if it was cost effective, to do and try." A bank-owned shop with a $250–500K average ticket does not blink at $18K/yr; **the friction is approval process and priority, not price.** Compare Armada, where the same number drew "I don't like the price." The number isn't the variable — the frame and the buyer's ticket economics are.

**Salesforce integration was quoted at "probably free."** Alek: *"assuming it's not a two-week sprint on integration, no."* At a bank, a Salesforce org is rarely simple and rarely open to an outside vendor without review. This is an unpriced, unscoped liability that is now heading into a written quote.

**Org instability is the real risk.** The head of sales left the seat two weeks ago, there is no replacement, and our only contact is the person who vacated it and now sells his own deals. Anything requiring an internal champion has no seat to sit in.
