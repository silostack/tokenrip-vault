## Running Intelligence
- **Diamond trade structure**: JTD sits at the *origin* of the supply chain (rough → India cut/polish → Antwerp inventory → wholesale). Most Antwerp traders are pure affiliates ("I know you, I know the client, I have the supplier — I connect and live off commissions"). Firsthand sourcing via African siteholder agreements is uncommon and is JTD's moat.
- **Pricing**: A published benchmark ("Rap" = Rapaport) sets diamond price by clarity/color/size/cut. On sourced stones JTD is a price-taker to Rap; on their own inventory they set price freely — the source of their margin advantage.
- **The business is deliberately invisible**: no website, no online store — only an Antwerp address + a filed company profile with tax number. All sales by phone and word of mouth, 30 years running. Zak frames the upside as an *awareness* problem: "why buy here and not outside [where you pay triple]?"
- **Operations are archaic**: inventory is *transcribed manually / written down by hand*. Zak himself flagged this as a possible (low-priority) automation target — "nice-to-have."
- **Regulatory thread (VERIFIED 2026-07-17)**: The mandate is the EU/G7 Russian-diamond traceability regime — and it is **already in force**, not "later this year": since 2026-01-01 importers of in-scope polished diamonds into the EU must sign a Due Diligence Statement on origin, and mixed-origin rough imports need KP certificates naming exact countries of origin (after two postponements; some sub-deadlines have continued to slip). Antwerp is the designated import node → JTD squarely in scope. Zak lives in this trade and knows the regime — do NOT explain it to him; use it as the commercial angle (siteholder provenance = marketing asset under the regime). Sources: Rapaport, AWDC G7/EU sanctions FAQ, National Jeweler, JCK (searched 2026-07-17).
- **The real strategic knot**: JTD is weighing a B2C/brand move. Two forks they named — (1) a *corporate/informational* site (wins more B2B clients) vs. (2) a *storefront/web shop with staged jewelry* (opens B2C). The blocker is channel conflict: going B2C makes them a competitor to the jewelry-shop buyers who are their B2B customers. This — not AI — is their actual live internal debate.
