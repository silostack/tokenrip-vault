## Who / What

**Armada Financial** — Toronto-HQ independent equipment-finance lender, expanding into the US. Buffalo NY address is a legal shell only; all US staff are remote. Began doing US business ~2018–2019, but **Vickie and one other person were the first actual US hires and started January 2026** — the US motion is roughly six months old and being built from scratch.

**Business mix:** majority **vendor/dealer financing** today. The direct-to-borrower motion is the growth area and the reason they took this call.

**Buy box (from Bill, on the record):** SME. **Average ticket ~$200K**, range **$20K–$500K**, "not precluding any other size of transaction, but from a sales perspective that's where we would be looking, that's where we're investing." Sectors funded most: **transportation/trucking, healthcare, construction** — "but a lot of the industries and sectors they've got listed there we are in, or getting into."

**Vickie Rocco** — remote from Kansas City, MO. Long-tenured equipment-finance operator ("I have a lot of experience in this industry with other companies"). Technical/CRM-literate — asked about DOT records, Salesforce API, field mapping, incumbent-lender search, and exclusion filters unprompted.

**Bill Tsiobanos** — **Chief Revenue Officer**, Toronto (Mississauga). Owns the sales team, controls the budget, and speaks in credit-committee terms (linkage, associated entities, red flags, "we check against the negatives as well as the positives"). Skeptical opener, enthusiastic closer. He and Vickie played the classic authority two-step ("it's usually Vickie's decision" / "he's lying, it's his decision") and then Bill resolved it himself: *"I'd like to get this, but I want to talk to Vickie about how we would implement it."*

**Why they matter:** a real direct lender, in the exact ticket band Quintel's signal set serves, whose **entire US strategy is greenfield origination with no existing relationship base**. That is the cleanest structural fit for a sourcing engine in the pipeline — they cannot rely on a US rolodex because they don't have one yet. This is also the **first prospect where the economic buyer said "I'd like to get this" on call one.**

## Running Intelligence

**Company mechanics:**
- Toronto HQ; Buffalo address is a mail drop. All US staff remote. US expansion is ~6 months old at the staffing level.
- Majority-vendor book today; direct origination is the growth motion — hence the interest.
- Uses **Salesforce**. Vickie immediately jumped to field-mapping ("the key would be making sure that we had fields in Salesforce for everything we wanted to export out of here") — she is thinking about deployment, not evaluation.
- Sales team includes named vertical heads: **Bob, Harold, Tony, Nick**. Bill is over all of them.
- Existing prospecting method: reps build their own lists off marketplace sites (**Truck Trader, Tree Trader**) to find dealers. "There's a lot of competition for that."
- **Paid diligence is per-lead and expensive** — "each of them costs money, so we wouldn't want to run it on a group of cold list leads." They only pay for verification once a lead looks real. This is a cost structure Quintel can attach to.
- Canada is not the target. Bill volunteered that Canadian data is bad. US-only is a non-issue for them — arguably an asset.

**The value frame they gave us (verbatim, and load-bearing):**
> **Vickie:** "I would look at this as giving the sales people **time back from creating their own lists**, and finding a more targeted list for them to call to… If you think about it in the funnel, **this is going to be the least qualified lead**… but there's some rigor behind it, in that **there's a reason for them to call out, which I like**. But it's not going to be doing all of that due dilly… At the point that it's in here, **it's just a lead**."

This is simultaneously the clearest articulation of the product's job we have received from a lender and a **hard price-ceiling anchor**. She has categorized Quintel as a *better list*, not as intelligence. A better list is worth rep-hours; intelligence is worth a headcount. Compare Stauss's unprompted **$7–15K/mo** ([[bd/calls/contacts/stauss-paulos]], 2026-07-22) — same product, ~5–10× the number, because he framed it as an intelligence platform. **Framing, not the product, is setting the price.**

**Their real diligence need is credit/fraud, not sourcing:**
Bill's entity-linkage ask is a **workflow adjacency**, not a sourcing feature: new entity filed at SOS → who owns it → what else did that owner run → did it dissolve/go bankrupt → red flag. He wants the negative signal surfaced next to the positive one. Vickie framed the same thing as pre-diligence layered onto the list before their paid sources get spent. **This is the same "surface the negatives" theme that keeps recurring across lender calls and is currently unbuilt.**

**Sub-sector granularity ask (Bill):** cardiologists vs. ophthalmologists inside healthcare; wheel loaders / chippers / tippers inside construction. Vickie corrected him toward the right unit — the *company type* that owns the equipment (landscaping company ⇒ chippers), which is exactly Quintel's inference direction. **Handle as scoring/labels, not as filters that remove leads** — Vickie was explicit: *"if we're paying for a vertical, we would want all of the leads in that vertical."*

**Second/third-order sourcing landed clean.** Alek's data-center→local-landscaper example was completed by Vickie herself on a $183M contract: *"it's going to be the suppliers and the vendors that they're working with that are going to be more likely to be our customers."* Independent convergence with the Stauss "waterfall" thesis. This is the differentiator that consistently lands with lenders — lead with it.

**Sector pricing strained again.** The $1,500 / 3-sector tier immediately created a scarcity debate ("if we're only picking three, we've got to pick the three that are going to have the highest reading"), forced a live volume test that **exposed thin healthcare coverage**, and led Alek to concede a free fourth sector unprompted. This is the **second independent lender in nine days** to push back on sector-based tiering — Stauss rejected it outright on 2026-07-14 ("sector- and asset-class agnostic… puts too many limitations on wanting to buy"). Two for two. See Open Commitments #7.
