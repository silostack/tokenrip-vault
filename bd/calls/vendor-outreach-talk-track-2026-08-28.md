# Quintel Vendor Outreach — Merged Talk Track (2026-08-28)

One page both founders call from. Merges Alek's "managed finance desk" track (guardrails, mechanism, revenue share) with the "deals that didn't close" hook. **Dealers are the primary track.** The mom-and-pop track is included to show how the pitch changes when the vendor has no finance desk to relieve.

## The one line (both tracks)

> We fund the equipment deals that don't close today — the declines, the overflow, the quotes that went quiet. Your team sends the quote; we run the desk behind it, shop it to multiple funding sources, and you get paid on funded deals.

## Guardrails (both tracks, non-negotiable)

- Never say Quintel is a lender. "We run the finance desk and coordinate multiple funding sources."
- No numbers on approval rate, funding speed, rates, or revenue-share % until program terms are papered. Say "typically same day" at most; never "an hour."
- Never send internal pages to a vendor.
- Revenue share is "on eligible funded transactions, per program terms." Nothing more specific.

## Objection responses

**"We already offer financing / our captive handles it."** *(both)*
"Keep it. We're not asking you to replace anything that works. The question is what happens to the ones they decline, the ones under their minimum, and the used or off-brand units they won't touch. That's all we want."

**"Are you a lender?"** *(both)*
"No. We run the finance desk behind the request and coordinate multiple funding sources so the buyer gets evaluated for something that fits."

**"We don't get many financing requests."** *(mom-and-pop especially)*
"The requests you don't get are the customers who never asked and walked. Send me ten quotes that didn't close and we'll find out how many were a payment problem."

**"We use Clicklease / a point-of-sale lender."** *(mom-and-pop)*
"Good, keep them for the small stuff. What do you do when they decline someone, or on the units above their cap? That's our lane."

**"Our customers pay cash."** *(mom-and-pop)*
"How many pay cash because nobody offered payments? Ten dead quotes will tell us."

**"What's the catch / how do you make money?"** *(both)*
"The funding source pays us on funded deals, and we share that with you. You never see a fee."

**"Not interested."** *(both)*
"Fair. Is that because financing is fully covered, or because it rarely comes up?" (Diagnostic. Log the answer either way.)

**"Send me something."** *(both)*
"I'll send a five-line email. Then I'm calling you Thursday. Fair?"

## Cold call script (dealer, primary)

> "Hi [name], this is [Simon/Alek] with Quintel. I know I'm interrupting. Do you have 30 seconds to see if this is worth your time?"
>
> *(stop; wait for "go ahead")*
>
> "We get the equipment deals funded that don't close today: the declines, the quotes that went quiet, the buyer who never came back. Your guys send us the quote, we run the followup and shop it to multiple funding sources, and you get paid on anything that funds."
>
> "Right now, when your finance program turns somebody down, what happens to that deal?"

Then: "And who owns that followup, sales or somebody else?" → Q3 → CTA.

**Never ask "do you have a financing program?"** It's a yes/no and both answers close a door ("yes, we're covered" / "no" + feels like a pitch to fix him). The presumptive question qualifies without asking. If the answer is **"we don't really have one":** "Then that's the whole thing. Your guys send us the quote and we're the desk. What happens today when a buyer asks about payments?" No incumbent is the best answer on the list.

**Mom-and-pop:** same opener and hook, but do *not* presume a program. Q1 is "when a customer asks 'can I do payments,' what do you do?" It works whether the answer is Clicklease, "my bank guy," or nothing.

**Rule for every discovery question:** ask the one whose every answer moves you forward.

## Skeleton (both tracks)

1. **Opener, then silence.** The pause is the ask.
2. **Hook:** the track's line above.
3. **Discovery:** the three questions. Don't pitch until Q1 and Q2 are answered; Q2 tells you the incumbent.
4. **CTA:** the track's ask. One ask. Get a yes/no.
5. **Log** before the next dial (see labels below).

## Voicemails

Under 25 seconds, one reason to care, no pitch, number twice, and name the email so the two touches reinforce each other. Send the email before hanging up so "emailing you now" is true when they check.

**Dealer, first attempt (~20 sec):**
"Hi [name], it's [Simon/Alek] with Quintel. Quick reason for the call: we work with equipment dealers on the deals their finance program turns down or won't touch, the used units, the sub-fifty-K stuff, the thin-credit buyers. We fund those, and the dealer gets paid on the ones that close. I'm sending you a short email right now so you can see it in writing. [Simon/Alek], Quintel, [number], again that's [number]. Thanks."

**Mom-and-pop, first attempt (~18 sec):**
"Hi [name], [Simon/Alek] with Quintel. Quick one: when a customer gets a quote from you and says 'let me think about it,' a lot of those are a payment problem, not a price problem. We fund those deals, at no cost to you. I'm emailing you now so it's easy to look at. [Simon/Alek], [number], that's [number]. Thanks."

**Second attempt, day 4–5, either track (~15 sec):**
"[Name], [Simon/Alek] with Quintel again. Not going to keep leaving these. The email I sent has one ask in it: send me your last ten quotes that didn't close and I'll tell you which ones we'd have funded. If it's zero, no harm done. [Number]. Thanks."

Delivery: slow down on the number, say it like you're writing it, stop talking the second it's done. Never "no obligation," "just following up," or "I'd love to."

## After a good call: the ladder

The phone call only closes the sample. The readout closes the program.

1. **Sample** (on the call): last month's declines / last ten dead quotes. Nothing to sign.
2. **Readout** (within 2 days): "Of the 10, we'd have funded 4. Here's the monthly on each. Two are still live, want us to reach out?" Unfakeable, because it ran on his paper.
3. **First live deal**: one real stuck quote, forwarded today. One deal, one outcome, one rev-share check.
4. **Program agreement**: one page, signed. Eligible transactions, rev-share math, payment trigger, attribution window, who talks to the buyer, states covered. This is the "thing."
5. **Steady state**: every stuck quote (ideally every quote), payment line on the quote, Quintel chases the stale ones. Quarterly "here's what we funded for you."

What the vendor sees at the end: an address he forwards quotes to, and money showing up. No portal, no login, no training.

**Needed for the ladder, not yet built:** a pre-screen for the readout (Providence soft-screens, or a box-fit check off their stated criteria); the one-page program agreement (same missing paper as Providence #13, vendor side); a decision on who contacts the buyer (default: the salesperson sends, Quintel handles the application, never cold-contact a dealer's customer uninvited).

## Interested but won't send quotes: downshift, don't upshift

Book nothing that has no event attached. Take the smallest thing he'll commit to that produces a real event on a date, in this order:

1. **Anonymized sample.** "Don't need names. Equipment, price, why it died. Ten lines in an email." Usually the objection is customer data, not effort.
2. **The next one.** "Next time a buyer stalls or your program says no, forward me the quote right then. One deal." Then pin it: "How often does that happen, weekly? I'll check in Friday."
3. **The finance owner.** "Who sees those when they happen? Can you put the three of us on 15 minutes?" Right rung when the person on the phone doesn't hold the paper.
4. **Paper first.** "I'll send the one-page program terms today so you can see how you get paid. Thursday I'll call to walk it."

Every call ends with **a trigger, a date, and a name.** "Sounds good, send me something" with none of those is logged as a no.

Recap email inside the hour: what you heard about how they handle declines, what he agreed to (the trigger), when you're calling.

Needed now: a standing intake address (a quintel.ai inbox both founders watch, never a personal inbox). Log "sample" and "live forward" separately; a live forward is slower but proves behavior change, a sample only proves box fit.

## Log on every call (six fields)

1. Track (dealer / mom-and-pop) and equipment category
2. Incumbent financing (captive / independent / POS lender / none)
3. Who owns applications today
4. Volume: quotes or financing requests per month (their number, even rough)
5. CTA outcome: sent declines or quotes / agreed to send / meeting / no
6. If no: "covered" vs "rarely comes up" vs other (their words)

Twenty calls with these six fields beat two hundred without.
