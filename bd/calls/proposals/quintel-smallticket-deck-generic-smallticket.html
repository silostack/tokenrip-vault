Timing
The replacement cycle

The equipment you didn't finance is on a clock.

The clock is public

When a business finances equipment, a UCC-1 goes on the state record: who lent, who borrowed, what date. Terms run two to five years.

That filing dates the moment the conversation reopens, and it has been sitting in the public record the whole time.

Today only the incumbent knows

Whoever wrote the paper knows when it matures and calls ahead of it. Everyone else finds out after the deal is re-papered, which is why renewals feel locked up.

They are not locked. Nobody is assembling the filings.

And you are calling a proven borrower

They needed equipment before. They were underwritten and approved before. They were financed by lenders writing the kind of paper your firm writes.

Two guesses come off every dial at once: whether they need it, and whether it can get approved.

Same engine, one more public record. Filings get read and ranked against your box alongside the permits, awards and expansions, so renewal timing and new demand arrive on one list rather than in two places.
