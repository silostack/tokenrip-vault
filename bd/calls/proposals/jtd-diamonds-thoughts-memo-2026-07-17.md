# JTD Diamonds: Building Visibility Without Breaking the B2B Channel

**Prepared for:** Zak Shenal, JTD Diamonds
**Date:** July 2026

---

## Scope

JTD is weighing its first online presence after 30 years of operating entirely on relationships and word of mouth. Two directions are on the table: a corporate site that presents the company to the trade, or a storefront that opens a consumer channel. The open question holding both back is channel conflict — whether selling to consumers puts JTD in competition with the jewelry-shop buyers who make up its current customer base.

This memo gives a recommendation on direction and sequence, and closes with where we could help and where we couldn't.

## Bottom line

Build the corporate site now, and build it around provenance rather than general awareness. Formalize the private-client sales that already happen informally, without a public storefront. Treat a true consumer storefront as a separate, later decision that only works under three specific conditions — and note that a first step, digitizing the inventory, is required no matter which path is taken.

## 1. The corporate site should be a provenance story, not a brochure

The strongest commercial asset JTD has for a public presence is the thing that is currently invisible: firsthand siteholder sourcing, own cutting pipeline, and GIA-certified own inventory held in Antwerp. In a market where origin documentation has moved from a back-office concern to a front-of-mind one for every buyer in the chain, "we can show you exactly where this stone came from, because we brought it out of the ground ourselves" is a selling argument most Antwerp traders cannot make.

A corporate site built around that story does three things at once:

- Wins more trade buyers for JTD's own merchandise — the half of the business where JTD sets its own pricing, and where an additional buyer is worth the most.
- Gives existing and prospective counterparties a public reference point ("who is JTD?") that today resolves to nothing but an address and a tax number.
- Lays the brand foundation that any future consumer move would need anyway, without committing to one.

Which origin and sourcing claims can be made publicly, given the confidentiality norms of the trade and the siteholder relationships, is a judgment call that sits entirely on JTD's side. The recommendation here is only about what the site should be *about*: verifiable origin, not generic "quality and service."

## 2. A consumer storefront is a pricing decision, not a website decision

The instinct to hold back on the storefront is correct, but the risk is more specific than "going B2C." The damage scenario is price-led consumer selling: a stone offered publicly near trade prices undercuts every buyer downstream and, once visible, cannot be walked back. That risk follows the pricing, not the corporate structure — a separately branded entity does not neutralize it, because in a trade this small and this documented, ownership does not stay unknown for long. Discovery through the grapevine is strictly worse than disclosure, because it converts a business decision into a trust breach.

A consumer storefront becomes viable only if all three of the following hold:

1. **Full retail pricing.** The consumer brand competes on origin, certification, and story — never on price. The moment it competes on price, it is competing with JTD's own customers.
2. **Proactive disclosure to key accounts.** The most important trade buyers hear about it from JTD first, framed accurately: a retail-priced brand that does not undercut them.
3. **A dedicated operator.** A consumer brand is a second business — content, imagery, acquisition, service on emotionally significant purchases. Run as a side project of a five-person trading team, it will be mediocre at best. The workable structures involve a partner or joint venture where someone else runs the brand and JTD is the supplier behind it.

If any of the three is unacceptable, the storefront should stay off the table — not indefinitely, but until it isn't.

## 3. The private-client channel already exists; it can be formalized without a storefront

Direct sales to individuals already happen at JTD by referral and word of mouth. That channel can be deliberately grown without ever becoming public: referral-only, no published prices, no online basket — an invitation, not a shop. It captures the consumer margin on own-inventory stones with none of the channel exposure, and it is the natural testing ground for whether a consumer brand would ever be worth the trouble. How far to push it, and with whom, is a relationship judgment that belongs to JTD; the point of this memo is that it is a real third option between "stay invisible" and "open a shop."

## 4. First step on every path: the inventory becomes digital

On the call this was mentioned as a nice-to-have. It is more than that — it is the prerequisite for everything above:

- A corporate site that shows the strength of the inventory needs stones as structured records: shape, cut, color, clarity, certificate number, origin, status.
- A private-client channel needs shareable, current stone lists that don't require re-transcribing by hand.
- Any storefront, ever, is impossible without it.
- Origin documentation and due-diligence paperwork get materially easier when provenance lives in a system rather than a notebook.

This is a bounded piece of work — weeks, not months — and it carries no strategic risk: it is valuable even if JTD decides to stay entirely offline. Whatever direction the internal debate lands on, this is the no-regret move to make first.

## Where we could help, and where we couldn't

Two areas of the above are squarely work we do; one is not.

- **Inventory digitization (point 4):** turning the handwritten records into a structured, searchable system, with certificates and origin attached — this is our core competence, and the piece we would propose starting with.
- **Technical lead on the corporate site (point 1):** building the site and connecting it to the inventory system, working alongside whoever handles design and brand.
- **Brand and creative work:** not our lane. If JTD moves on the corporate site or, later, a consumer brand, the visual identity and storytelling should come from a specialist — we would work beside them, not replace them.

The suggested next step is a short call to react to this memo: where it matches JTD's internal read, where it doesn't, and whether the inventory piece is worth scoping properly.
