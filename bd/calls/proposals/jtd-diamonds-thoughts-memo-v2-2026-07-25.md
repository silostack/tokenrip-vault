# PART 2 — Memo

**JTD Diamonds: Sequencing the Growth Decision**
Prepared by Simon Pettibone · 25 July 2026 · Based on discussion of 17 July 2026

*Basis and limits: this memo rests on a single conversation. Statements about JTD's internal operations, and about how the wider Antwerp trade is structured, are inferences drawn from that call and from outside research, not established fact. Section 4 labels each load-bearing claim and how to test it cheaply.*

---

## Bottom line

JTD is weighing a binary choice (corporate website vs. consumer storefront) that cannot be answered well yet, because both options and every alternative on the table depend on an asset the business has not built: inventory and client history in structured form.

Recommended sequence: **build that asset first, then execute two moves that carry no channel risk, and defer the consumer decision until it is cheap and reversible.**

---

## 1. The channel dilemma is real but it's not the first decision

The stated fork is a corporate site that wins more B2B clients, or a storefront that opens the consumer market. The block is real: consumer sales put JTD in competition with the jewelry-shop buyers who are its current customers. That concern is correctly identified and should not be rushed.

It is, however, the third decision in the sequence, not the first. Each option on JTD's list has the same prerequisite:

| Option JTD is considering | What it requires first |
|---|---|
| Corporate site that wins B2B business | Per-stone detail, not company history |
| Consumer storefront | Per-stone detail plus imagery |
| Being found by buyers searching on volume and price | Structured, factual, stone-level information |
| Origin documentation buyers can use | Origin recorded per stone, not per shipment |
| Calling the right six clients when new production lands | Inventory and client preference history in one place |

Today that information appears to exist on paper and in individual memory. It was raised in conversation as "archaic" and a possible nice-to-have. The reverse looks true: it is the gating item, and the cheapest item on the list.

**Second-order effect worth weighing:** building it first converts the consumer decision from a large one-way bet into a presentation choice that can be reversed later. That alone justifies the sequence.

---

## 2. The growth target is measured in accounts, not in traffic

At roughly $50–60M of revenue across five people, a materially better year is a small number of new relationships, not a large volume of web visitors.

This rules out the expensive answer. JTD does not need a marketing engine. It needs a specific list of the right retailers, designers and online sellers, each with a specific reason to engage this quarter, so that outbound calls land at a higher rate. That is targeting work, not brand work, and it is closely adjacent to work already built for a different industry.

---

## 3. Recommended sequence

### Phase 1 — Structure the inventory and the client history

Every stone as a record: certificate detail, originating rough batch, location, cost, who has viewed it, who bought its near-twins. Accounts recorded the same way, so client preference is documented rather than remembered.

- Unglamorous, and where the leverage sits
- Weeks, not quarters
- Directly in scope for the capability described in the covering email: documents and handwritten records into structured, queryable data

### Phase 2 — Two moves with no brand risk and no channel conflict

**2a. Sourced-half execution (likely the largest number in this memo)**

Roughly half of volume is sourced from the network on a client's behalf, where JTD is a price-taker to the benchmark. That sourcing appears to run on phone calls and memory, with no record of who held what, who quoted what, who filled quickly and who did not.

- On this volume, modest improvements in fill speed and fill price are worth a meaningful annual figure
- Zero positioning cost, zero brand exposure
- Recommended as the first place to look, on the basis that it is unmeasured today

**2b. Provenance as a delivered document**

Siteholder agreements allow JTD to answer the origin question per stone, back to the rough. The read from outside is that most of the Antwerp trade cannot, sitting two or three layers removed from origin.

The commercial consequence: the B2B pitch shifts from *"we have better prices"* (claimed by every trader, provable by none) to *"buy from us and your documentation is already done."*

- Deliverable: a per-stone origin dossier, generated automatically from Phase 1 records, that a buyer can hand to their own auditor unedited
- This is the corporate-site play in a form that wins accounts rather than describing the company
- The argument strengthens over time rather than decaying

### Phase 3 — Revisit consumer, with a third option

Deferred deliberately: this is the only decision on the list with real downside.

Neither stated door is the strongest option. A third structure: JTD builds the storefront once on top of Phase 1 records, then gives each jewelry-shop buyer a branded version of it running JTD's live inventory as their own. The consumer buys from the shop. JTD fulfills. JTD never sets a retail price and never appears as a competitor.

If it holds, the channel conflict inverts: rather than taking buyers' customers, JTD becomes the reason those buyers can sell online at all, carrying no inventory to do so. Visibility is achieved without brand risk.

**This is a hypothesis, not a recommendation.** It depends on whether JTD's buyers want it, which cannot be assessed from outside. Capital-constrained shops are the likely yes; others will refuse. Twenty minutes of buyer conversations settles it before anything is built.

*Adjacent note, unverified:* putting inventory in front of buyers has historically required photographing every stone. Certificate measurements may now offer a cheaper route. Flagged as something to check, not as a claim.

## 4. Confidence, and what would change the read

| Claim | Status | Cheapest test |
|---|---|---|
| Structured inventory is the gating item for all options | High confidence | Attempt any one option and observe the blocker |
| Sourced-half execution is inefficient today | **Inferred, medium** — described as phone calls, never described as a problem | How many people get called for an unheld stone, and is their last quote known? |
| Buyers would want a branded storefront on JTD inventory | **Inferred, low** — roughly even odds | Three buyer conversations |
| Buyers feel their own origin-documentation obligation | **Inferred, medium** | Has any buyer requested origin documentation this year? |
| Most Antwerp competitors cannot document origin to the rough | **Inferred, medium** — from outside research, not from the trade | JTD's own view of what its competitors can produce |

The weakest load-bearing claim is 2a. If sourcing is already tight through long-standing relationships, that item collapses and Phase 2 rests on provenance alone. It is worth testing directly before any work is scoped around it.

---

## 5. Where this goes next

**Three questions, cheap to answer, that would materially sharpen the above:**

1. Would three of your buyers want their own online storefront running JTD inventory? Their answer settles Phase 3 faster than any outside analysis.
2. Has any buyer asked for origin documentation this year, or is it still treated as JTD's paperwork rather than theirs?
3. Besides Zak, who weighs in on the website decision internally?

**A conversation, not a proposal.** The useful next step is a second call to work through which parts of this hold up against how the business actually runs, and which do not. Nothing here needs to be committed to in order to have that conversation.

**If Phase 1 turns out to be the right call**, the smallest possible way to test it is one handwritten inventory log and a batch of certificates turned into structured records, plus two sample origin dossiers to put in front of a real buyer. Small, fixed, and disposable if it proves the wrong path.

**The one-line summary:** the expensive decision JTD is stuck on gets easier, cheaper and more reversible after settling the inexpensive one that's being presented. That's the whole argument.
