# Alliance Funding Group — Individual Quintel Evaluation

**Jim:** We’ve got a couple of bigger IT projects. I think we’re doing a new CRM system and a new phone system, so that’s what we’re dealing with as a company. But I’m currently using AI as part of my outreach through LinkedIn. I was curious what your product could do for me from an individual perspective, and if I should switch.

**Alek:** How are you sourcing deals now?

**Jim:** It’s basically a cold-call method. I make a ton of calls in the vendor channel, calling equipment dealers. We provide financing for their customers. I do research online for various equipment types. I’m trying to get more into emerging markets—warehouse management, supply-chain stuff—in addition to traditional equipment vendors like yellow-iron construction equipment and over-the-road stuff.

**Alek:** We use AI to aggregate market signals across news, postings, hiring, UCC filings, permits and awards. We look at the deals you want to fund—ticket size, sector—and match that with what’s going on in the market and who’s likely looking for financing in the near future. Instead of a big list of companies, you can be more intentional about outreach and get to companies before competitors.

**Jim:** We can get that high. The largest one we’ve done up to this point is 75. We do a lot of small ticket. Our application-only program goes up to 300,000, and if we have a decent vendor that’s going to give us decent volume, we can bump that up to about five, five-fifty.

**Alek:** Each deal gets a score based on how strong the financing signal is and how well it fits your funding criteria. You can see the trigger, when it surfaced, ticket size and sector. For example, if there’s an announcement of a new facility, we look at what equipment they’re likely looking for and whether now is a good time to reach out. You can generate a briefing for every company and enrich a contact, so you know why you’re reaching out rather than working a super-cold list.

**Jim:** How does your engine know they’re looking for equipment and potentially financing? What are your sources to get that information, and how accurate is it? I want to be able to find a lot of hotter leads so I can generate some immediate income. I know end-user outreach is a lot more difficult compared to calling on the vendor channel, but this seems like it would make that a lot easier. If you get an end-user deal and haven’t done business with the vendor yet, you want to bring them on board as a vendor.

**Alek:** We look at the types of events and whether there’s typically an equipment-financing event when those things get announced—for example, a new facility or data center. We can corroborate that: does the company have a history of UCC filings that show it finances equipment, and who was its lender? It’s hard to put a percentage on accuracy. The idea isn’t that every one is a guaranteed deal; if you call these companies rather than a cold list, you’ll have a higher rate, and you get the market information in real time.

**Jim:** This is all web-based? There’s not an app I need to put into my work PC that might cause a problem? I’d be able to integrate it with my CRM and manually input it. I don’t have a problem with that. Show me the criteria again to do a search.

**Alek:** It’s just a website dashboard. The buy box has sectors and subsectors, geography by state, trigger types, ticket size and revenue bands. You can use a lightweight CRM in Quintel if you want, but it’s up to you.

**Jim:** It kind of helps with the issue of timing in our business. You can get a vendor signed on or a bunch of end-user business, but they don’t mean a thing if they’re not needing anything. My pitch is pretty uniform—I’ve been using the same one for about 30 years. If you’re able to reach out for a specific reason, it’s kind of perfect.

**Alek:** Is the end-user use case useful enough to consider?

**Jim:** I’m using one right now. It’s a company out of England, and they’re in sync with my LinkedIn profile. Two hundred is something I could probably live with if it’s worth the investment. If I get a few deals a month, not a problem at all. My monthly goal is only four to five hundred thousand a month. I can get to that with a couple of deals a month, but if I can do four to five deals a month, I’m really kicking it. I’m willing to try it for 30, 60, 90 days and just can the other one.

From the first point of contact, when they become a warm lead, it’s generally fairly quick—within the month. From there, funding takes longer. On the lower-ticket side, the feedback loop is quicker, but we do not have as many people there yet. Those deals are mostly yours right now.
