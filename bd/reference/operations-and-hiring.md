## 1. Capital-Assumption Implications

The decision to assume YC/a16z capital lands inverts the BD optimization function.

**Pre-capital optimization function:** survive long enough to validate. Generate revenue ASAP. Keep a single motion focused. Avoid bets that don't pay back within 90 days. Demo-build + sales is the highest-leverage founder activity because nothing else funds the runway.

**Post-capital optimization function:** demonstrate a category. Produce growth curves. Justify the round size. Set up Series A in 12-18 months. Recruit and retain talent that scales the platform. Move from product-market fit discovery to category formation.

These are structurally different gameplans.

### Why Curves > Flat Dots

Five $25K firm pilots is five logos. Twenty-five published mounted-agent imprints with weekly operator-count growth of 15% is a curve. YC and a16z partners model curves into 24-month projections. They model flat dots into revenue snapshots. Curve metrics get bigger round sizes; snapshot metrics get smaller seed rounds.

This is the single biggest reason the firm-direct gameplan got deprecated. It was structurally a flat-dot motion and the assumption shift made that incompatible with the new optimization function.

### Robustness to YC Outcome

The pivot is robust to YC outcome. **Even without capital, audience-led is the right motion** — firm-direct $25K pilots aren't a fundable path to a venture-grade company anyway. The capital assumption makes the motion more aggressive (more parallel hires, more lighthouse spend, faster substrate roadmap), but doesn't change the motion itself.

Translation: if YC doesn't accept and a16z doesn't follow, the strategy is unchanged. The team size, hire pace, and content velocity get smaller, not the wedge or the audience.

## 3. Hiring Plan

Recruiting takes 8-12 weeks for the right people. Sourcing starts NOW even before capital lands. Offers are ready to go the moment capital is confirmed.

Order matters: substrate hires before sales hires.

### Hire 1: Frontend / UX Engineer (Source Week 2-3)

**Why first:** highest-leverage substrate hire. The "build an agent" wizard, creator dashboard, and marketplace UX all gate on this person. Without this hire, Motion E volume caps at Simon's hand-built capacity (~5-10 deploys total).

**Role shape:** senior frontend engineer with strong UX instincts. Comfortable owning the end-to-end creator experience. Equal parts engineer and product designer. Bias toward someone who's shipped creator-facing tools (Substack, Patreon-tier products, dev tools with non-engineer surfaces).

**Source channels:** Hacker News "Who's Hiring," Twitter/X (frontend-engineer accounts), referrals from existing network, EthGlobal-style hackathon alumni (web3-frontend tends to overlap well with agent-tools work).

**Target start:** week 8-10 (assuming sourcing starts week 2-3, interview cycle 4-6 weeks).

**JD shape (rough):** "Build the substrate experience for creators publishing AI agents. Own the creator dashboard, marketplace surfaces, and 'build an agent' wizard. Ship code daily. Ship UX weekly. Comfortable defining your own scope and deciding what doesn't ship."

### Hire 2: Developer Relations / Community Lead (Source Week 4-5)

**Why second:** Motion D (builder-direct) needs an active hand once the blog series is in market. Independent agent builders need community, support, and live engagement that Simon cannot scale into. Also seeds the creator pipeline for Motion E by surfacing builders-with-audiences from the community.

**Role shape:** developer relations professional with strong written + spoken voice. Has built or maintained a developer community before. Comfortable with the agent / AI-tools landscape. Either an existing creator themselves or has worked closely with creators.

**Source channels:** existing devrel community (Logan Kilpatrick / Twitter), conference speaker rosters, dev tool company alumni.

**Target start:** week 12-14.

**JD shape:** "Build the mounted-agents builder community. Run the Discord / Slack. Triage the developer issues. Author the technical content alongside Simon. Identify creator candidates from the community pipeline."

### Hire 3: Creator Partnerships Lead (Source Week 6-8)

**Why third:** Alek scales but hits a ceiling at ~30 creators. Specialized creator-side relationship management is needed once mid-tier deploys pass that threshold. Different skill from outreach (which Alek owns) — this is about retention, expansion, hero-tier negotiation, and creator-side success.

**Role shape:** background in creator partnerships, talent management, or business development for a creator-economy company (Substack, Patreon, Spotify, YouTube partner program). Comfortable with deal negotiation and revenue-share structures.

**Source channels:** creator economy company alumni, music-industry-rights talent agencies, YouTube partner program alumni.

**Target start:** week 16-20.

**JD shape:** "Own the creator partnership lifecycle. Negotiate deals at hero tier. Run the success motion for mid-tier creators (deploy support, performance optimization, expansion). Pipeline-manage the creator funnel post-deploy."

### Hire 4: Enterprise Sales Lead (Deferred — Post-Capital, Activation Trigger)

**Why deferred:** Motion B activates when 5+ Motion E lighthouse imprints are live + multi-tenant capability ships + named CTO buy-intent surfaces. Until then, no enterprise sales activity. Don't burn founder time on Motion B in 2026, and don't hire a salesperson with nothing to sell.

**Activation trigger:** mid-2027, possibly later. Hire JD and source channels to be defined when activation approaches.

### Recruiting Discipline

- **Sourcing happens now even pre-capital.** Build relationships, do informational interviews, get to "we want you the moment we can fund you."
- **Offers ready when capital confirmed.** Don't wait for the check to land before drafting offers — that loses 2-4 weeks per hire.
- **Founder-led for first 4 hires.** Simon owns interviewing for these. Recruiter help comes later.
- **Bias toward people who've shipped to non-technical users.** Mounted agents need to feel approachable, not engineer-only.

## 4. The Downside Scenario

If YC and a16z don't land, what changes?

### The Baseline Bad Outcome

No $25K firm-pilot revenue exists because Motion A was cancelled. Hiring plan can't execute on schedule. Substrate roadmap slows.

### Mitigation 1: Motion E + D Produce Compounding ARR

Tooling-tier upgrades from creator audiences and builder-direct subscriptions produce ARR. Slower than $25K firm pilots in absolute first-month revenue but compounds month-over-month as deploys accumulate. By month 6-9 of pure Motion E + D execution, ARR can hit $50-200K depending on conversion rates.

### Mitigation 2: Motion E Lighthouse Cases Make Motion B Viable Earlier

Even without capital, ten or more public mounted-agent deploys give the substrate-license pitch credibility. A vendor B deal at $250K-$1M covers a meaningful runway extension and unblocks the next round of fundraising on real revenue rather than promised growth.

### Mitigation 3: Capital Options Beyond YC / a16z

- Smaller funds focused on AI infrastructure (multiple plausible options).
- Angels — particularly AI-builder community angels who have lived the cloud-agent ceiling.
- Revenue-based financing (Lighter Capital, Capchase) once tooling-tier revenue is demonstrated.

### Decision Tree

If capital hasn't landed by week 12 AND the deploy curve isn't compounding (operator count growing <5% WoW per [[kpis]] week-9-12 primary KPI) AND no hero deal is visible:

- **Action 1:** activate the smaller-funds and angels list aggressively (treat that population as the fundraising path, not the contingency).
- **Action 2:** slow Hire 1 timeline; defer Hire 2 and Hire 3.
- **Action 3:** reassess Motion E weekly until either curve picks up or a structural change is needed.
- **Action 4 (only if all of the above fail by week 16):** consider whether a small hand-curated set of vendor conversations (Motion B exploratory, not active sales) makes sense to activate as a parallel revenue path. Risks A↔B conflict; only used as runway-bridge, not as primary motion.
