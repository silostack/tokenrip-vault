## 3 · Thesis

**Header:** The next big companies will be small.

AI-native companies are doing at five people what used to take fifty. The constraint isn't headcount anymore — it's that nothing your AI does in one place is known anywhere else. Every tool holds a fragment; no tool holds the company.

Big companies will spend the next decade retrofitting memory onto systems that never had it. You get to start with one.

**Pull-line:** A company brain is free to build at founding — and brutal to retrofit.

## 4 · Problem

**Header:** Every AI session starts from zero.

- Your co-founder's agent wrote the strategy doc. Yours has never heard of it.
- The call transcript exists — in someone else's notetaker. You ask them for the link. Again.
- Each teammate re-explains the company to their AI, every day, slightly differently.
- Switch tools, and your context stays behind. The vendor owns your memory.

**Kicker:** The AI tools got good. The memory never showed up. Tokenrip is the memory.

**Positions — what you believe.** Your ICP. Your pricing floor. Your voice. Your bets and the reasons behind them. The fifty things that make your company *your* company — written down once, read by every terminal.

**Pull-quote:** Every other "company brain" indexes what your company has written. Tokenrip also knows what your company believes.

Connect from Claude Code, Codex, Cowork, ChatGPT — via MCP or CLI, in about two minutes. Keep the full power of the tools you chose and the models you already pay for. No new app to live in. No vendor sandbox.

You, plus the research agent, the publishing agent, the closer. They work in different sessions and forget each other's work. Give them one brain, and one person runs like a company.

Contractors, partners, even your own website connect the same way: anything with an API key is a terminal, and each one sees only its slice.

## 7 · Modules

**Header:** Modules: abilities you install on the brain.

A module packages a capability your whole team can invoke — and everything it does lands back in the brain.

**Live example — the Publisher.** Type "publish the post" in any terminal. The module writes in your company voice (a position), generates the image through a shared API connection, publishes to your site, and files the post in the brain. Built once. Used by anyone, from anywhere.

**Line to keep:** Modules are generic. Your positions make them yours.

**Row of module cards:** Publisher (live) · Meeting memory · Research · Outreach · *Yours — we build custom modules →* (links to services page; FDE demoted to a path)

## 8 · Get started (the ladder)

**Header:** See it work in the next five minutes.

1. **Mount a brain — ours.** `npx tokenrip` or paste the MCP URL. No signup. Ask your own AI tool anything about Tokenrip — you're reading our real brain through a guest slice. *(This is also the docs.)*
2. **Build yours from your website.** Give it your URL. It extracts the facts and drafts your positions — labeled by confidence: *"inferred from your pricing page — correct me."*
3. **Correct it.** Fix what it got wrong about your company. Those edits are your first real positions.
4. **Open your other AI tool.** Connect. Ask it something about your company. It already knows.

**Caption:** The brain got built as a byproduct. That's the whole idea.

## 9 · FAQ

- **I could do this with a folder of markdown files.** You can — solo, in one tool. The folder dies at the first boundary: second person, second tool, an agent that needs its own slice, anything published. Tokenrip is what your notes become when they have to cross a boundary.
- **How is this different from Glean or Notion AI?** They index your files inside their app, with their models. Tokenrip works inside *your* tools with *your* models — and holds positions, not just documents.
- **Who sees what?** Every person, agent, and app gets a scoped slice. Nothing crosses a boundary you didn't draw.
- **What about my agents?** Agents are principals, like people: each gets a slice of the brain and a view tuned to its job. The closer reads sales calls; the publisher reads your voice.
- **What if we leave?** It's files and APIs. Export everything, any time.
