The Outbound Engine

Three machines and one record. The send machine (Alek's Six-State model) puts emails in front of owners. The reply machine sorts every answer, verifies it against the pool, suppresses what must never be emailed again, acknowledges the owner and creates the ledger row. The record is where David works and what the referral fee is paid on. Nothing in the engine talks to a prospect on its own; David does.

Three decisions this design makes

DECISION 1

Automate the sorting, never the conversation

Classification, verification, suppression, the acknowledgment and the ping run without a person from day one. The two to four positives a day go to David by hand. Drafted replies to prospects are off until the ledger holds 100 positives with David's reason codes.

DECISION 2

David's acknowledgment is a template with a clock

His spec (acknowledge, introduce me, copy me, give the timeline) is deterministic. One message, chosen by receipt time in Eastern, sent in the owner's thread from the mailbox he wrote to. No model writes it.

DECISION 3

One database, two ledgers, one bridge

The outreach layer records every company and every send, and it is ours. The handoff ledger records one row per positive reply, and both sides write to it. Only the reply machine creates a row in the second from the first.

The whole engine

Left to right: the send machine feeds Smartlead, replies come back into the reply machine, positives become rows in the record, David works the row. Two loops run underneath: every reply becomes a label on the send it answered, and every unsubscribe, bounce and CRM collision lands in one suppression table that the send machine checks before every push.

1

The send machine: Alek's model, with three corrections

Stages 1 to 5 of the Six-State Outbound Model stand as designed: nightly pull, ledger and buy-box gate, two drafted lines from stored facts with a validator, a 4 am planner, Smartlead sending from 30 mailboxes on six state domains. Three things change after the source-feed fetch and the 09-22 call.

Scoped in Alek's artifactMailboxes warming since ~09-22Connectors and tables to build

Contact append is the gate, not the connectors

Only about six of the 26 build-now feeds carry an email. Colorado UCC, the TCEQ feeds and the diesel registrations name a company and an address and stop. The model assumes 50% "valid email found"; for rural 2–25 employee companies known by name and address that number is unmeasured. Run 200 through website → email → verify first. Under ~25%, the general bucket (FMCSA, email-bearing rosters) carries week one.

The planner needs a ramp, not a fixed 150 per domain

Alek's planner allocates 150 per domain per day from day one, which is 900 a day. David's ramp is 500, then 1,000, then 5,000, and mailboxes fresh out of warmup should not jump to 30 a day. The planner gets a per-mailbox ramp (10, 15, 20, 30 over two weeks) and a global daily cap Simon sets; the state split runs under the cap.

Second domain per state starts warming now

One domain per state means a flagged domain takes a state dark for weeks, and warmup is the long pole. The second family should be warming today so it lands when it lands; it also moves each domain from 5 mailboxes to 3, inside Smartlead's guidance.

A

The acknowledgment: David's spec made exact

His spec: the owner gets a response acknowledging his message; David is introduced in the same message and copied; the owner gets the timeline. Receipt time is the reply's arrival in Eastern, whatever state it came from, because the promise is about David's day. The message is a reply in the owner's thread from the mailbox he wrote to, so it threads, carries the domain's DKIM, and does not look like a new cold email.

Why "today" is the dangerous word

A promise the ledger's two-hour clock cannot back is worse than no promise. Rule: the acknowledgment's timeline and the re-ping are generated from the same availability config (working hours, days off, holidays) that David edits himself, so the two never disagree and the message never names a day he is not working.

The scheduling link

David: people like picking a time, it creates an obligation, but a Calendly link in the first email might get caught in spam. The spam concern does not apply to an in-thread reply. Rule: no link in the cold sequence; a link in the acknowledgment, second to the phone promise. It writes the booked time to the ledger row. Drop it if bookings are under one in ten over the first 50 positives. Focus group Q12 (optional) tests the preference on Saturday.

Thanks for getting back to me, [first name]. David LaSaee, with one of the lenders we work with, will call you [today / tomorrow morning / Monday] at [phone]. If a specific time works better, you can pick one here: [link]. Nothing is needed from you before the call.

The sender problem the spec exposes

"I get introduced in the same message" assumes someone other than David is sending. But all 30 mailboxes are provisioned with David as the named sender. Those are two different machines, and the mailbox provisioning has already picked one while the spec assumes the other. It has to be settled before 10-06, and the cheapest test arrives Saturday.

The handoff chain, under variant I

  1. Owner replies; acknowledgment goes out from the Ironmark mailbox naming David as "with one of the lenders we work with." Nobody is copied
  2. Ledger row created and David pinged; he runs the CRM check; the thread is forwarded to his Providence address once clear
  3. David calls, identifies as Providence, says the partner line
  4. Credit application goes through Providence's own channel; Ironmark never holds an SSN, a bank statement or a guarantor's ID

Copying David at Providence in step 1 would surface Providence before the CRM check; copying him at an Ironmark address would contradict "he is with the lender." So no Cc.

Why the application stays with Providence

  • It is the pattern everywhere in lending: the intermediary stops at pre-qualification; LendingTree says so outright, Lendio adds a human who stays through the lender's application
  • The FTC Safeguards Rule now names "finders" and lead generators as financial institutions for consumer-purpose transactions; commercial EF is probably outside it, and holding no application data makes the question moot
  • An application taken by Ironmark is an application before the CRM check

Weaker than it looks: EF brokers take one-page applications with SSNs from new borrowers every day, and the Fed's studies say owners' mistrust of online lenders is about spam, not data. Focus group Q3 measures comfort instead of assuming it.

3

The record: two layers in one database, one bridge

Alek's stage-2 tables and David's ledger are not competing designs; they are two layers. The mistake to avoid is building David's ledger as a view over the outreach tables. It cannot be: the outreach layer holds every company we ever emailed and is ours alone; the ledger holds only people who replied positively and is shared with a counterparty who is paid on it.

Scoped in ledger.mdQuintel already has the handoff layer's schemav0 sheet due 09-24 · v1 page before 10-06

The sheet and the page

Keep the order in ledger.md (sheet now, page before 10-06) with one change: the sheet is a rendered export of the handoff layer that David also types into, synced both ways on his columns only. The pipeline writes the sheet, not a person, because the row and the reply text must exist minutes after the owner writes. If two-way sync with a sheet costs more than a day, skip it and ship the page with the same columns.

What David sees, in the order he hits it

  1. His queue: new leads not yet contacted, oldest first, clock running, the reply and the email replied to on the row
  2. The CRM check: clear, existing customer, contacted within nine months, other AE. Collisions go to suppression as pcf_book
  3. Export in his LeasePath column order, one click
  4. Four flags with dates, LeasePath id, referral coded
  5. Reason code and one line on every dead lead

The other moving parts

Beyond Alek's five stages and the reply machine, these have to exist or the engine is unsafe to run. Ordered by what breaks first.

01 · BREAKS A DOMAIN

One suppression table

Our touches across every domain, unsubscribes, hard bounces, DNC, pcf_book returns, David's April list, Alek's Origami campaigns, flpool touches. Checked at plan time and send time; mirrored to Smartlead's block list nightly and on every DNC event.

02 · BREAKS A DOMAIN

Deliverability monitor with brakes

Per domain and mailbox, daily: bounce, reply, spam-complaint rate from Google Postmaster, warmup health. A domain over 3% bounce is paused by the planner, not by a person noticing. A mailbox under the reply floor after 600 sends rotates out.

03 · BREAKS A DOMAIN

The ramp in the planner

Per-mailbox ramp (10, 15, 20, 30 over two weeks) and a global daily cap Simon sets. David's 500 → 1,000 → 5,000 is the same rule; Alek's 900 a day on day one is its third step.

04 · BREAKS TRUST

David's availability config

Working hours, days off, holidays, editable by him. Drives the acknowledgment's promise and the two-hour re-ping from one source so they never disagree.

05 · BREAKS TRUST

Ping and digest

Positive: Slack immediately, text if he wants it, with the packet and the reply. Two-hour re-ping if not marked contacted. 7 AM Eastern digest of overnight positives. Weekly numbers Tuesday. Nothing else pings.

06 · BREAKS TRUST

The trust surface

Site v0 before the first send: entity in the footer, street address, about page with names, LinkedIn page, the Quintel parent link. The landing page the email links to. The "how did you get my information" line the focus group picks.

07 · GATES VOLUME

Contact-append measurement

Website → email → verify on 200 address-only companies from Colorado UCC and TXG11. The found-and-verified rate replaces the model's 0.5 and decides whether the intent bucket fills at all.

08 · GATES VOLUME

Sampled morning review

A queue with approve and reject; a random 20 drafts total, not 20 per state. The push is blocked on rejections above a threshold, not on a person having read 120 emails before 4:30 am.

09 · LEGAL

Compliance footer and opt-out path

Ironmark's postal address (the entity has to exist), an opt-out that lands in suppression within minutes, plain text, no pixels. State disclosure and the truthful-header question go to counsel by 10-03.

10 · LEARNING

Measurement

Per arm, copy, state, feed, campaign: sent, delivered, bounced, replied by class, positive, routed, hours to contacted, application, funded. Wilson intervals; gates pre-registered (3% bounce; scale at 1% positive; rewrite under 0.3% after 600).

11 · LEARNING

Not-now re-entry and triggered sends

Parked rows re-enter on their date. Later, a row enters when its event fires (a new UCC, a truck added on the FMCSA diff). Write the planner so a row can enter from a trigger, not only the nightly batch.

12 · LEARNING

CRM collision write-back

When David marks a row existing customer, nine months, or other AE, the company goes to suppression as pcf_book and the send machine stops touching it. Until the sync exists, a person copies it.

Everything in the first block is small; the risk is not effort but forgetting one. The reply machine is built before the connectors, because the general bucket carries week one and the first positive arrives the first morning.
