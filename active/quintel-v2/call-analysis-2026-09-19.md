# The 09-19 call: David replaced the commercial model, and offered a narrower deal than the one we came for

## 1. The so what

The call was billed as a kickoff. It was a renegotiation. David put four things on the table that the gameplan did not contain and one that it had ruled out:

1. **The rev share we built the ladder on does not exist.** It is Max's, not Providence's; ownership has not approved it and, per David, would not. Anything above 2–3% of the house side is "dead on arrival."
2. **The replacement is a referral fee on funded deals**, coded by the AE at deal level, capped around $1,000, tiered by fee size, 100% to us, plus an undefined share of David's own 45%. Written and signed by David. Not by Providence.
3. **Applications are worth $10, maybe $100. Leads $3–4.** "Forget the application. Funded deals." That deletes the middle rung of our ladder.
4. **Everything routes through one AE.** We become David's ICP research, email and partner-sourcing arm; he closes; he grows a junior team once he hits $150–200K a month; the owners get an API conversation at $200K a month.
5. **The brand is Providence's.** He wants 200 Providence Capital Funding / PFC state domains, registered and held by us, sent from under Providence's name. That is commitment #19, which we reversed on 09-16, coming back with a bigger number.

Alongside, he gave the best operating data we have had: the real GM per deal, the cost base, the vendor share of business, the vendor acquisition base rate, the vendor ICP, a packaging lane, the trade-show playbook, and a validation of website/backlink help as the thing we can offer a vendor that a Danny Stewart cannot.

**Recommendation in one line.** Take the referral fee as the settlement mechanism and the vendor ICP as the lane; keep the brand, the site and the sending domains ours; get the fee confirmed by someone who owns Providence before signing anything; and treat David as the strongest single channel we have, not as the program.

## 3. What the deal on the table actually pays

Run the numbers David asked us to run.

**Per-deal ceiling.** Referral fee ≈ $800–1,000 per funded deal. Plus a share of David's 45% that he left open. At $7K GM, David's side is $3.5K; a 20% share of that is $700. So the realistic all-in is **$1,500–1,700 per funded deal through David**, and $1,000 through anyone else at Providence.

**Volume ceiling.** Providence funds 50–60 deals a month across 27 AEs. David alone reaching 20 funded a month would make him the top producer and hit his $150–200K target. That is the point at which he gets juniors. Until then, **our ceiling is roughly 20 deals × $1,500 = $30K a month, and the first months are a fraction of it.**

**What feeds it.** From the email arm: 900 sends → 1 application → at a 10% application-to-funded rate (David: 50 apps a day to get 5 approved) → 0.1 funded deals → ~$150 per 900 sends, ~$0.17 per email. Cold email alone does not get to 20 deals. Vendors do: five vendors at two to three deals a month is 10–15 deals. **The deal only works as a vendor-sourcing business.** That is what David said, from the other side of the table: "if you want this thing to scale up, you've really got to work on this vendor thing."

**Fee stacking.** Every mouth eats from the same ~$7K: the vendor asks "what's in it for me," a shop wants a finder's fee, we want the referral, the AE keeps his half, ownership keeps its half and the residual. David is right that if partner fees get large there is nothing left. Consequence: the partner's carrot has to be non-cash where possible (the website, the financing tab, speed of funding, the two-hour rule) and cash only on funded deals and only once.

## 4. What changes in the program, and what does not

**Changes.**

- **Commercials.** Drop the per-application rung. The ladder becomes: verified rows free → referral fee per funded deal (Providence's mechanism, David's chart) → a share of David's side, in writing. The 2–3% house-side figure is the ceiling for any later system-wide arrangement; note it and stop designing for 15–30%.
- **Lane order.** Vendors first, ahead of end-user email, ahead of technicians. The vendor ICP is scrapeable today: association member lists, outskirts filter, financing-tab scrape. The technician lane stays in the folder as a later experiment; David's fee-stacking point is the reason, not his skepticism.
- **The partner door becomes the vendor's financing tab.** The website fix Simon offered ("free if we can put a link on it") is the partner link from `site.md` in a different costume: our calculator embedded on the vendor's site, vendor pre-filled as seller. That is one component, shipped once, and it gives us the attribution David wants without an API into ListPad.
- **Trade show as a time-boxed experiment**, if at all. The list side is ours and cheap: exhibitor list, periphery booths, financing-tab scrape, a 5–7 touch sequence, a booking link. The floor side is David's body. Decide by 09-26 whether one show fits before the holiday gap; if not, plan Q1 and do the list build anyway.
- **The live ledger replaces David's Excel.** He asked for it. The funnel ledger (`funnel.md` §8) with four flags per row (application pulled, approved, funded, referral coded) is the attribution instrument and the thing that makes the referral fee auditable. Build it before the API conversation, not after.

**Does not change.**

- **The site and the sending domains are ours.** David's 200 Providence-branded domains are a request for Providence to make of Providence. We do not register or hold domains carrying another company's name, for three reasons already in `providence.md` §3: attribution, portability, and that David is not authorized to license the brand. Offer instead: Providence-branded sends from Providence-owned domains that David or Max registers, with our landing pages "in partnership with Providence Capital" if ownership signs that line. Our brand carries the end-user side.
- **The two-step thesis holds, with a higher bar for step 2.** David laid out what a broker of record is: credit pulls, a finance-company setup, lenders treating you as competition, and a "dying business" unless you stand behind deals. That is the graduation gate in `thesis.md` §7, stated by someone who has run one.
- **No demographic fields, no PII on our side, no guessed emails.** Nothing on the call touched these.

## 5. Risks the call opened

- **Contract with an AE, not the firm.** "I sign it." The referral fee is paid by Providence and coded by the AE; the share of David's 45% is David's to give. The first part needs a Providence signature (CFO or owner). The second part is a side agreement with an individual employee about his commission; ask whether Providence permits that before relying on it.
- **Max.** David undermined him on the record. Max is our other relationship and Alek's channel for warm leads this week. "Take care of Max" is David managing that. We should not be the ones who tell Max his rev share is not real; we should get ownership's version.
- **Single-AE dependency.** David is new, has no vendors, no juniors, and his own plan depends on hitting a number. If he leaves or stalls, the program is a list-building lab again.
- **Brand conflict on the borrower side.** Providence's back end is residuals, including the balloon the customer forgets. A site whose trust promise is "we tell you what it costs" is routing owners into that structure. Not a blocker for step 1; it is a line the site's disclosure and the "how this works" page must handle honestly, and a reason the routing table needs a second originator by v2.1.
- **Deliverability thinking that looks like spam infrastructure.** "Burn domains," 200 of them, PFC lookalikes matching a caller ID. Our outbound plan should stay on the side of the line where a regulator reading it would shrug.
