## 3. The brand decision: reverse commitment #19, with the reason

On 09-10 Alek agreed to Providence-branded domains (10–20 by state) on our infrastructure. The step-1 design reverses that for the funnel's outbound: Quintel-branded domains, landing on the Quintel site, replies landing with us. Reasons to give him, in his own terms:

1. **Dedupe.** He said the three-minute-per-row check against two locked CRMs is the thing that kills scale. A Quintel-branded send does not need it: if an existing Providence customer replies, David gets a warm call with his own customer and nobody stepped on anyone. The AE-karma problem only exists when the send carries Providence's name.
2. **Trust is solved at the handoff, not the header.** The email and the site never ask for PII. The first thing a responder is asked for that matters is asked by Providence, on Providence's paper, with Providence's 22 years. This is the funnel he heard Simon describe and did not reject.
3. **Fraud is solved by verification, which is his religion.** We only send to verified FMCSA/SOS entities and check every reply against the filed record. GW Capital sent blind. We do not.
4. **Specificity is the real trust signal.** An email that names his lease, his lender and his truck reads as someone who knows his business. A generic email from any brand, including Providence's, reads as spam. (This is arm B from flpool's email plan; still untested and worth saying so.)

What to offer so it is not a reversal but an addition: if he still wants Providence-branded sequences for rows we hand him (his warm callbacks, his existing book), we can run those too on the infrastructure already provisioned. Those are his sends to his people. The funnel's sends are ours.

Do not say: "this lets us sell to other originators." True, and he already knows it (he said the same thing would be asked by the next company). It does not need to be the headline on the call where he is asking how he gets paid.

## 4. The commercial answer for Saturday: a clean handoff of deals, not leads

He asked twice. Bring a structure, not a number that pretends to be final. The structure is a **clean handoff**: we find the borrower, pre-qualify him, verify the business, hold the relationship through the point where he has asked for a number, and hand Providence a **qualified application**, warm, with the evidence block. Providence takes it from there (application, PII, credit, funding). Three consequences that simplify everything he has worried about:

1. **CRM presence stops mattering.** We are handing over a deal, not claiming a lead. If the borrower is already in Providence's database, Providence routes him to the right rep internally; the deal still came through our door on our timestamp. The dedupe problem, the three minutes per row, the AE-karma rule: all gone, because we never claim a name, only a transaction.
2. **Existing-customer collisions stop being our problem to prevent.** His CRMs cannot batch-export and Providence would never share customer data anyway, so nothing in the design depends on knowing who is already theirs. Under the clean handoff the only list we screen against is our own (prior touches, unsubscribes).
3. **The deal is portable.** A qualified application is routed to the originator whose box fits. Providence is the first and, today, only originator on the panel. If Providence passes or cannot fund it, we place it elsewhere. Say this plainly; it is normal, and it is what makes the fee a fee for a deal.

**The ladder.** Compensation rises with how much of the borrower's journey we complete:

| Rung | What Providence receives | How we get paid | Policing |
|---|---|---|---|
| Verified row (today's pilot) | a dialable, in-box, verified company | nothing; R&D, continues as the lab | n/a |
| Qualified application, handed off | a business that asked for a number, verified, evidence block attached, warm | flat fee per qualified application. His own comparable: the $4–6 UCC lead that produced 5 apps per 10,000 | our timestamp; his confirmation of contact within one business day |
| Funded | a closed deal we sourced | share of Providence's revenue on the deal. He floated ~25% of funded value and ~$300 per funded deal unprompted on 09-10 | attribution definition below; monthly funded report |

**Attribution, written before any revenue share:** an attributed deal is one that entered through our funnel (our timestamp), where Providence's application date is after that timestamp, and funding occurs within 12 months, any rep. No CRM lookup on either side. He called the Max split unpoliceable; this is the policeable version and he will recognise it because the record is a transaction, not a name.

**He has already named the two streams.** His 09-18 message calls them "lead gen provider income for fully qualified leads (warm handoff)" and "participation income (shares in deal revenue)," and asks for pros and cons "and how to monitor it." Those are rungs 2 and 3 of the ladder in his words, and "how to monitor it" is the attribution definition. Let him present them; agree that both exist; put the monitoring answer (timestamp, monthly funded list) on the table as the thing that makes participation policeable. Pros and cons as we see them: lead-gen income is simple, early and paid whether or not Providence closes, but it makes Quintel a vendor and caps upside; participation aligns both sides on close rate, pays late, and only works with an attribution rule neither side has to trust the other's CRM for. The ladder takes both because they answer different risks.

**What we ask him to say yes to Saturday:** the clean-handoff shape; the ladder; the attribution definition in principle; and that he takes it to whoever at Providence signs. Numbers on the application fee follow the first 20 routed applications, which is the honest position and the one he has already accepted (test, then scale).

**His role, stated plainly if he opens the door:** he has said "the three of us" and "joint venture" on two calls and separated himself from Providence throughout. If ratios come in, the live question is whether he is on Providence's side of the table or ours. Do not raise it; if he does, the answer is that step 1 needs Providence as the first originator and needs him to run it, and that the structure above pays him through Providence today. Anything beyond that is a conversation after the first funded deal, not before.
