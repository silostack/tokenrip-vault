How the Ironmark Engine Works

Ironmark finds owners who are likely to buy equipment, emails them under its own name, and hands every real reply to the right lender with the context needed to call. The lender does everything from the call onward. A shared ledger records each step so both sides see the same thing and the referral is provable.

At a glance

Six steps. The first two are Ironmark's. The check is David's. The handoff is where the lead changes hands. Everything after it belongs to the lender.

Who is who

Ironmark

The name on the email and the website. A real person at Ironmark (a founder) is the sender and answers anything before the call. Ironmark's site says plainly that it is not the lender, that it works with a small number of funding partners, and that the application goes to the partner.

  • Finds and emails the owners
  • Sends the one handoff email after the check
  • Keeps the ledger and the suppression list
  • Holds no application, no SSN, no bank statements

David, at Providence

The first lender on the panel. He is introduced by name, with Providence's name, in the handoff email once the book check is clear, and copied on it at his Providence address. From the call onward it is his process.

  • Checks each reply against Providence's records
  • Calls the same day
  • Takes the application through Providence's own channel
  • Updates the ledger: contacted, application, approved, funded, and why not

The shared ledger

One place both sides look. A row exists only when a real person has replied with interest, never for a name on a list. Ironmark fills in what it knows; David fills in what happens.

  • The reply and the email it answered, on the row
  • The book-check result
  • Four flags with dates
  • Referral coded, fee reconciled monthly

The handoff, step by step

What happens between an owner replying and David's call. The book check comes before the owner hears anything back, so the one email they get already names the right person at the right lender.

Where a reply goes

The book check decides the route. The owner gets one email either way, and it names whoever is actually going to call.

What the owner hears, and when

The check comes first, then the reply.

When a real reply lands, David gets the ping right away with everything on the row. The owner's reply email waits for his check. That way the one email they receive already says who is calling and from which lender, and David is copied at Providence with nothing to walk back later. If the check has not happened within an hour of his working day, the owner gets a plain "someone from one of the lenders we work with will call you today" so nobody is left waiting, and the named email follows the check.

The email also offers a pick-a-time link after the phone promise, never in the first cold email. The promise and the two-hour reminder to David are driven by the same calendar, so the email never names a day he is not working.

What David sees

His queue

New replies not yet contacted, oldest first, with the clock running. On each row: the reply text, the email it answered, business name, state, equipment, years in business, fleet, any lien on file, the phone.

His columns

The book-check result. Four flags with dates: contacted, application, approved or declined, funded or dead. The LeasePath id and whether the referral is coded. A reason code and one line on anything that dies.

The reconciliation

Funded rows for the month with the referral fee due and paid. The row's timestamp is the attribution: the reply came through Ironmark before Providence's application date, and it funded within twelve months.

People and software

Software does

  • Finds the companies and writes the first email from public facts
  • Files every reply, creates the row, pings David
  • Sends the one handoff email after the check, with the right name in it
  • Stops emailing anyone who says stop, bounces, or is in Providence's book
  • Keeps the ledger and the numbers

People do

  • David checks the book and records the result
  • David calls, quotes, takes the application, closes
  • The Ironmark sender answers any question an owner asks before the call
  • David writes why a lead died, which is how Ironmark learns what to send next

No software ever talks to an owner on its own beyond the one handoff email.
