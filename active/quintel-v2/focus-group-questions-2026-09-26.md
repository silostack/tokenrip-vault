# Focus group questions for the weekend

Each question shows real text and asks for a choice. Show one variable at a time. Rotate the order of options between groups so the first one shown does not win by default.

## Before the questions: who is in the room

We need the split, not the names: how many own or run a business; of those, how many own trucks or equipment; how many have financed a unit in the last three years. Answers from owners who have financed count differently from everyone else's, so please keep them separable.

## 1. The first email: which one gets a reply

Show three emails, same offer, three senders. Ask: which would you answer, which would you delete, which would you report as spam. Then: why, in one line.

- **A, finance-forward:** from "[Name] at [Name] Equipment Finance." Subject: "Financing for your next truck." Body: we finance trucks and equipment for businesses like yours; are you adding or replacing anything this year?
- **B, under the radar:** from "[Name] at Rigsum." Subject: "Your next vac truck, the monthly." Body: we help owners in vac, towing, and septic see what a unit costs a month before they talk to a dealer; no application, no personal information; want the number on something you're looking at?
- **C, Providence:** from "David at Providence Capital Funding." Subject: "Financing for your next truck." Same body as A.

## 2. "How did you get my information"

Show three one-line answers and ask which one makes you keep reading and which makes you stop.

- "Your company is listed in public business records; we look at those, nothing private."
- "Your DOT registration is public; we saw you run [N] trucks in [state]."
- No explanation at all.

## 3. "What's the catch"

Show two answers to the question and ask which you believe.

- "We don't lend. When you want a real quote, a lender we work with calls you. They pay us a referral fee if it funds. You pay nothing to us."
- "There's no catch. We help owners find financing."

## 4. What you do next

After you get an email like B, what do you actually do? One choice: nothing; reply; Google the company; look at the website on your phone; ask ChatGPT or another AI whether it's real; ask a friend or your accountant. Then: what on a website tells you a company is real? Open answer; we want the list in their words (address, people's names, how long in business, reviews, phone number that is answered).

## 5. The handoff

Show this line and ask whether it makes you trust the company more, less, or the same: "We are not the lender. We send your request to a funding partner who has been doing this for over 20 years, and they call you." Then ask: would you rather the first email came from the lender, or from a company like this that sends you to the lender?

## 6. The monthly-payment tool

Show the calculator fields: equipment type, price, new or used, term, state, years in business. Ask: would you use this to see a monthly before calling a dealer, yes or no. Then show it with a phone number field added and ask again. Then with an email field. We want to know where the drop-off is.

## 7. Picking a time

Two replies to "yes, I'm interested." Which do you prefer: "What's the best number and time to reach you?" or a link where you pick a time on a calendar. Ask whether a calendar link in the very first email would make you more or less likely to reply.

## 8. Where the last truck came from

For anyone who has bought a unit in the last three years: where did you buy it (dealer, private party, online marketplace, auction) and who arranged the financing (the dealer, your bank, a finance company that called you, nobody, paid cash). One line each. This tells us where buyers actually meet lenders.

## What we need back

- Counts per option for each question, split by owners-who-financed versus everyone else.
- The one-line "why" answers verbatim for questions 1, 3 and 4. The words they use are the copy.
- How the group was recruited and how the questions were presented (read aloud, shown on a screen, sent by text).

## What we are not asking

Anything about Providence's rates or terms, anything about David's role, and no question that describes the product instead of showing it.
