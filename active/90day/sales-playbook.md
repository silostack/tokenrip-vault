*Created 2026-07-15 · Owner: Simon · Status: LIVING DOC — update after every week's review. Version log at the bottom of [[call-log]].*

> **The one-line mission: 5 funded Quintel customers by ~Oct 13. You get there through ~30–50 decision-maker discovery conversations, run by you, iterated relentlessly.**

## 0. The math (why the volume is the strategy)

Work the funnel backwards from 5 funded:

| Stage                      | Conservative rate                                 | Needed               |
| -------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- | -------------------- |
| Funded customers           | —                                                 | **5**                |
| Proposals / offers made    | ~40% close (founding-customer pricing, real pain) | ~12–15               |
| DM discovery conversations | ~30% advance to proposal                          | **~40–50**           |
| Dials + connect attempts   | ~10–15% reach a DM live                           | **~400–600 touches** |

400–600 touches over ~60 working days = **20 dials + 25 LinkedIn connects a day**. That's the whole secret. Nobody is out-thinking this funnel — you feed it or you starve it. When results lag, the first question is always *"did the volume happen?"* — only after a yes do you debug the script.

**Timezone trick that extends your calling day:** call East Coast 8:30–10:30am ET (before their day swallows them), Central/Mountain midday, West Coast 4–6pm ET. Decision-makers answer their own phones early and late — receptionists work 9–5.

## 1. The ICP — who gets a dial

**Company:** independent / regional equipment-finance **lenders** first (~70% of list), high-volume placement/brokerage firms second (~30%). 11–200 employees. US only for now.

- Why lender-weighted: lenders showed pricing power ($10K setup + $5K/mo "not flinching" — 6/30); the 2-person shops balk (Bo Brauer 6/30); balance-sheet lenders have the budget line.
- Why not banks' EF divisions: committee buying, 9-month cycles. Park them.

**Person (the ONLY titles that get a dial):**
- Owner / President / CEO / Founder / Managing Partner (small shops — they sign)
- Chief Credit Officer / CCO (they own the buy box)
- **VP / SVP / Director / Head of Originations** ← the Katharine profile — the strongest pull in the log came from exactly this title
- Chief Sales Officer / SVP Sales (mid-size)

**Hard exclusions:** Sales Manager, Account Manager, BDR, marketing titles. Seven weeks of flat calls with these titles is enough data. If one lands on your calendar anyway, run the call *only* to get referred up (§7, "referral flip").

### The 30 seconds — pain hypothesis (VERSIONED — A/B one variable at a time per §9)

Lead with a **specific, falsifiable pain sentence they react to** — never "what's your biggest pain with sourcing?" (too much work for a cold stranger to generate; that's a *warm-call* question, §6). Hand them a sentence; they agree or disagree. Then **shut up** — whatever they say next is the call.

Variants ranked by how well the pain is corroborated in the call log (see §14, 2026-07-16). **v1.1 is the best-corroborated pain (3 voices); v1.0 is the incumbent control (1 voice).** Freeze two, run them head-to-head over the first ~20 dials, let the market vote.

- **v1.1 — Triage grind** *(RECOMMENDED lead — Mike Ryan + Katharine + Bo)*:
  > "…the sentence I keep hearing from heads of origination is that Monday morning they're staring at a few thousand companies with no real way to know which ten deserve a call this week — so it's gut feel, or whoever's on top of the list. Is that how it works at [Company], or have you got that dialed in?"
  >
  > *Simon's own shorter spoken cut (the one that got a forward on 7/17 — use whichever feels natural):* "We've been hearing that sales teams are having trouble ranking their prospects, so they know who to call first. Is that something your team can relate to, or do you have that figured out?"

- **v1.0 — Signal latency** *(the control — Katharine)*:
  > "…here's the sentence I keep hearing: *'we find out about the good deals after somebody else funded them.'* A company expands a facility, wins a contract, files a UCC — and by the time it's visible, it's shopped. Does that happen at [Company], or have you got sourcing fully handled?"

- **v1.3 — Filter-the-junk** *(the inverse — reach for it the moment they say "we're not short on leads"; Devan + Katharine)*:
  > "…a lot of originators tell me the problem isn't too *few* leads — it's that most of what they chase turns out un-financeable: wrong equipment, no hard asset, bad credit, and they only find out after burning the call. Is that a thing for your team?"

- **v1.4 — Fragmentation** *(turns the "we already do this with ChatGPT" objection into the hook; Katharine + TFG)*:
  > "…what I keep hearing is sourcing has quietly become one person with a ChatGPT tab and a spreadsheet, cross-referencing ZoomInfo and UCC filings by hand — works right up until they leave or volume grows. Sound familiar, or is yours more systematized than that?"

- **v1.2 — BDO-forward** *(optional — leads with the headcount frame instead of a pain; pre-empts the ZoomInfo reflex inside the opener)*:
  > "…I build something that works like the research half of an entry-level BDO — reads the entire public record, never sleeps, and hands your originators a ranked call list of who's about to need financing, with the *why*. Most desks already have lead data; what they don't have is someone reading all of it and saying who to call first. Is that the gap at [Company]?"

**Routing after the hook:**

- Pain shows up → §6 discovery spine.
- "We're fine / all referral / all vendor-sourced" → one probe: *"Got it — rough number: what share of your volume is those channels? And if a financeable deal existed in your box outside them, would you even want to see it?"* If genuinely no gap: *"Fair enough — sounds like it's working. Who do you know at a shop still hunting cold?"* (dead call → referral attempt) and out in 90 seconds. **A fast no is a win.** Log it, next dial.
- They ask "what is it? / how does it work?" → that's the cue for the **BDO/headcount answer (§3), not a product dump** — it sets the $4–6K slot, not the $500 tool slot.

## 6. Discovery spine (the meeting itself — questions before demo, always)

Run these in rough order. **Do not open a screen until pain is on record in their words.**

1. **The missed deal:** "Walk me through the last deal you found out about too late — one that funded with someone else. What would you have needed to catch it?"
2. **The volume math:** "How many deals does the team fund a month? How many do you *look at* to fund one?"
3. **Current sources:** "Where does flow come from today — referral, vendor, cold? Rough percentages?"
4. **The cost:** "When a deal in your box goes to another lender, what's that worth? Ballpark margin on a typical funding?"
5. **The team:** "Who does the hunting — how many originators, and what does their day look like?"
6. **The Monday-morning triage:** "When your originators sit down Monday, how do they decide who to call first? What does that list actually look like today?"
7. **The decision path:** "If this turned out to be exactly what you needed, what happens next on your side — who else looks at it, and what would they need to see?"
8. **Budget mapping (listen, don't ask directly):** do they compare you to a *tool* (ZoomInfo, $500/mo mental slot) or to a *hire* ("this is like a junior BDO," $4–6K/mo slot)? Steer with the BDO frame from §3.

**What the answers tell you:**

| Signal | Read |
|---|---|
| Specific missed deal, named, with visible irritation | Pain voice. Advance hard — propose within 48h. |
| Vague "we could always use more leads" | Vitamin. One more probe, then disqualify politely. |
| Q6 answer = gut feel / no real list / "whoever answered the phone" | The ranked-stream value prop is live — triage is the product. |
| They ask price unprompted | Buying signal (Katharine did this). Answer confidently, no discounting, return to discovery. |
| They redirect to comp/rev-share (Max pattern) | Not pain — economics tourism. Give the §3 partnership line ONCE, then require a pain answer before more structure talk. |
| "Send me something" | The brush-off. Counter: "Happy to. So I send the right thing: [Q1 again, compressed]." If still nothing — send the one-pager, park, next. |

**Demo rules (when earned):** ≤30 min · their sectors, not a generic tour · start from their stated pain ("you said X — here's X") · end with the next step scheduled **on the call** and costing them something (data, a signed pilot, a dated owner-call).

## 8. Rules of engagement (the ten commandments — tape these up)

1. **Calls before code. Every day. No exceptions during the front-load.**
2. Titles that can't sign don't get dials — they get referral flips.
3. Discovery before demo. No pain on record = no screen share.
4. **Every next step costs the prospect something.** Money, data, a signed pilot, a dated call with the signer. If the next step is "Simon builds something free," the answer is no.
5. One offer, one price, spoken with a straight face. No discounting on warmth — warmth is when your leverage is *highest* (Stephanie call 1 lesson).
6. Every conversation ends with an ask. Meeting, pilot, referral — something.
7. A fast no is a win. Disqualify cheerfully, harvest a referral, next.
8. Ask, don't explain. When you're talking >40% of a discovery call, you're losing it (your documented 70/30 inversion — run 30% explain / 70% extract).
9. Log every touch same-day. An unlogged call didn't happen.
10. **The pitch is on trial, never you.** Bad call = data for the next script version. That's the whole deal we made.

## 9. Script iteration protocol ("vary every 5 calls," elaborated)

This is engineering — you already know how to do this. It's A/B testing with a sample size of your own mouth.

1. **Version everything.** Opener v1.0, pain-hypothesis v1.0, close v1.0 — written down in [[call-log]], never improvised drift.
2. **After every 5 dials, a 10-minute retro.** One question: **where did calls die?** (Before the 30 seconds? During the pain hypothesis? At the meeting ask? On an objection?)
3. **Change ONE variable per version.** Death at the open → new first sentence (v1.1). Pain hypothesis not landing → new pain sentence (v1.2). Never change two things at once — you learn nothing.
4. **Graduate what works.** A line that produces 2 meetings stays frozen until something beats it. Log *which version* each meeting came from.
5. **Steal from the market.** The best script lines are sentences prospects say. The first time a lender describes the pain in words better than yours (they will), that sentence becomes v-next verbatim. Three different buyers using similar words = the message has written itself; stop iterating that block.
6. **Calibration rule: calls 1–10 don't count.** They're for finding your voice. Expect them to be rough *by design*. Review them for mechanics (talk ratio, did you ask for the meeting) — not for verdicts.
7. **Recording:** don't record cold dials (consent laws vary by state; not worth the overhead). Structured notes within 60 seconds of hangup instead. DO record scheduled Zoom discovery calls (with the standard "mind if I record?") — and review one per week against §6.
