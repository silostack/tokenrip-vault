# Martin Roth — Meet-and-Greet Tear Sheet

## The read (10 seconds)
- **Who he is:** elite B2B sales operator. Reads people fast; values clarity, directness, hustle, coachability, and builders who *ship*. Mentor type — likely warm and generous.
- **The subtext:** Petar told him he needs an engineer and floated you. So even with "no agenda," he's probably quietly sizing you up as a possible technical partner. You don't pitch — but be ready for it to come up.
- **Your job on this call:** be genuinely curious, be easy to work with, show (don't claim) that you ship. That's it. The rest takes care of itself.

---

## The one thing to decide BEFORE the call
**"Would you ever be up for building something like this / are you open to that kind of work?"**
Have an answer ready so you're neither trapped nor dismissive. A clean default:
> *"I'm heads-down on my own thing right now — but I'm always open to working with sharp people on interesting problems. Let's get to know each other and see."*

Warm, honest, keeps every door open, commits to nothing. Decide your real posture (focused / open / actively interested) so this comes out steady, not improvised.

## Questions you might genuinely ask him (no-agenda, and you'll actually learn)
Pick 2–3 you're honestly curious about — these are what you'd ask any mentor-figure who's done what he's done:
- **Selling before the product exists** — how do you hold pre-sold customers while the thing gets built? How much do you pre-sell vs. build? *(This is your exact challenge; he's the master of it.)*
- **Selling into an old, skeptical, relationship-run trade** — construction, equipment. What actually cracks the first deals? What objections kill them? *(Your Mike "what's your hit rate?" problem, from the master.)*
- What pulled you out of the Procore world to start something new?
- What's your vision for Filmore — where does it go after launch?
- For a technical founder grinding toward first revenue — what would you do differently if you were me?

## Handling the overlap (important)
- **Be open, be collaborative, be un-threatened.** You're one seat apart, not head-to-head. Naming it plainly builds trust; hiding it looks cagey.
- **Hold the crown jewel light.** Fine to say "we rank on the customer's own deal history." No need to walk him through the revealed-preference ranker / the exact IP. Warm ≠ full disclosure.
- If it clicks, the genuinely interesting idea to let *emerge* (don't force it): **dealer-side and lender-side are two products on one engine.** Only go there if the conversation earns it.

---

## Posture reminders
- **Show, don't claim.** Ship-ability comes through in *how* you talk about building, not in a résumé.
- **Match his energy** — direct, concrete, a little fast. Vague and hedgy reads as weak to him.
- **It's a two-way vibe check.** You're also deciding if *he's* someone you'd want in your corner. A $500M-exit GTM operator who likes you is worth a lot regardless of whether Filmore ever happens.
- **No pitch. No ask. Leave him wanting the next conversation.**
