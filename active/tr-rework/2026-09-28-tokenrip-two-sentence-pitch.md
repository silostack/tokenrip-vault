# Tokenrip’s pitch should lead with shared work and continuity

Date: 2026-09-28  
Mode: Startup  
Status: DRAFT

## Recommended wording makes the product and benefit concrete

> Tokenrip is a shared workspace for people and AI agents. It keeps a project’s files, discussions, and decisions together so any agent can pick up where the work left off.

The audience is people using agents for ongoing work, with non-coding examples such as sales, research, and deals. The objective is a spoken answer to “What is Tokenrip?” that a listener can accurately repeat after hearing it once. Simon endorsed the continuity framing; this tighter wording remains a recommendation pending review.

An optional example for follow-up:

> A deal workspace gives your team, outside collaborators, and their agents a shared place for the deal’s materials and discussions.

## Continuity is the benefit; shared context enables it

In this office-hours conversation, Simon described shared workspaces that any agent can connect to, including sales materials and deal rooms accessible to third parties. He confirmed that the model supports materials, version history, reasoning, discussions, and decisions. Recording that context depends on users and agents making use of those capabilities. These are founder-provided descriptions, not independently verified product or customer evidence.

Simon also identified a constraint: agents can generate enough context that relevant information becomes difficult to find. The pitch therefore depends on agents being able to recover useful context, not just on the ability to store records. Reliable continuation remains the key product assumption to demonstrate.

Earlier vault framing emphasized a [collaboration layer for agents](../../product/tokenrip/tokenrip-positioning.md) and [deployment of business workflow agents](../../product/tokenrip/external-positioning.md). This draft reflects the audience and benefit clarified in this session; it does not update those older documents.

## Direct product language fits this audience better than a Git analogy

- **Recommended: shared workspace plus continuity.** Immediately usable in conversation; concrete enough for nontechnical agent users. Its main risk is sounding like generic document storage unless the continuation benefit lands.
- **Technical alternative: repository continuity for knowledge work.** Reuses the familiar relationship between current state and history. It requires familiarity with development workflows and invites technical comparisons that distract from the intended audience.
- **Earlier wording: workspace manager for agentic collaboration.** Compact, but leaves the listener to infer both the activity and benefit.

Coding is excluded from the lead examples as a positioning choice. The assertion that its market is “taken” was not validated and is unnecessary to this choice.

## Test comprehension before adding more explanation

Use the two sentences in conversation or a short written introduction. No implementation, publishing, outreach, or broader positioning-document changes are included in this brief.

**Assignment:** Show only the two sentences to one person who uses AI agents for non-coding work. Ask them to describe what Tokenrip does and name a project where they would use it. An answer about continuing work with retained context supports the wording. An answer limited to file storage, or an inability to name a use, indicates that the benefit or example needs revision. This tests comprehension and relevance, not willingness to pay.

This follows [YC’s two-sentence comprehension test](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/how-to-pitch-your-company/). The remaining concern is whether “pick up where the work left off” communicates a credible, distinctive benefit without additional explanation.
