# Travo, PARES, and the Data-Flywheel Path to an AI-Native Quintel Brokerage

**Research date:** 2026-08-20

**Depth:** Deep dive

**Decision supported:** How Quintel should sequence a proprietary borrower-data operation into a full equipment-finance brokerage, and what must be proven before the second phase follows from the first.

## Executive Summary

**Travo and PARES together support one staged Quintel motion: first build a proprietary ground-truth engine by doing origination work, then use that informational advantage and operating system to own more of the transaction. They do not establish that phase two follows automatically.** Travo shows the first half cleanly: automate the work required to collect, reconcile, refresh, and prove data that incumbents do not possess. PARES supplies the second half: software initially built for brokers becomes the internal operating system of a brokerage and property-management firm. The combined pattern is data production → workflow leverage → service ownership.

The transferable insight is not the label “AI-native brokerage.” It is that **the service operation creates the proprietary data, and the proprietary data makes the service operation increasingly advantaged.** Quintel's borrower calls can create dated financing maturities, capex windows, incumbent relationships, financing-method history, signal labels, lender-routing outcomes, and eventual funding results. That ground truth is more defensible than the public trigger feed and more valuable than a generic brokerage CRM. It is also the information required to run a better brokerage.

The strategic correction is that one motion still requires **two independent proofs**. Phase one must show both that calls create reusable proprietary timing data and that the data converts into lender-accepted opportunities at viable economics. A useful dataset with no transaction lift supports an intelligence company, not a brokerage. Conversely, a founder who closes one idiosyncratic deal without repeatable data capture supports a services business, not a compounding AI-native one. Quintel should advance only when both loops work.

The recommended sequence is therefore:

```text
public evidence + lender box
  → human-led borrower outreach
  → proprietary timing / need / incumbent data
  → qualified, permissioned lender handoff
  → accepted submission and outcome ledger
  → one-deal concierge packaging test
  → segment-specific full brokerage where added work earns added economics
  → software becomes the internal operating system and, selectively, a lender-facing product
```

This is one strategic motion but not one universal workflow. Envision currently offers the same 1% for a raw referral or a worked application; VFI requires a lender-ready package and live borrower introduction before brokerage economics begin. Full transaction ownership is rational in the second lane and value-destructive in the first unless it materially increases the funding rate. [[ricky-da-silva]]:30-39 · [[stauss-paulos-2026-08-17]]:51-64.

## Core Questions Explored

1. What does Travo actually do, how does it create proprietary data, and where does it stop short of brokerage?
2. Which parts of Travo's operating model transfer to Quintel's borrower-timing and origination thesis?
3. What does PARES reveal about moving from software for brokers to an AI-native services firm?
4. Can the data flywheel and full brokerage be treated as one motion, and what gates connect the phases?
5. What first- and second-order effects would follow if Quintel owned the borrower relationship and transaction process?

## Travo Is a Proprietary-Data Company, Not a Brokerage

### Travo uses AI to industrialize the data-collection labor incumbents perform manually

**Fact.** Travo describes itself as real-estate data infrastructure serving private-equity firms, developers, investors, and brokers. Its current product covers ownership, zoning, pricing, rental comparables, operating data, market activity, and property-level analysis. It starts with under-covered asset classes including RV parks, campgrounds, mobile-home parks, marinas, and multifamily. [Travo](https://www.travoai.com/) · [Y Combinator profile](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/travo).

**Fact.** The collection system combines public records and web crawling with emails and phone calls. This matters because the highest-value operating data does not consistently exist in county systems or commercial databases. The company says it tried building real-estate workflow agents first, then concluded that an agent could not materially outperform a strong analyst without better underlying data. It consequently made the dataset the foundation and applications the downstream layer. [Y Combinator profile and launch](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/travo).

**Fact.** Travo's homepage makes trust and provenance visible: records are cross-referenced and deduplicated; individual fields expose their origin and date; conflicting values are flagged rather than silently collapsed. The company publicly claims 18,000+ properties, 100M+ data points, and customers managing $320B+ of assets. These are company-reported scale claims, not audited revenue, retention, or accuracy evidence. [Travo](https://www.travoai.com/).

The incumbent comparison confirms that the work is real. CoStar reports that its research organization collects commercial-real-estate information through phone calls, email, field inspections, public records, news monitoring, third-party feeds, and customer input. Travo's innovation is not inventing a new data source; it is attempting to replace the labor-heavy collection and reconciliation system with a smaller AI-mediated one. [CoStar 2024 annual report](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1057352/000119312525107498/d861510dars.pdf).

### Travo's moat claim is accumulated, verified coverage—not an autonomous agent

Travo's external language is instructive. The headline is “Real Estate Data Nobody Else Has,” not “an AI analyst that replaces your team.” AI sits inside the production system; the customer buys hard-to-find, current, inspectable truth. The visible output is a structured property record, a comp set, an off-market search result, or an operational recommendation. [Travo](https://www.travoai.com/).

This is a stronger framing than generic AI because it makes four things testable:

1. **Coverage:** does the record exist?
2. **Freshness:** when was the field last confirmed?
3. **Provenance:** where did it come from?
4. **Decision value:** did it change sourcing, underwriting, or asset operations?

Travo's data rights are also deliberately bounded. Its terms say customer data remains the customer's, is not shared with other customers, and is not used to train models deployed to other customers; aggregated or de-identified data may be used to improve the service. This is structurally similar to Quintel's “share the evidence, privatize the score” doctrine, though the implementation and contractual details differ. [Travo terms](https://www.travoai.com/terms) · [[quintel-sourcing-intelligence-prd-2026-06-29]]:117-134.

### Travo does not validate transaction ownership

**High-confidence fact.** The public materials reviewed describe paid data and intelligence services, not representation of buyers or sellers, transaction negotiation, or commission income. Travo's terms define the service as informational and require customers to verify outputs with qualified professionals. [Travo terms](https://www.travoai.com/terms).

The distinction is load-bearing. Travo owns the **information-production loop** while leaving fiduciary judgment and transaction execution with the customer. It proves that direct outreach can manufacture proprietary ground truth without becoming the intermediary. It does not prove that adding intermediary obligations improves the business.

## The Travo Translation: Borrower Calls Should Manufacture Ground Truth

### Quintel's call operation can become the data product even when the borrower has no current need

Quintel's focus memo already defines the raw ingredients of the analogue. Every reached borrower call is meant to capture whether the public signal is real, whether it implies equipment, the financing method, current financing maturity, next capex window, incumbent lender, disposition, and permission to recontact. [[quintel-focus-realignment-2026-08-15]]:186-231.

The Travo lens changes the interpretation of that work:

| Call result | Transaction interpretation | Data-flywheel interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Live need | Immediate deal | Signal label + need + timing + route + outcome |
| Need already financed | Lost deal | Incumbent capture, channel, timing, and lender evidence |
| No current need, dated capex | No deal today | Proprietary future origination calendar |
| Signal false | Dead lead | Negative label that recalibrates the scorer |
| Wrong contact, referral given | Friction | Decision-maker graph correction |
| Do not contact | No opportunity | Durable suppression and trust protection |

This makes the data flywheel economically relevant before funding, but it does not make every call valuable. A generic note, an undated “not now,” or a fact already available in a UCC filing is not a proprietary asset. The qualifying standard should be **new, structured, attributable, dated, permissioned, and joined to a future action or model outcome.**

### The sprint requires a control, not only a volume target

The current sprint asks whether 150 contacted surfaced companies produce at least three confirmed needs and one lender-accepted submission. [[quintel-focus-realignment-2026-08-15]]:82-86. That is the correct company-level kill criterion, but it does not isolate whether Quintel's signals outperform ordinary prospecting.

The cheapest disconfirming design is a small control cohort inside the same lender box:

```text
80%: Quintel-ranked, signal-backed companies
20%: box-matched companies without a qualifying Quintel signal
```

Compare reach rate, confirmed-need rate per reached decision-maker, reusable timing-data rate, and accepted-submission rate. If both cohorts perform similarly, the founder calling motion may work while the proprietary signal thesis fails. Without the control, a successful broker can be mistaken for a successful data engine.

### The data flywheel needs its own ledger, rights, and economics

The ledger should distinguish four layers:

1. **Public evidence:** filings, awards, permits, UCCs, hiring, news.
2. **Borrower-stated ground truth:** timing, equipment plan, incumbent, financing method, budget.
3. **Transaction evidence:** permission, introduction, documents requested, proposal, submission, approval, funding.
4. **Derived intelligence:** score changes, lender fit, predicted timing, future priority.

Borrower-stated and transaction data require explicit usage and retention rules. The right to introduce a borrower is not automatically the right to retain sensitive financial documents, use the outcome to improve models, or contribute a de-identified result to a pooled intelligence layer. Those rights should be designed before the first full package, not inferred after it.

## PARES Shows the Second Phase: Software Becomes the Operating System of the Service Firm

### PARES has moved publicly from “software for brokers” to “we are the broker”

**Fact.** PARES launched through Y Combinator as an all-in-one platform for commercial-real-estate brokers: live transaction CRM, property targeting and skip tracing, outbound-call workflow, AI underwriting, and automated offering memoranda and broker opinions of value. [Original YC launch](https://www.ycombinator.com/launches/Nxo-pares-ai-helping-commercial-real-estate-brokers-find-close-more-deals).

**Fact.** Its current homepage and YC company description instead position PARES as an “AI-native real estate services firm” providing investment-sales brokerage and property management. The firm says it prospects sellers, underwrites deals, creates marketing materials, finds buyers, runs the marketing process, negotiates contracts, and performs back-office support. It presents the software as “the operating system behind every deal.” [PARES](https://www.pares.ai/) · [current YC profile](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/pares-ai).

The direction of travel is unusually relevant to Quintel:

```text
build software for incumbents
  → discover adoption / fragmentation constraints
  → operate the workflow directly
  → make the software internal leverage
  → sell the service outcome, not software adoption
```

Public evidence does not establish why PARES changed models, whether it has fully stopped selling software, or whether the brokerage has superior margins. A recent founder post still promotes standalone valuation/underwriting software, and the public LinkedIn company description retains the older software framing. The company appears to be in a recent or incomplete transition. [Zihao Wang on LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/in/zihao-wang-mh) · [PARES LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/company/pares-ai).

### PARES hides the technology behind a human service promise

PARES does not present the customer with autonomous agents. It leads with “your trusted partner,” “sell higher, close faster,” and “one firm, two services, zero hand-offs.” Brokerage is described as led by experienced brokers and powered by AI and proprietary data. Property management is described as run by operators. [PARES](https://www.pares.ai/).

This is the correct external hierarchy for an AI-native services firm:

1. **Outcome:** better transaction or operating performance.
2. **Accountability:** one firm owns the process.
3. **Human trust:** named professionals remain responsible.
4. **Technology:** the internal system provides speed, coverage, consistency, and margin.

If Quintel advances to brokerage, “AI-native equipment-finance broker” should remain an investor/operating description, not necessarily the borrower promise. The borrower-facing offer is closer to: **one equipment-finance origination partner that knows the market, brings the right capital, and stays accountable through close.**

### PARES uses an adjacent recurring service to bridge the space between transactions

PARES pairs episodic investment-sales commissions with property management, which creates recurring fees, continuous operational data, and a relationship before the eventual disposition. The services reinforce each other: management reveals asset performance and owner intent; brokerage monetizes acquisition, exchange, and sale events. [PARES](https://www.pares.ai/).

Quintel's closest analogue is not borrower software. It is an ongoing **capital calendar**:

- financing maturities and renewal windows;
- next equipment-replacement and capex cycles;
- vendor and asset-class changes;
- periodic market reads and lender-fit updates;
- a standing mandate to re-open financing at the right time.

The revenue may still be transaction-based. The recurring asset is the permissioned relationship and dated timing data, not necessarily monthly borrower SaaS. This preserves the focus memo's conclusion that borrowers should not be asked to use a product for an event occurring every two or three years. [[quintel-focus-realignment-2026-08-15]]:161-169.

## One Motion, Two Proofs, and a Progressive Ownership Ladder

### Phase one sets up brokerage only if the data changes transaction economics

The unified thesis is coherent:

> Working the market produces private timing and intent data. That data improves future sourcing, qualification, routing, and trust. Better transaction performance earns more borrower relationships and lender outcomes, which produce still better data.

The causal link is not yet proven. Phase one must pass two tests simultaneously:

| Proof | Required evidence | Failure means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Data compounding** | Reached calls regularly produce new, dated, reusable information and permission for a future action; corrections and outcomes improve ranking or timing | Calling is a conventional brokerage activity, not a proprietary data engine |
| **Transaction conversion** | Quintel-ranked outreach produces confirmed needs and accepted submissions at a better rate or lower cost than a matched control | The dataset may be interesting, but it does not create an advantaged brokerage |

The data asset cannot be used to excuse weak transaction economics. “The calls are teaching us” is legitimate during a bounded experiment and dangerous as a permanent defense of an unprofitable acquisition channel.

### Full brokerage should expand deal by deal, not arrive as a binary company pivot

Quintel should use a progressive ownership ladder:

| Level | Quintel owns | Evidence / economics required |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0. Intelligence | Ranked company, trigger, evidence, lender fit | Customer pays for software or data |
| 1. Qualified referral | First contact, timing, need, permissioned introduction | Referral fee plus outcome data |
| 2. Packaged opportunity | Financial-document collection, capital ask, equipment/use-of-funds package | Higher acceptance/funding rate or higher fee covers incremental work |
| 3. Brokered transaction | Multi-lender routing, proposal comparison, negotiation support, process control | Licensing, borrower mandate, lender bench, auditable fee and event controls |
| 4. Capital relationship | Renewal calendar, repeat capex, proactive market reads | Repeat transactions and lower reacquisition cost |

The ladder reconciles Envision and VFI rather than forcing one model onto both. Envision currently pays the same fee at Levels 1 and 2, making Level 1 the rational default unless qualification materially improves funding probability. VFI says Level 2 plus a live introduction is the threshold for any success fee, making one concierge package the correct test. [[ricky-da-silva]]:30-39 · [[stauss-paulos-2026-08-17]]:51-64.

### The transition to phase two needs explicit gates

The Sep. 26 decision should not be “broker or software.” It should ask whether Quintel earned the next level of ownership.

**Minimum gates for a full-brokerage pilot:**

1. **Signal advantage:** the ranked cohort beats the matched control on confirmed need or time-to-need.
2. **Reusable private data:** a meaningful, measured share of reached calls produces a dated financing/capex record or a durable correction. The first 50 reached calls should set the baseline before a threshold is fixed.
3. **Transaction proof:** at least three confirmed needs and at least one opportunity accepted for credit submission, consistent with the existing sprint.
4. **Work-content economics:** incremental packaging and transaction work either earns a higher fee or lifts expected funding enough to cover fully loaded labor and compliance cost.
5. **Capital optionality:** at least two credible lender destinations exist for the selected box; otherwise Quintel is a captive lead generator, not a borrower-side broker.
6. **Control and trust:** borrower permission, document-security workflow, lender-event reporting, fee attribution, and data rights are explicit.
7. **Legal readiness:** counsel has mapped licensing, disclosure, solicitation, and compensation requirements for the jurisdictions and handoff mode in the pilot.

Only gates 1–3 belong inside the current six-week experiment. Gates 4–7 are the preconditions for expanding one accepted opportunity into a full brokerage pilot.

## Strategic Analysis

### First-order effects

- **The customer changes.** Lenders become capital partners and downstream buyers of packaged opportunities; the borrower becomes the primary relationship Quintel must earn and protect.
- **The product changes.** The deal, introduction, and execution become the output. The software becomes the operating system, evidence ledger, and margin engine.
- **Founder time initially increases.** AI does not remove discovery, trust, negotiation, exceptions, or lender management before the process is known. The first brokerage phase is more services-heavy than the current product.
- **Pricing resistance falls but revenue becomes contingent.** A lender can accept a deal without buying seats; Quintel gets paid later and only if contractual and transaction conditions are satisfied.
- **The moat becomes outcome-linked.** Signal → conversation → timing → package → lender decision → funding is stronger ground truth than clicks, saves, or rep enthusiasm.

### Second-order effects

#### Brokerage can weaken the lender-software moat it is meant to create

Once Quintel represents borrowers and routes deals across lenders, lender customers may become less willing to contribute books, outcomes, or private scoring context. The same company would be their software vendor, deal supplier, and allocator of opportunities. That conflict is manageable only if data ownership and routing are structurally separated.

The likely long-run shape is two controlled surfaces:

```text
Quintel Intelligence
  shared public evidence + private customer overlays + scoring / workflow

Quintel Origination
  borrower relationships + permissioned transaction data + routing / brokerage
```

They may share infrastructure, but lender-private data cannot silently become brokerage routing input. The existing owner-tagged evidence model is a useful foundation; contracts and access controls must make the boundary legible. [[quintel-sourcing-intelligence-prd-2026-06-29]]:117-134.

#### Trust failures compound faster than data advantages

If a lender changes terms, fails to report funding, mishandles a borrower, or avoids a fee, Quintel's name absorbs the damage. Stauss's Catflow example—large referred volume followed by decaying reporting—shows that signed paper without event-level evidence is not operational control. [[stauss-paulos-2026-08-17]]:79-85.

The minimum trust product is an auditable event ledger:

```text
borrower permission
  → lender introduction
  → documents delivered
  → proposal / term sheet
  → submission
  → approval / decline + reason
  → funding
  → fee due / paid
```

Onset's executed agreement is not a clean template for this future. Each Platform transaction still requires written fee paper; Onset retains broad objection rights; funding notice is only a commercially-reasonable-efforts obligation; and borrower documents submitted for credit may not be altered, summarized, or recharacterized. [[onset-executed-master-broker-referral-agreement-2026-08-05]]:67-73, 145-167.

#### The business may become more defensible while receiving a lower software valuation

A brokerage with proprietary data, repeat borrower relationships, and superior contribution margins can be a strong company. It will still be judged on transaction volume, take rate, gross margin, cycle time, concentration, compliance, and repeat behavior—not on nominal SaaS ARR. Calling transaction revenue “software-enabled” does not change the underlying volatility.

The correct financial claim is therefore not “AI gives brokerage SaaS margins.” It is a hypothesis to measure:

```text
expected contribution per contacted company
= P(reach)
× P(need | reach)
× P(accepted | need)
× P(funded | accepted)
× expected fee
− fully loaded data, outreach, packaging, compliance, and servicing cost
```

The data flywheel should improve the probabilities and reduce the costs over successive cohorts. If cohort economics do not improve, the moat is not compounding in commercially meaningful form.

#### Automation increases regulatory exposure before it proves differentiation

Travo uses phone and email outreach to collect information, but Quintel would use outreach to offer or arrange financing. The contexts are not interchangeable. California, for example, licenses commercial-loan brokering under the California Financing Law, with fact-specific exceptions and restrictions around referrals and compensation. [California DFPI overview](https://dfpi.ca.gov/regulated-industries/california-financing-law/) · [DFPI licensing FAQ](https://dfpi.ca.gov/regulated-industries/california-financing-law/california-finance-lenders-license-frequently-asked-questions/).

Automated voice should not be copied from Travo into Quintel's first motion. The FCC treats AI-generated outbound voices as artificial or prerecorded voices under the TCPA and requires prior express consent in covered consumer calls; exact applicability depends on number type, purpose, and other facts. [FCC declaratory ruling](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/FCC-24-17A1.pdf). Human-led calls are therefore not merely good discovery practice; they avoid introducing a high-risk automation layer before the script and economics work. Counsel should determine the actual requirements rather than this memo drawing legal conclusions.

## Opportunities for Quintel

### 1. Build the equipment-finance timing graph no lender can assemble alone

The most defensible near-term asset is a company-level history of equipment-finance timing and behavior: maturity dates, planned replacements, financing channel, incumbent lender, asset class, budget, vendor involvement, and response to known public signals. Every record is tied to its source and date. Lenders see only their own book; Quintel can observe the market with borrower permission and preserve private overlays.

### 2. Become the origination layer for segments too awkward for point-of-sale finance

The full-brokerage lane should concentrate where human structuring and lender matching are valuable: complex, multi-asset, non-captive, brokered, or mid-market situations. Standardized small-ticket vendor finance rewards speed and low work content; it may remain a qualified-referral lane. [[research-quickfi-2026-08-20]].

### 3. Use brokerage outcomes to make the software sellable later

A lender-facing platform backed by a demonstrated book of signal → need → accepted → funded outcomes is a different product from today's ranked list. The proof can support three downstream offers:

- lender-specific intelligence and call prioritization;
- qualified opportunity feeds with an outcome ledger;
- the internal operating system licensed to lenders or placement firms that want to run the same process themselves.

This is the PARES sequence applied deliberately rather than accidentally: do the work, instrument it, learn which portion is repeatable, then expose the repeatable portion as software.

### 4. Build a borrower relationship without building borrower software

The capital calendar gives Quintel a reason to return before maturity or capex. A borrower does not need a dashboard; it needs useful timing, a market read, and a trusted person when a transaction becomes live. This keeps software density on the operator side while preserving relationship density on the borrower side.

| Risk | Calibration | Cheapest disconfirming test / control |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Signals do not outperform box-matched cold outreach | **Unknown; load-bearing** | Run a matched control cohort in the current sprint |
| Borrowers will not share useful timing data | **Inferred, medium** from the call-capture thesis; not tested | Measure reusable timing records and recontact permission across first 50 reached calls |
| A proprietary dataset automatically creates brokerage advantage | **Inferred, low-medium** | Join timing/signal fields to accepted and funded outcomes; require cohort lift |
| Full packaging pays for itself | **Segment-dependent; contradicted across VFI vs. Envision** | One Level-2 concierge package in a VFI-class box; compare fee/funding lift with Level 1 |
| Lenders will report outcomes and pay reliably | **Low confidence**; Onset contract/reporting gaps and Catflow history are warnings | Independent event reconciliation, per-deal fee paper, audit/reporting controls before scale |
| Brokerage and software can share one brand/data plane safely | **Inferred, low** | Draft access/data-routing rules and test them with two lender partners before pooling anything |
| AI meaningfully improves margins | **Unproven** in Travo, PARES, and Quintel | Track human minutes and exception rate per stage across cohorts |
| Nationwide brokering is legally simple | **False as a planning assumption** | Jurisdiction-by-jurisdiction counsel map keyed to raw referral vs. worked application vs. full broker role |

## Recommended Next Steps

### Through Sep. 26: run phase one as a dual data-and-deal experiment

1. **Keep the current sprint's company-level bar:** 150+ contacted, at least three confirmed needs, and at least one accepted submission. Do not let the brokerage vision soften the kill criterion. [[quintel-focus-realignment-2026-08-15]]:82-86.
2. **Add a matched control cohort.** Reserve roughly 20% of dials for box-matched companies without a qualifying Quintel signal. Keep scripts and caller mix constant.
3. **Add four data-flywheel measures to the ledger:** reusable dated timing record; financing-channel/incumbent correction; explicit recontact/market-read permission; and whether the fact was public, borrower-stated, or inferred.
4. **Track work minutes.** Separate research, dialing, qualification, document collection, packaging, lender follow-up, and exception handling. “AI-native margin” cannot be evaluated without labor by stage.
5. **Keep outreach human-led.** Automate research, call preparation, capture, follow-up drafts, and routing—not the live borrower voice—until compliance and trust requirements are mapped.

### At the first confirmed need: test the progressive ownership ladder

6. **Route by work-content economics, not ideology.** Send an Envision-class opportunity at Level 1 unless extra qualification is proven to lift funding. Run one VFI-class opportunity at Level 2: borrower permission, required financial package, capital ask/use of funds, and live introduction.
7. **Produce one cited handoff artifact.** Show the public trigger, borrower statements, fact/inference boundary, timing, permission, requested amount/equipment, lender-box fit, and remaining verification work.
8. **Contract the event ledger before the handoff.** Define proposal, submission, approval, funding, fee, reporting, objection, and audit events. Do not rely on dashboard access or informal status updates.

### Before full brokerage: establish the operating boundary

9. **Choose one cell:** ticket band × equipment type × borrower profile × lender bench × ownership level. The current evidence favors a complex/mid-market concierge test and a lighter small-ticket referral lane, not a universal full-service process.
10. **Complete a jurisdiction and solicitation map with counsel.** Include entity licensing, individual licensing, fee disclosures, lender-type restrictions, document handling, automated outreach, and borrower consent.
11. **Define the data compact.** Specify what the borrower owns, what Quintel can retain, what can be used for routing and model improvement, what may be de-identified, and what lender-private data can never enter the brokerage surface.
12. **Require two credible capital destinations in the chosen cell.** A borrower-side promise is not real if Quintel has only one lender and cannot compare or reroute.

### Decision rule

**Advance to a bounded full-brokerage pilot if:**

- the signal cohort outperforms the matched control;
- reached calls produce reusable, permissioned timing data at a rate worth maintaining;
- the existing three-needs/one-submission bar is met;
- one Level-2 package shows that additional ownership improves expected economics;
- legal, data, and lender-event controls are in place.

**Do not advance merely because:** founders enjoy the calls, lenders accept free-option deal flow, the timing dataset looks strategically valuable, or PARES has adopted the label. Each is encouraging; none proves a compounding brokerage.

## Open Questions and Unknowns

- What share of reached borrowers will provide an exact maturity or capex date and permission to recontact? **Unknown; first 50 reached calls should establish the baseline.**
- Does a Quintel signal improve confirmed-need rate versus a box-matched control? **Unknown and currently unmeasured.**
- Which first cell has enough fee pool to support human qualification and packaging? **Inferred: mid-market/complex is strongest; requires actual cycle and close-rate data.**
- Will two or more lenders accept borrower-side comparison and routing without restrictive exclusivity? **Unknown.**
- Can Quintel preserve lender trust while also representing borrowers? **Unknown; structural separation and explicit data rights are prerequisites.**
- Why did PARES move from software to services, and what are its current margins, transaction volume, staffing ratio, licensing footprint, and customer mix? **Not publicly established in the sources reviewed. A founder interview would be the highest-value next external research step.**
- What are Travo's actual paid-customer count, pricing, retention, data-acquisition cost, human review rate, and correction loop? **Not public. Its scale and deal claims should remain company-reported evidence.**
