## What Was Explored

Simon's struggle to articulate Quintel positioning for an Alek conversation + sales calls: Alek's "selling signals / surface deals before anyone" vs. Simon's "intelligence + decision-support engine." ## What Emerged

- **The fight was never about the product — it was one overloaded word.** "Signals" was doing three jobs: the **hook** (what opens a call), the **position** (what the product is), and the **price basis** (what the invoice is denominated in). Alek was right on hook (sourcing makes them lean in — Mike/Catherine/Scott all lit up). Simon was right on position (engine-on-their-data, not a feed) and price (access, not per-signal). Split the three and the either/or dissolves: lead with sourcing, position as an engine on their data, price for access. Simon had *already solved this on the homepage* ("sourcing is the hook inside How-it-works") and hadn't noticed it ends the Alek argument too.

- **"Runs on their data" is the entire position, and it's one fact wearing three hats.** Three fuels fused: public record (fuzzy, cheap — the "signal") + their book (hard, only they have) + closed-deal graph (only we have). The signal is the cheapest fuel; we sell the fusion. The Tesla/Austin example proves it's *bidirectional* (a customer's hard data point retroactively re-weights public signals the engine discarded as noise) — which makes positioning (not-a-commodity), moat (deal-graph network effect), and the sourcing mechanism (we surface more/better than a pure-public competitor) literally the same fact.

- **"Never say 'intelligence' — say the formula."** The abstract noun is exactly what made Alek say "I don't even know what that means." The fix for the call is the same as the homepage's make-or-break rule: replace the word with the concrete formula + a verb ("what closes for you, run against the whole market"). "Intelligence" is for the category/page, never the spoken pitch.

- **Negative-signal pressure-test (it cracked in useful ways).** Confident negatives = *deterministic disqualifiers only*; probabilistic "low-probability" suppression is **more dangerous than a positive** because a false negative kills a real $1–100M deal *silently* (nobody calls a suppressed lead → no feedback → the cheap-miss asymmetry inverts). It's not a standalone product (it's the tail of ranking their own pipeline). And it needs *more trust to act on* than a positive (loss aversion → a 30-yr broker calls anyway), so it's a phase-2 trust-gated expansion, not a land pitch. What survives strongest: it's the cleanest *proof we're not a list vendor* ("a list vendor adds names; we take them away").

- **Pricing reveals positioning.** Per-signal pricing tells the buyer the signal IS the product and it's scarce → re-triggers every objection. Peace treaty with Alek's tiering instinct: keep the optionality (which-package-not-whether), tier on buy-boxes/seats/hands, kill the per-signal-count axis.

- **The one real crack: day-one cold-start in the *sale*.** Before they share data, we only have public data — we *are* the commodity feed we're avoiding. The escape = the live-drop closer ("run it on one of your own deals"), which is also the unfakeable demo and the exact moment the position becomes true. So the closer and the positioning are gated on the same act.

- **The deepest cut (third pass): "surface deals from public data" hides two capabilities with opposite risk classes — and the pitch was anchored on the wrong one.** *Capability 1* (rank/resurface their own flow + enrich known borrowers; built on their hard data, improved by market data) is a **dial** — risk = *how much* lift, floor positive, definitely buildable. *Capability 2* (cold-surface net-new borrowers from public data alone) is a **switch** — risk = *whether the signal exists at all*, outside our control, backtest found ~4 deals in years (real but sparse). Simon's concern confirmed and *sharpened*: it's not "the inference is hard," it's "you can't infer a signal that isn't in the public record." Rule: don't bet the company on a switch you don't control; bet on a dial whose floor is positive. Lead with sourcing as the hook, but deliver + measure the pilot on Capability 1; cold sourcing is upside, proven by the backtest, never the pilot's success metric. **Disarming point for Alek:** he already conceded this in writing (Telegram v1: "the external 'find more' part especially [is unproven]") — the verbal pitch over-claims exactly the part his own written v1 set aside. The customer's data also makes sourcing *more buildable* (narrows the search + corroborates weak candidates), so even sourcing is strongest resting on hard data — same conclusion from the engineering side.
- **Sourcing can't be live-demoed** — a closed deal is *known*; cold sourcing must never be shown live (highest whiff-risk; a live miss proves the opposite). Sourcing is proven retrospectively (backtest) + forward (pilot). The only live-safe face = the **enrichment beat** in Demo B (market data on a hard-data anchor). Also added a live-demo risk-ordering: lead with zero-setup beats (extraction-with-provenance, enrichment); hold the lender-match punchline until the panel is loaded and it's surfaced the real funder in rehearsal.
