## What Emerged

- **Private/shared boundary, first principles: share the evidence, privatize the score.** The prior design shared the score (account-agnostic baseline) and privatized the gate — inside-out. The score is *inherently* per-principal ("attention" is always someone's), so there is no shared score; only the *evidence* is shared (public) + private (per-principal), and the score is a per-principal read over (public ∪ own-private), owner-tagged. **Bidirectionality is emergent** (private evidence corroborating a public signal = F1 corroboration over the visible set — build nothing extra). **Leak-safety is structural** (the scoring query's input set is *defined by* `owner ∈ {public,P}`). Two learning loops: private personalization (lock-in, no leak) + shared calibration (network effect, anonymized, scale-gated). **Moat relocation:** the moat is the learned function + public-research graph/cascade IP, NOT pooled private data (which never crosses — which is what makes customers willing to give it).

- **This is the multiplayer private/shared-boundary thesis, made concrete + paying.** The deal-engine is the first real tenancy instantiation of the abstract substrate boundary (2026-06-12: "each side reads from shared ∪ own-private, nothing crosses between private brains"). Not a Quintel detour — it's the substrate getting its first tenancy model.

- **Alek round 1 (deal history can't predict cold):** he conflated deal *files* (financials/collateral — post-submission, underwriting, cold-useless — he's right) with deal *history/metadata* (sector/ticket/channel/outcome — cold-observable, the sourcing dial). Same deal data → two models (underwriting vs. sourcing-ranker). Box = stated preference (filter, flat in/out); history = revealed preference (ranker, real weights) over the *same* cold-observable attributes. **Channels are the biggest non-box use** (history reveals which OEMs/partners/lenders feed them → monitor those = private edges). Don't predict the cold deal, *aim* the search.

- **Alek round 2 (only box matters / he's thinking positive-signal-only):** the negative signal isn't separate — it's the *bottom of the same revealed-preference ranker*. He sees the top of the list, ignores the bottom (half of hit rate). Hit rate = numerator (find better, hard cold) + denominator (stop wasting attempts, easy cold). Revealed negatives work on the cold-observable attributes he concedes you have, and they're his own mirror (low trust bar).

- **The Mike/Apollo crystallization (the gold).** Mike entered his box into Apollo → 3,500 leads → calling in order. **He is not the credit team** — deal history is the *encoded, transferable form of credit's tacit judgment*, put at the top of his funnel instead of the bottom. An information gap *inside his own firm*; closing it is the value (nothing to do with cold prediction — his 3,500 are his list). **Ranking beats cutting** because the rep calls down the list (first 100 dials: random → best-100), and ranking is the *safe* negative (down-rank, never silent-delete). → the cleanest v1 + demo: **"give us your list + deal history, we rank it"** — no market data, no cold sourcing, demonstrable on their own data, dodges every objection. Deal history alone is sellable; market data is the *crank* (timing + extension).

- **PRD direction (Simon's framing).** Build customer-data-first (dial baseline), market data as a layer (inverts external-data-first prior). Incorporate GTM: the $10k setup = a per-customer *deal-data ingestion engine* (ingest/clean/structure their deal history for Quintel) — which justifies the price, earns the "built-for-you/AI" claim (vs. a glorified newsletter), and builds the moat. EF reality: deal history is mixed into CRM data (lost-deal reasons live there); cleanest baseline unit = the **funded credit memo** (a clean "package," similar across customers). Design from the customer's POV (Mike's interaction: feed list → rank → enrich → import new → track deals to leads); signals are no longer "the main thing" of sourcing.
