## Key angles that emerged

1. **Sector packaging contradicts the buy-box-primary product thesis** — the product says "your box filters the whole market," the price said "buy shelves by sector." Buyers arbitrage the menu (Vickie), and Alek's honest answer ("you get all the leads anyway") collapses the tier. Two lenders rejected sector tiering in 9 days.
2. **Price the desk, not the market**: two flat bands keyed to *average ticket* (stated, contractual representation) — Core ≤~$500K / Institutional above. Buy box stays 100% customer-controlled; price and targeting deliberately decoupled (price keyed to the configured box = paying customers to poison the ranking input).
3. **The Onset insight: rev-share is the native purchase format for broker-culture large-ticket shops** — the budget line (broker comp, 1–2% of COP, wired 5 days post-funding) already exists. Success fee off cost of property, no base; commitment substitutes for the base (12-mo term + dedicated pod — Jerman's own design, worth more than any monthly minimum).
4. **Verification ≠ sourcing on UCC data.** Bulk all-state coverage is a sourcing need; attribution only needs point lookups by debtor name on a bounded lead register — free state SOS portals suffice. Four-layer bridge: register → reporting covenant → spot checks → audit clause, with a written upgrade path to self-executing.
5. **Fee-eligibility = *activity test*, not presence test** (revised in-session off Simon's Stauss datapoint: lenders carry 50K–200K+ dormant records — mere "not in your CRM" would be a unilateral kill switch and invites bulk-import gaming). Rule: fee-eligible unless documented activity in the prior 12 months; 10-business-day challenge window from register delivery with evidence, else eligibility locks. Disputes move to surface time ($0 stakes), not funding time. Dormant accounts Quintel reactivates ARE fee-eligible — reverses the earlier "concede dormant" position.
