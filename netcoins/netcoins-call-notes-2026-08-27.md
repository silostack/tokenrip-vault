## Bottom line

The headline tripled — CAD$200K to CAD$700K — and the paper genuinely improved in six or seven places
that matter. It is a better document than v5 and Fraser negotiated in good faith throughout.

The trade that produced it ran in one direction: **equity up, cash down.** The consideration is
BIGG-gated currency it costs Fraser nothing to promise. The monthly fee — which the battle plan
itself calls the thing the deal is actually worth (*"the deal is worth the monthly fee times the

engagement abandoned entirely. Every one of those concessions was volunteered, none was traded, and

below market; the equity consideration carries the balance"*) was never invoked and does not appear
in v8.

Guaranteed value on the table today: CAD$100K in restricted shares at closing, plus three months of
fees at US$4,000–7,500 per founder. Roughly CAD$110–130K per founder committed. The other CAD$600K
is milestone-, revenue- or BIGG-gated.

**Recommendation: sign by 3 September, with one redline as a condition of signing and two asks made
in the same email.** The economics are non-binding and get renegotiated in the Definitive Agreement.
What binds on signature is exclusivity, confidentiality, disclosure control, expenses and the break
fee — and the break fee, as drafted, cannot be invoked by RebelFi at all.

### The IP trap — read this one twice

Three clauses chain together:

1. **§6:** background IP *incorporated in* the RebelFi product is *"assigned or, at the Purchaser's
   election, licensed."* BIGG chooses.
2. Once assigned, that code becomes intellectual property **of the Business.**
3. **Schedule A §7:** *"Neither founder will use, in Tokenrip, Quintel or any other venture, any
   intellectual property, source code or confidential information of the Business."*

Net effect: any pre-existing library, tooling or agent scaffolding of Simon's that touched RebelFi
can be taken outright, and Simon would then be contractually barred from using his own prior work in
Tokenrip. The 24 August counter left this open — it addressed only background IP *not* incorporated,
and BIGG filled the gap.

**The fix is one clause:** incorporated background IP is *licensed to the Purchaser on a perpetual,
irrevocable, royalty-free, **non-exclusive** basis* — delete the election. BIGG loses nothing it
needs; it still gets permanent free rights to run the product. Non-exclusive is the word that matters.
