3. Canaccord raises against a story, and a story needs an asset. **The acquisition therefore has to precede or accompany the raise.**
4. If the reset is already scheduled — filings drafted, banker engaged, board decided — then the acquisition occupies a **slot**. A slot is a deadline. A deadline held by one side is the only durable leverage the smaller party ever gets.

| Requires a **special shareholder meeting** | Circular, notice period, mailing, cost — realistically 2–3 months |

## 2b. The Licence Stack — and Why the Marquee Licence Is the Wrong One

**Headline finding: the licence everyone is proud of is the least relevant to what the NewCo would build.** CIRO is a *securities* regime. Payments are not securities. Nothing in a cross-border settlement, virtual-account or agentic-payments product requires or benefits from CIRO membership.

### What Surge actually holds

| Instrument | What it really is | What it buys |
|---|---|---|
| **FINTRAC MSB registration** | **Not a licence.** FINTRAC states plainly it does not issue licences or approve businesses. National, no fee, no capital minimum, no discretionary approval | Covers **FX dealing, money transferring/remittance, and dealing in virtual currency**. Genuinely useful: Canada has no US-style 50-state transmitter regime, so MSB + banking ≈ you can move money. Quebec runs a separate MSB licence — **unverified whether Netcoins holds it** |
| **CSA / BCSC exemptive relief (Restricted Dealer)** | Time-limited permission, **extended 2025-09-29 for two years → expires ~Sept 2027** | Lets Netcoins operate a CTP. **Conditional**: positive risk-adjusted capital by 2026-01-31, and a complete CIRO application filed by **2026-06-01** and formally accepted. Miss it and Netcoins must restrict business and **wind down Ontario operations** |
| **CIRO membership** | **Application accepted for review 2026-06-24** (filed May 27). Not membership | They cleared the milestone by roughly three weeks. This is **a clock they are running against, not a moat they are standing on** |
| **US operations** | ~48 states via an agreement with **Zero Hash** (Sept 2024) | **The US licences are Zero Hash's, not Netcoins'.** Rented rail, per-transaction cost, counterparty risk. Verify whether this is still the arrangement |

> **Read for us:** the payments-relevant assets at Netcoins are the **banking relationships, Interac e-Transfer and wire access, USDC mint/burn (Circle), custody (BitGo/Fireblocks), KYC infrastructure and the FINTRAC MSB.** Not CIRO. CIRO is two years of management attention and regulatory capital pointed at the exchange, not at us — and it is a plausible answer to why the NewCo needs to be run by people Fraser doesn't have to supervise.

### What is missing to do "stablecoin payments" properly

| Door | Requires | Surge status |
|---|---|---|
| Buy/sell crypto for Canadians | CSA relief → CIRO membership | Relief to ~Sept 2027; CIRO in progress |
| Move fiat / remittance / FX in Canada | FINTRAC MSB (+ Quebec licence) | MSB yes; Quebec unverified |
| **Operate a payments business** (hold end-user funds, execute EFTs) | **RPAA registration with the Bank of Canada** | **Unverified — the single best licence question to ask** |
| **Real-time CAD settlement without a sponsor bank** | Payments Canada membership + **Real-Time Rail** participation | No public evidence of an application. **Window opens 2026-08-24** |
| Virtual accounts / named account identifiers | Sponsor bank or trust company — **Canada has no EMI regime** | Bank relationships yes; issuance no |
| US money movement | State MTLs, or a partner | **Rented from Zero Hash** |
| Issue a stablecoin | Bank of Canada registration under the Stablecoin Act | **Not available to anyone yet — 2027** |
| EU | MiCA CASP + EMI | None |

**On the Bridge question specifically.** Bridge issues virtual IBANs in end users' names on the strength of a **Luxembourg EMI licence plus MiCA CASP authorisation** (both announced July 2026). The EMI is the piece that permits it. **Canada has no EMI equivalent.** RPAA registration is a *supervisory* regime for operational risk and funds safeguarding — it is not authority to hold client money as e-money or issue account identifiers. So **Surge cannot do what Bridge does in the EU, and neither can any other Canadian entity today.** The two things that change that are direct RTR participation (removes the bank from the settlement leg) and the Stablecoin Act framework once in force.

**On RPAA scope.** Since 2025-09-08 a PSP must be registered *before* performing retail payment activities. Netcoins' trading flow is probably covered by the securities exclusion — but Bank of Canada guidance is explicit that the exclusion reaches only payments made *for the purpose of giving effect to a securities transaction*, and that being securities-regulated does not exclude an entity's other activities. **A cross-border B2B payments business is not a securities transaction.** Whether registration sits at Netcoins, at the parent, or has to be obtained fresh by the NewCo is a real gating item.

> ⚠️ **The Bank of Canada's PSP registry and applicant list are JavaScript-rendered and returned empty to automated retrieval. "Not found" here is not evidence of "not registered." Treat as unverified, and ask.**

## 2c. Canadian Regulatory State of Play — Everything Below Is New Since Mid-2025

**Canada passed a federal stablecoin law and it is not yet in force.** That gap is the opportunity.

| Development | Date | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Stablecoin Act** enacted inside Bill C-15 (Budget Implementation Act 2025 No. 1) | **Royal Assent 2026-03-26** | First federal framework for fiat-backed stablecoins. Expands the **Bank of Canada's** mandate to supervise issuers |
| **Not in force** | Provisions commence by Governor-in-Council; regs expected 12–18 months → **2027** | **The window.** The rules are known; the obligations have not landed |
| Requirements on issuers | — | BoC registration · 1:1 HQLA reserves · redemption at par · qualified-custodian segregation with bankruptcy protection · monthly certified attestations · recovery & resolution plan |
| **Prohibition on yield** | — | Issuers may not pay interest **"directly or indirectly"** — stricter than the US |
| Extraterritorial reach | — | Applies to **foreign** issuers distributing fiat-backed stablecoins to Canadians, whatever the denomination |
| Consequential **RPAA** amendment | — | Pulls "payment service providers using prescribed stablecoins" into Bank of Canada supervision |
| Federal **Policy Statement** | **2026-02-09** | CSA **retains** jurisdiction over non-fiat-backed stablecoins and over *trading* of stablecoins by marketplaces and Dealer CTPs. Split regime, not a federal takeover |
| **Real-Time Rail**: by-law and rules in force | **2026-08-24** — four weeks away | System launch **Q4 2026** |
| **PSPs eligible for Payments Canada membership and direct RTR participation** | New | Path: RPAA registration → PC membership → RTR application → readiness. Requires ISO 20022, fraud controls, liquidity management. Not all PSPs will qualify |
| **QCAD** — Canada's prospectus-receipted CAD stablecoin | Final prospectus **2025-11-20** | Since: Deloitte partnership · **VersaBank** as first stablecoin customer · **TD Bank as reserve custodian (2026-07-15)** · live CAD/USD settlement on **Circle's StableFX** · deployed to Arc |
| **US GENIUS Act** | Enacted Jul 2025, effective **2027**. Treasury AML rule proposed 2026-04-08; OCC rules proposed 2026-03-02 | Bars the *issuer* from paying interest but **not exchanges from sharing reserve yield** — Coinbase pays 4%+ on USDC. Canada closed that door; the US left it open |

### Three consequences that change what we say on the call

**1. The right build is distribution over someone else's compliant coin — and the CAD leg already exists.**
QCAD is prospectus-receipted, TD-custodied, VersaBank-banked and already settling CAD/USD on Circle's StableFX. **A Surge NewCo doing cross-border does not need to build or issue anything on the CAD side.** It needs to be the licensed distribution and application layer on top of QCAD and USDC. That is *precisely* the "Surge brings the rails, NewCo brings the rest" split — and it is a named, checkable partner rather than a hypothesis.

**2. The Real-Time Rail is the best strategic idea available to bring to this call.**
PSP eligibility for direct RTR participation is brand new, the by-law lands **August 24** and the system launches **Q4 2026** — the exact quarter this NewCo would form. Direct participation gives real-time CAD settlement, 24/7/365, without a sponsor bank: the missing fiat leg of any stablecoin cross-border product. It is simultaneously **a durable capability and an announceable event**, which is the rarest combination on Fraser's list. Ask whether Payments Canada membership is on anyone's roadmap. If the answer is no, that is a gap we can own.

> **Live risk worth raising as a question, not an accusation:** if Netcoins pays any reward or staking yield on stablecoin balances today, the indirect-interest prohibition may reach that product line in 2027. Asking demonstrates depth and is genuinely useful to him.

### The diagnostic questions — trigger, alternatives, rejection

Standard JTBD interviewing gets at the job through the *triggering event* and the *alternatives rejected*, not by asking what someone wants. Translated for this call:

1. **"You started looking in March. What happened that made you start looking then?"** — the trigger names the job.
2. **"You've had a number of these conversations and haven't closed one. What was missing in the ones you passed on?"** — **the sharpest question on the list.** Rejection criteria reveal the real hiring bar far better than asking what he's looking for. It also directly tests whether a clean cap table is the scarce thing.
3. **"You already have a dev team, a piece of tech, and a customer. What can't you do with those that an acquisition solves?"** — the honest answer *is* the job. If it comes back as "nothing, but I need a transaction," that is fine and useful — and it should change the terms.
4. **"What happens to the plan if you don't acquire anyone this year?"** — reveals whether the job is essential or nice-to-have, and surfaces the real competing alternative (build in-house).
5. **"If this works perfectly, what's the headline twelve months out?"** — whether the headline is about revenue or about the story tells you which job he hired for.

**Listen for which job he describes, then price and structure to that job.** Do not assume it is the one we would prefer.

**The opposite posture has the mirror failure, and financially it is worse.** Going hard when compensation was set on an assumption of part-time minimal effort hands a public company enormous surplus for free: full-time output at contractor rates, plus paper, at a company whose budget sits inside an unclosed financing.

> *"Sounds like you bring the money movement and we bring the product and the customers on top. Is that the split? And do you want us to come back with the product thesis, or do you have one you want executed?"*

Either answer is fine. Not knowing which is not.
