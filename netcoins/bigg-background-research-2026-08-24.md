# BIGG Digital Assets and Fraser Matthews — What the Public Record Shows

## Bottom line

There is no fraud, no securities sanction, no enforcement action, and nothing in Fraser Matthews' personal record that should stop this deal. The "sneaky, slimy" pattern the broker warned about does not appear in the file.

What the record shows instead is more specific and more useful: **BIGG is a serial acquirer whose shares have lost roughly 97% of their value since its last major financing, whose most recent stock-funded acquisition was written down to zero within fifteen months, and whose main operating business runs on time-limited regulatory relief it has already failed one deadline against.** The exposure is not to bad faith. It is to the currency we are being paid in, and to a clock we do not control.

Three findings change what we should do. They are in §7.

## 1. Fraser Matthews is credentialed, new, and not the problem

**Career.** Bachelor of Arts, Political Science, Western University (2002–2006). Oxford FinTech Programme, Saïd Business School (2018). Management consulting at PwC (Financial Services Advisory), Capco (Technology Advisory), and EY Dubai (Senior Manager, emerging digital financial services). Then Managing Director of TribalScale's Middle East business, then Digital Strategy Lead at Banque Saudi Fransi. Joined Netcoins as President on 7 June 2022. Appointed to BIGG's board February 2024. Appointed CEO of BIGG on 24 February 2026, while remaining CEO of Netcoins.

**What that means.** He is a strategy consultant and digital-transformation operator, not a founder, not a dealmaker, and not someone with a public-markets track record of his own. Nothing negative surfaced: no litigation, no regulatory history, no prior company failures attached to his name.

**What to take from it:**

- He has been CEO of the parent for **six months**. He is BIGG's **third CEO in under three years** (Mark Binns out October 2023; Dan Reitzik interim for 28 months; Matthews from February 2026).
- He owns little of the company. Total individual insider ownership across all directors and officers is **5.11%** — 18.2M of 355.8M shares. Lance Morginn (co-founder, chair) holds the largest personal block at 3.26%.
- His operating record at Netcoins is real: revenue C$4.9M (2022) → C$11.5M (2024), trading volume past C$1B in 2025. Growth is genuine but has stalled (§6).
- Minor sloppiness worth noting only because it is a tell about their press discipline: BIGG's 2024 board-appointment release credited him with "17 years" in financial services; the 2026 CEO release says "15 years." The 2026 release also drops Banque Saudi Fransi and EY from his history entirely. Not deceptive, just careless — which is how the LOI drafting reads too.

**Assessment:** Fraser is a professional counterparty operating inside constraints set by other people. The people who actually price this deal are the CFO and the board (§4).

## 3. The TerraZero precedent: what happened to the last founders BIGG bought with stock

This is the closest available analogue to our deal, and it is the reason to read the consideration terms carefully.

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| Dec 2021 | BIGG invests C$100K in TerraZero (metaverse/Web3 studio, founded 2021) |
| Feb 2022 | BIGG invests **C$9.8M cash** for 14M shares + 2M warrants → ~33% fully diluted |
| 25 Aug 2023 | BIGG signs definitive agreement to acquire the rest for **~62M BIGG shares, stated value ~C$20M** (~C$0.54/TerraZero share). TerraZero holders to own ~20% of BIGG |
| 28 Sep 2023 | Deal closes |
| 16 Oct 2023 | **Three weeks later**, BIGG's CEO Mark Binns steps down. **Dan Reitzik — founder and CEO of TerraZero, the company BIGG had just bought — becomes interim CEO of BIGG.** Lance Morginn becomes board chair |
| FY2024 | BIGG writes off **the entire C$16.1M of TerraZero goodwill**. Stated reasons: no comparable public companies, no historical revenue to support estimates, platform still in development with "virtually non-existent revenue generating activities." BIGG's FY2024 net loss: **C$25.8M** |
| FY2024–25 | TerraZero revenue: C$0.2M (2024), **C$0.12M (2025, down 42%)**. Segment "undergoing strategic restructuring" |
| Feb 2026 | Reitzik ends 28 months as "interim" CEO. He retains 4.32M BIGG shares (1.21%) |

**What this tells us, in order of usefulness:**

1. **BIGG paid roughly C$30M — C$9.8M cash plus ~C$20M in stock — for a business now generating C$120K a year, and wrote the goodwill to zero inside fifteen months.** Their diligence and valuation process failed completely on the last technology acquisition they made.
2. **The sellers were paid in paper that then fell ~80%.** 62M shares issued at an implied ~C$0.32 are worth ~C$0.07 today. Not because anyone cheated them — because the currency fell.
3. **The seller ended up running the buyer** for over two years, during which the buyer wrote off the goodwill on his own company. Whatever else that is, it is not an arm's-length governance culture.
4. **The pattern is narrative acquisition.** BIGG bought a metaverse company in February 2022, at the exact peak of metaverse enthusiasm. It is now buying a stablecoin company in August 2026, at the peak of stablecoin enthusiasm. That is the same trade. Our July read — that BIGG wants the *announcement* — is now supported by their acquisition history, not just by the asset list in §3 of the LOI.

## 4. Governance is concentrated, thin, and drawn from the junior resource market

Board elected 9 July 2026: **Lance Morginn** (co-founder, chair, president), **Fraser Matthews** (CEO), **Kim Evans** (CFO **and** director), **Robert Birmingham**, **Mark Healy**, **Anthony Zelen** (co-founder, "independent," sits on **both** the audit and compensation committees).

- **The CFO sits on the board.** In a company this size that is common and still a weakness: the officer preparing the numbers helps oversee them.
- **A co-founder sits on the audit committee** while being described as independent.
- **Kim Evans** — the person Fraser said would "talk you through" the structure, and the person the price went to on 23 August — is a Certified General Accountant with **25 years as a director/officer of TSXV-listed companies, formed in the junior mining sector**: founder of Golden Reign, founder of Mako Mining, President & CEO of Marlin Gold, CFO of CoTec Holdings. CFO and director of BIGG since 2017.

**So what.** The LOI's instincts — share consideration rather than cash, milestone tranches, set-off rights against tranches, uncapped specific indemnities — are the standard toolkit of a junior-resource-market financier, not of a tech acquirer. That is not malice. It is the house style of the person pricing us. It also means the counter should be argued in her language: certainty, quantified exposure, closing risk, and what it costs the company in cash.

**Founder attrition.** Of the four founders (Shone Anstey, Lance Morginn, Anthony Zelen, Marty Anstey), Marty Anstey sued BIGG in the BC Supreme Court in November 2019 for constructive dismissal (BIGG called it meritless; no public resolution found). Shone Anstey left and returned as a strategic advisor in December 2023. Only Morginn and Zelen remain on the board.

## 5. Netcoins operates on time-limited regulatory relief, and it has already missed a deadline

This matters because Netcoins' regulated rails are the thing that makes our integration milestone — and our second tranche — achievable.

From the OSC/BCSC decision of **29 September 2025** (2025 BCSECCOM 439) and Netcoins' undertaking of 22 September 2025:

- Netcoins has operated as a **restricted dealer on interim exemptive relief since September 2021**, renewed four times (2021, 2022, 2023, 2025). It is **not** a CIRO dealer member.
- It was required to apply for CIRO membership by 15 May 2024. It did. **On 24 May 2024 CIRO responded with "a number of deficiencies that need to be resolved before the membership application will be formally accepted for review."** The application was never accepted. It took until **27 May 2026** — two years — for a re-filed application to be formally accepted.
- The regulators required a written undertaking with hard milestones. **Missing them triggers mandatory "business restrictions," which the company has disclosed include winding down operations in Ontario.**
- One condition is bluntly financial: *"For the financial year ending on December 31, 2025, the Filer will calculate its risk adjusted capital in accordance with … CIRO's Form 1 by January 31, 2026. In the event the risk adjusted capital … is negative, the Filer will implement business restrictions as required."* Regulators do not write that clause unless capital adequacy is a live question.
- **The relief expires 30 September 2027.** To operate in Ontario and Québec past that date, Netcoins must complete CIRO membership and investment-dealer registration.
- Retail clients outside AB/BC/MB/QC/SK are capped at **C$30,000** net acquisition cost per twelve months.

**Separately, the CRA matter.** In November 2024 the CRA reassessed Netcoins for GST/HST on FY2018 at **C$8.36M** (C$6.07M principal plus C$2.29M interest and penalties), on the theory that Netcoins acted as principal rather than agent in crypto sales. BIGG booked a C$8.46M provision in 2024. After objection, the CRA reduced it in December 2025 to **C$2.21M principal / ~C$3.23M all-in**. Still outstanding, against C$6.2M of cash.

**So what.** BIGG's ability to deliver the environment where our product gets deployed and certified is not fully within its control. Nothing here says the milestone will fail — Netcoins is operating, growing volume, and the CRA number came down. But a milestone tranche that BIGG certifies, in a business running on relief that expires in September 2027, is not a safe place to leave a third of our consideration.

## 6. The core business is growing volume and losing pricing power

FY2025 (reported 28 April 2026): revenue **C$12.86M**, up 3.5%. Net loss **C$1.27M** (versus C$25.8M in 2024, the improvement being almost entirely the absence of the TerraZero write-off).

By segment: Netcoins **C$10.48M**; Blockchain Intelligence Group **C$2.27M** (record, +36%); TerraZero **C$0.12M** (−42%).

- Netcoins gross trading volume grew **29% to C$1.068B**, but **average fee rates compressed to 0.97%**, leaving revenue roughly flat. They are buying volume with price.
- **Trailing-twelve-month revenue is C$10.78M, down 15.9%.** The FY2025 figure was 12.86M. Whatever is happening in 2026 is worse than 2025.
- Cash C$6.2M; cash plus crypto ~C$15.3M; no debt; C$3.2M CRA liability outstanding.
- **The last press release before the 21 August Surge announcement was 5 May 2026** (a Talos partnership). Three and a half months of silence. A stock-tracking piece on 3 August attributed the decline to "a lack of recent positive news."

**So what.** A company with a shrinking top line, a compressing take rate, a three-month news vacuum, a rename, and a 7:1 consolidation needs an announcement. We are the announcement.

## 7. Three things this research changes about our counter

### 7a. The pricing mechanic prices us after our own announcement — and we already accepted it

**Status check, 2026-08-25.** Not addressed in LOI v5, and not raised in our counter. Worse: our 24 August changes list says under Section 5, verbatim, *"Share issuance mechanics as drafted in the letter,"* and opens with *"Everything in the 22 August letter not listed below is accepted as drafted."* This is a concession already given, not an open item.

**What v5 §5 says:**

> "Shares issued as Consideration will be priced at the greater of (i) the volume-weighted average trading price of BIGG's common shares on the TSXV for the ten trading days ending three business days prior to **the relevant issuance date**, less the maximum discount permitted by the Exchange, and (ii) the minimum price permitted under Exchange policy…"

**The problem is the reference date, not the formula.** The dollar amount is fixed; the share count is derived from the price at each issuance. Under §12 BIGG controls the announcement, which lands on signing the Definitive Agreement — weeks before closing. So the closing tranche is priced inside the post-announcement window. BIGG is buying us *because* announcing a stablecoin acquisition should move a C$23M stock that has had no news since 5 May. If it works, we receive fewer shares: we buy in at the pop we created, then hold through the four-month restriction while it fades. The milestone tranche is worse — it is priced up to six months later, and BIGG chooses when to certify, so BIGG effectively chooses the price.

Being fair about it: this is a two-way exposure, not a trap. If the market reads the deal as dilution and the stock falls, the same formula hands us *more* shares. The objection is not that the formula is unfair — it is that it leaves both tranches floating on an event BIGG controls the timing of.

**The Exchange already has a convention, and v5 does not use it.**

- **Policy 5.3, §2.1:** *"Where securities are issued as consideration in a non-cash asset transaction, the deemed value of the transaction will be calculated using the Discounted Market Price of the securities."*
- **Policy 1.1, "Market Price":** *"the last closing price of the Issuer's Listed Shares before either the issuance of the news release or the filing of the Form 4A – Price Reservation Form required to fix the price at which the securities are to be issued or deemed to be issued (the 'Notice of the Transaction')."*

The Exchange's reference point is **the last close before the news release announcing the transaction** — pre-announcement — with a formal mechanism, the **Form 4A Price Reservation**, that exists precisely to lock a price ahead of issuance. BIGG's drafted ten-day VWAP ending three business days before each issuance date is a custom, later reference point of their own construction.

Two related provisions worth knowing:

- **Consolidation/Split Exception (Policy 1.1(a)):** Market Price is adjusted for a concurrent or recent consolidation. With the 7:1 effective ~26 August, the reference will be the post-consolidation price (~C$0.49), not C$0.07. Same value; do not let the bigger number read as a better price.
- **Material Information Exception (Policy 1.1(b)):** if BIGG announces other material information after Notice of the Transaction, Market Price floors at the close the day after that announcement. The Exchange has already thought about post-notice price moves and resolves them partly against the vendor. Expect BIGG's counsel to cite this.

**The ask — better than the "lesser of" version I first drafted.**

> **Fix the number of consideration shares for both tranches at the time of the Definitive Agreement**, priced at the Market Price as defined in Exchange Policy 1.1 — the last closing price before the news release announcing the Transaction, adjusted for the Consolidation — with the resulting share count for each tranche stated in the Definitive Agreement and reserved by Form 4A if the Exchange requires it.

Why this version:

- It is **symmetric.** We give up the upside case (stock falls, we get more shares) along with the downside. That is what makes it winnable — a "lesser of" formulation is a free option and BIGG's counsel will say so.
- It uses **their own Exchange's convention**, so the argument is "align §5 with Policy 1.1," not "improve our terms."
- It **removes the milestone-timing lever entirely.** A fixed share count cannot be re-priced by choosing when to certify. That is the larger of the two wins.
- It is a **drafting-alignment re-entry**, which is how to reopen something already conceded without looking like a re-trade.

**Honest caveats.** Whether the Exchange will accept a share count fixed six months forward for the milestone tranche is the open question, and it is one for counsel and for BIGG's own Exchange filing — not something to assert on a call. If the Exchange requires the second tranche to be priced at its own issuance, the fallback is to keep the dollar amount but tie the reference for both tranches to the pre-announcement Market Price.

### 7b. Correct the escrow flag — it is probably four months, not thirty-six

I earlier flagged TSXV Policy 5.4's 36-month Tier 2 escrow as a risk. On the numbers that almost certainly does not apply. Policy 5.4 escrow attaches to New Listing transactions and to "Principals." At C$200K–C$700K against a C$23M market cap, this is a 1–3% deal — nowhere near a Reverse Takeover or Change of Business — and neither founder approaches the 10% ownership threshold. **The real restriction is the statutory four-month hold under NI 45-102, which the LOI already states.** The one open question for counsel is whether becoming an officer of the acquired entity could make either of us a "Principal" via a material-operating-subsidiary argument. Worth one line in the counsel email; not worth negotiating over.

### 7c. Ask whether a financing is coming, and whether we are in it

A rename plus a 7:1 consolidation with no financing announced is, on the TSXV, most often the setup for a raise: you consolidate to get the nominal price up so you can issue at a respectable number. Fraser has already told us the payments business "would be a standalone company… wholly owned by our parent company. And we would raise funds to scale it up."

This cuts both ways and both matter to us:
- **Against us:** a financing priced at a discount right after we take shares dilutes us immediately, before our hold expires.
- **For us:** it is the funding source for our consulting fee, and it is the trigger in Schedule A's fee-review clause.

The Kim Evans question list already asks what changes if the financing does not close. Add: *is a financing contemplated in the six months following closing, and at what indicative size?* Their answer tells us how firm the monthly fee actually is.

## 8. What this does not change

- **The deal is still worth doing.** Nothing here is disqualifying. BIGG is a real, audited, TSXV-listed operating company with C$12.9M of revenue, C$15.3M of cash and crypto, no debt, and a genuinely growing regulated brokerage. It is not a shell.
- **The strategy stands.** Everything found here reinforces the July thesis: *the leverage is not on the price of RebelFi, it is on the terms of engagement.* The paper has lost 97% since 2021 and 71% since 2024. Arguing for more of it is arguing for more of a depreciating asset. The cash fee is the deal.
- **Their clock is real and it is ours to use.** The consolidation is effective 26 August, the LOI expires 28 August, and they have had no news since 5 May. Pushback is cheapest for us right now.

## 9. Open questions worth answering before signing

1. Is a financing contemplated post-closing, at what size, and at what price? (Ask Kim Evans.)
2. ~~Which entity is the purchaser?~~ **Resolved in v5 — correcting my own flag.** §1 states: *"For clarity, the Purchaser will not be Netcoins Inc."* The consideration is BIGG common shares (§5), so the CRA liability and the regulatory undertaking sitting inside Netcoins are not in the payment path. What survives is a minor drafting point: §1 says who the Purchaser will *not* be, and otherwise "BIGG, or a wholly-owned subsidiary designated by BIGG." Ask them to name it in the Definitive Agreement. Not worth call time.
3. What is BIGG's Q2 2026 quarter? TTM revenue is down 15.9% and the FY numbers do not explain where. The interim statements are on SEDAR+.
4. Board approval: is the acquisition approved, or is Fraser negotiating ahead of a board that has not seen it? (Already in the Kim Evans list.)
5. Was the Marty Anstey constructive-dismissal claim resolved, and how? Low priority; a BC court registry search would answer it if counsel is already engaged.
