## Key Intelligence / What Changed

### 1. Kim Dwyer named the structural reason the NewCo exists — and it is better than the recap narrative

> *"We have to keep Netcoins, the regulated entity, as lean as possible. And then if we want to build other pieces, it kind of has to be outside of that entity."*
>
> *"You can't really do anything that is too new or novel, or the regulators' heads explode. And there's a lot of pressure to be profitable at all times, so you can't load a whole bunch of costs into the business."*

The prep doc's read was that the NewCo exists mainly to feed the recap story. **That was incomplete.** Netcoins *structurally cannot host new product* — regulatory tolerance and profitability pressure both forbid it. That makes the NewCo genuinely load-bearing rather than decorative, which is a materially better answer to the JTBD question than "make the story legible."

**It cuts the other way too:** the same profitability pressure sits over the group. "You can't load a whole bunch of costs" is a sentence about budget discipline, said by the COO, unprompted.

### 2. Eight months to approve basic staking — against a 90–120 day plan

> **Kim:** *"When we deployed staking — the most basic form of staking — I think it took eight months to get approval. Just through BitGo. It was nothing crazy."*

Set against Fraser's *"we think we could do that in the first, I don't know, 120 days. Maybe 90."* And Kim's own description of the architecture: *"this service is consuming services from Netcoins Inc, the regulated entity."*

**These are not reconcilable as stated.** Anything the NewCo does that changes what the regulated entity does sits on a regulatory clock measured in quarters, not weeks. This is the single most important operating datapoint from the call, and it is a contradiction *inside their own team* — which means raising it is helpful, not adversarial.

### 3. The US capability is weaker and later than Fraser described

> **Fraser:** *"We'd have money transmission licences in all states except for New York."*
>
> **Kim:** *"Outside of those 18 states where we have no-action letters, the MTLs... the setup we're talking about **would have** the banking relationship and the MTLs."*
>
> **Kim:** *"We worked with Zero Hash in the States as well. They used to power the US business. They're very rigid. **Our relationship ended on May 15th.**"*

Present tense from the CEO; conditional from the COO. **Netcoins USA's ~48-state footprint ran on Zero Hash's licences, and that ended 2026-05-15.** Today the US position is 18 no-action-letter states plus an unnamed prospective banking + MTL partner. Confirms and updates the vault's "rented rail" finding — the rail has since been returned.

### 4. The product thesis is now defined, and the target is Bridge

> **Simon:** *"So just to understand — the direction is kind of like a Bridge-type setup where you can offer virtual accounts and then the whole flow?"* — **Kim:** *"Yes, yes."*

Plan of record: MSB Canada + US → cross-border CA↔US + domestic → on/off-ramp, swaps, merchant/B2B/B2C payments via **a second, undescribed technology asset** → treasury yield on idle balances → then LatAm/Europe, liquidity-source agnostic (*"It could be Kraken, it could be Binance. It doesn't matter."*).

**Against the vault's regulatory research, the virtual-account leg is the hard part.** Bridge issues virtual IBANs on a Luxembourg EMI plus MiCA authorisation. **Canada has no EMI equivalent** — in Canada that requires a sponsor bank or trust company, and the Bank of Canada's RPAA regime is supervisory, not an authority to hold client money as e-money or issue account identifiers. The US leg is achievable *if* the MTL partner is real. Nobody on the call flagged this.

### 5. Fraser named the working structure — contractor, hours-based

> *"What I think we'd probably figure out is a bit of a contract agreement, so you can work on both projects. Then we'll figure out — how many hours do you want to do on this, how many hours on that, what the pay would be."*

**Good:** a contractor agreement is far easier to carve IP out of than an employment agreement with a standard invention-assignment clause. The Tokenrip/Quintel forward-IP risk becomes tractable.

**Bad:** "how many hours" is precisely the frame Simon and Alek agreed to reject, and it went unchallenged. Hours-based contracting makes them vendors rather than operators and caps the upside.

### 6. Distribution partly exists, and it has a name

> **Kim:** *"We do have a dedicated OTC team right now... a lot of their clients have these types of requirements as well, and they're very connected in that world. So we could deploy them as a sales team, potentially across both businesses."*
>
> **Kim:** *"We can't do anything cross-border at the moment. So there are definitely some clients we could bring over."*

Answers the prep doc's Q7 and Q11: Netcoins is both channel and customer, and there is an existing OTC team that can be pointed at the NewCo. This is real and it reduces the "does Alek have to find every customer" risk.

### 7. Kim named the competitor and asked the differentiation question — and got a soft answer

> *"The stablecoin sandwich itself isn't that unique... you have people like Kraken with Payward services. They're really pushing that right now, and they already have on/off-ramps and a lot of the key corridors."*
>
> *"Is the yield the differentiator for you guys, or...?"*

Alek answered "yield plus customer understanding." **Simon's answer was the strong one and it came late:** corridor coverage (*"if you're sending money to Pakistan, virtually there are no options"*) and **minimums** (*"Bridge... you need a minimum of $2 million a month, and that's at the highest rate"* — against RebelFi's aggregated-volume model with lower monthly minimums, plus yield offsetting bps).

**That is the only concrete differentiation stated on the entire call, and it is defensible.** It should become the spine of the demo.

### 8. Structure confirmed: parent-owned NewCo, scale-up contingent on a raise

> *"This would be a standalone company. It'd be outside of Netcoins. It'd be wholly owned by our parent company. And we would raise funds to scale it up."*

Plus Kim: *"the rates we put forward, we would structure them so it would make sense for the overarching business — because we know we want to win the flow."* **Transfer pricing will be set to optimise the group, not the NewCo.** Fine if consideration is parent equity; a problem for any NewCo-performance-linked earn-out.

## Simon's Performance

### Coaching Priorities

**1. The valuation anchor was conceded, and it was conceded on the wrong basis.**
Alek: *"Given the no-revenue part, I'm not exactly sure what they're going to say. So — nothing. I guess you could say we're open to an offer."* The prep doc's line was almost right — the damage is the clause before it, which accepts revenue as the valuation frame for an asset nobody is buying for its revenue.
→ **Better language:** *"We haven't set a number, and honestly we'd rather see how you're thinking about structure first. What's on the table is a working stack, a clean cap table with no investors to clear, and two operators — and that's worth different things depending on what you want us to run."*
→ **Why it matters:** it declines to anchor, refuses the revenue frame, and re-lists the assets on the way past. This is Simon's to correct in writing before the LOI, since it was Alek's line on a joint call.

**2. "Engineering is not the bottleneck in my opinion" — said to the person deciding what Simon is worth.**
Fraser's reply was *"That's good. Music to my ears."* It is true and generous, and it was the wrong moment. Simon then spent five minutes proving the opposite — Dakota's SWIFT misrepresentation, corridor failures, Bridge's $2M minimums, Circle's mint/burn thresholds — without ever connecting it to his own value.
→ **Better language:** *"Engineering isn't the bottleneck — the bottleneck is knowing which integrations actually hold up in production and which vendors will string you along for six months before telling you they can't do the corridor. That's the year we spent, and it's the part you can't hire quickly."*
→ **Why it matters:** same modesty about code, converts it into scarce operating knowledge, and it is exactly what Kim was fishing for with the differentiation question.

**3. The prepared point of view never fired.**
The know-your-agent / BitRank thesis — the one differentiated thing on the prep sheet, §7 — was never said. Fraser *volunteered* the forensics business (*"we actually own an on-chain forensics business"*) and Simon replied *"super helpful, yeah."* That was the opening, and it went past.
→ **Better:** *"That's more interesting than it sounds. Real-time address scoring is the primitive 'know your agent' needs — who is this agent acting for, what is it authorised to spend, and can you prove it to a regulator. That's the part of agentic payments nobody has, and you already own it."*
→ **Why it matters:** it is the one story on Fraser's shopping list none of his other targets can tell, it costs him nothing, and it activates a C$2.26M orphan. **Still available at the demo.**

### What Worked

- **The roadmap question was the highest-yield move on the call.** *"What does the stablecoin product look like at 90 days, six months, a year, two years?"* produced the entire 90–120 day plan, the second technology asset, the liquidity-agnostic strategy, the end state, and the standalone-parent-owned structure. One question, most of the prep doc's §4 and §5.

Fraser's immediate response — *"if we brought a banking partner to the table, conceivably we could get this thing rolling"* — is the reframe the prep doc wanted, produced by Simon rather than argued for.
- **The corridors-and-minimums answer** was the only concrete competitive differentiation stated by anyone, and it directly answered the hardest question asked. It should be the spine of the demo.
- **Zero revenue was conceded without flinching, and it cost nothing.** The judgment call was right.
