Onboarding and KYC — customer-of-record

RebelFi × BIGG Digital AssetsDraft for discussion · v2 · 23 August 2026
Prepared by RebelFi

In both onboarding models the licensed entity is customer-of-record for every money leg, and identity data and money travel on separate paths. RebelFi receives a verification result and a reference ID, not documents. Screening on every flow is provided by Blockchain Intelligence Group. Grey dotted arrows are RebelFi instructions.

Model 1 · Direct business (B2B) — treasury, merchant or B2B payer is the customer
  1. 1Business submits entity documents, beneficial owners and authorised signatories to the licensed entity's KYB programme KYC.
  2. 2Result + reference ID to RebelFi, which records the approved risk policy: thresholds, protocol whitelist, permitted counterparties.
  3. 3Ongoing monitoring and periodic re-verification by the licensed entity; transaction screening by Blockchain Intelligence Group KYT.
  4. 4Fiat legs: the licensed entity is customer-of-record C MTL.
  5. 5USDC delivered to business-controlled wallet(s) N.
  6. 6Policy engine gates every outbound instruction against the recorded policy.
?8 large business customers: Balance directly, or via Netcoins USA for OTC liquidity
Model 2 · Partner's end users (B2B2B, pass-through) — a payment network, fintech or marketplace brings its own users
  1. 1Partner completes KYB and is approved as an introducing partner under the licensed entity's programme.
  2. 2Partner collects end-user KYC to the licensed entity's standard KYC.
  3. 3Passed through via API; end users become customers of the licensed entity.
  4. 4Per-user result + reference ID to RebelFi, mapped to partner sub-accounts under the partner's risk policy.
  5. 5End-user fiat legs: licensed entity is customer-of-record, partner is introducer C MTL. The partner is not a money transmitter.
  6. 6USDC to partner-controlled (omnibus + sub-ledger) or per-user wallets N.
  7. 7End users transact in the partner's interface; RebelFi routes; the licensed entity settles.

On-ramp and off-ramp — the licensed legs

RebelFi × BIGG Digital AssetsDraft for discussion · v2 · 23 August 2026
Prepared by RebelFi

The two ends of every flow are the only legs that require a money-transmission licence. Fiat enters and leaves through a virtual account at the bank partner; Balance converts and transmits; USDC is minted or redeemed with Circle. Everything between the two ends is on-chain.

Customer sees
1Funds the virtual account by ACH or wire
2USDC arrives in the wallet
3To exit, sends USDC to the off-ramp address
4USD arrives in the bank account
On-ramp · USD → USDC
  1. 1Customer requests an on-ramp; RebelFi records amount and destination wallet.
  2. 2RebelFi instructs Balance with the customer reference ID from onboarding.
  3. 3Customer sends USD to their virtual account at the bank partner C.
  4. 4Balance converts and transmits MTL.
  5. 5USDC minted with Circle (directly or via Netcoins USA).
  6. 6USDC delivered to the customer wallet N.
  7. 7Balance confirms; RebelFi updates the customer ledger.
Off-ramp · USDC → USD
  1. 1Customer requests an off-ramp to a registered bank account.
  2. 2RebelFi instructs Balance; Balance issues a deposit address.
  3. 3Customer sends USDC to the deposit address; Balance now custodies C.
  4. 4Balance redeems USDC for USD with Circle.
  5. 5USD lands in the customer's virtual account.
  6. 6Bank partner pays out to the customer's bank account MTL.
  7. 7Balance confirms; RebelFi closes the request.

Yield on idle balances — no licensed leg

RebelFi × BIGG Digital AssetsDraft for discussion · v2 · 23 August 2026
Prepared by RebelFi

Idle USDC is deployed into whitelisted protocols under a per-customer risk policy. Funds move from the customer's wallet into on-chain positions the customer owns. No entity takes custody and no licensed leg is involved.

Customer sees
1Sets a risk policy, or accepts the partner default
2Idle balance is deployed within policy
3Accrual is visible; withdraw at any time
Deployment and withdrawal
  1. 1Customer or partner sets the risk policy; for B2B2B, the partner sets defaults per sub-account.
  2. 2RebelFi evaluates idle balance against thresholds and the whitelist.
  3. 3Proposed deployment goes to the wallet owner for approval, or executes automatically within pre-approved limits.
  4. 4USDC moves into a protocol position owned by the wallet N.
  5. 5Accrual is tracked and attributed per wallet or sub-account.
  6. 6RebelFi monitors venue risk and rebalances only within the policy.
  7. 7On withdrawal, USDC returns to the wallet.
Policy options
OptionEffect
Approval thresholdsAmounts above the threshold require explicit approval
Protocol whitelistOnly named venues are eligible
Insured depositsRestricts to venues with deposit cover
Sharia-compliantRestricts to compliant venues and structures
Partner defaultsApplied across a partner's sub-accounts (B2B2B)
Treasury modeBusiness balances swept above a floor, returned on demand

Custody along the flow

StageFunds held by
Idle balanceCustomer / partner wallet N
DeployedOn-chain position owned by the wallet N
RebelFiInstructions only A

Notes

Yield is a feature applied to balances that already sit in a wallet; it does not change who holds the funds. Partner-level sweeps use the same sequence with the partner wallet as the owner.

Escrow payments — conditional release on-chain

RebelFi × BIGG Digital AssetsDraft for discussion · v2 · 23 August 2026
Prepared by RebelFi

The payer funds a permissionless escrow contract; funds release to the payee only when pre-set conditions are met. While funds are in escrow, neither RebelFi nor any licensed entity can move them. Fiat entry and exit use the on-ramp and off-ramp legs.

Customer sees
1Payer creates a payment with terms
2Funds are locked in escrow
3Conditions met: payee is paid
4Not met by expiry: payer is refunded
Lock, release, refund
  1. 1Payer sets amount, payee and release conditions.
  2. 2RebelFi deploys the escrow with those parameters; the contract is permissionless once live.
  3. 3Payer locks USDC in the contract N.
  4. 4RebelFi monitors the conditions and submits attestations where it is the designated source.
  5. 5Contract releases to the payee when conditions are met.
  6. 6Contract refunds the payer on expiry or per the dispute outcome.

Agentic payments — policy-bound agent accounts

RebelFi × BIGG Digital AssetsDraft for discussion · v2 · 23 August 2026
Prepared by RebelFi

An AI agent pays from a policy-bound account owned by a KYC'd business. The owner sets each agent's spending limit and the account enforces it on-chain, so a mistaken or compromised agent cannot overspend. Agents never become customers: the owner is customer-of-record, the agent is an operator on the owner's account, and fiat legs run through the licensed entity as on any other flow. Compatible with agent payment rails such as x402.

Customer sees
1Business creates an agent account and sets a limit per agent
2Agent pays within its limit; over-limit attempts are rejected by the account
3Payments for work that takes time sit in escrow until delivered
4Idle balances earn yield under the owner's policy
Authority, intent, enforcement
  1. 1Owner creates the account, adds each agent as an operator with its own spending limit, and can pause all agents at once.
  2. 2Agent forms a payment intent: amount and payee.
  3. 3RebelFi builds the unsigned transaction; the agent checks that it matches the intent before signing.
  4. 4Agent signs on its own machine and submits. RebelFi never holds agent keys.
  5. 5Owner funds the account; on-ramp as on page 2.
  6. 6The account enforces the limit: within limit the payment executes; over limit it is rejected by the chain, not by the agent N.
  7. 7Payment lands with the payee directly, or in escrow for work that takes time (page 4).

Rails such as x402 define how an agent pays a service. The account defines whether it may, and how much. The rail requests; the account authorises within the owner's limits; screening applies as on any flow.

Cross-chain transfers and clean wallets — CCTP

RebelFi × BIGG Digital AssetsDraft for discussion · v2 · 23 August 2026
Prepared by RebelFi

USDC moves between chains by burn-and-mint through Circle's CCTP; no custodian holds funds in flight. A clean wallet (also known as a ring-fenced wallet) accepts only balances whose source passes screening, so everything inside it has a known origin.

Customer sees
1Chooses the destination chain
2USDC is burned on the source chain
3USDC is minted on the destination chain, within minutes
Burn, attest, mint
  1. 1Wallet owner requests a transfer to another chain.
  2. 2USDC is burned on the source chain N.
  3. 3Circle issues an attestation for the burn.
  4. 4RebelFi relays the attestation to the destination chain.
  5. 5USDC is minted on the destination chain N.
  6. 6For clean-wallet destinations, the source is screened by Blockchain Intelligence Group before the balance is accepted KYT.
Clean wallets

A clean wallet is a customer- or partner-owned wallet with entry rules enforced by RebelFi. It is used where a counterparty, auditor or regulator needs assurance that every balance has a known source.

RuleEffect
Accepted sourcesOn-ramp output, identified counterparties, transfers from other clean wallets
Screening at entryInbound balances are checked by Blockchain Intelligence Group before acceptance; failures are rejected or quarantined KYT
Source trailOrigin recorded per unit for audit and reporting
OutboundUnrestricted; the wallet owner controls keys N

Cross-border Canada ↔ United States — two licensed legs, one stablecoin leg

RebelFi × BIGG Digital AssetsDraft for discussion · v2 · 23 August 2026
Prepared by RebelFi

A Canadian-dollar payment settles in US dollars by passing through both licensed entities with a stablecoin leg between them. Each entity handles its own domestic leg; the stablecoin leg in between has no custodian. The reverse runs the same sequence: Balance sends, Netcoins Canada pays out in CAD.

Customer sees
1Canadian sender funds CAD and names the US recipient
2USD arrives in the recipient's account
3Reverse: US sender funds USD, CAD arrives in Canada
Canada → United States
  1. 1Sender creates the payment; RebelFi resolves the recipient to a US payout.
  2. 2RebelFi instructs both licensed entities with matching reference IDs.
  3. 3Sender funds CAD with Netcoins Canada C MTL.
  4. 4Netcoins Canada converts CAD to USDC and sends it on-chain; CCTP if the chains differ.
  1. 5Balance receives the USDC C.
  2. 6Balance redeems to USD into the recipient-side virtual account MTL.
  3. 7Bank partner pays out to the US recipient's bank account.

Custody along the flow

StageFunds held by
CAD legNetcoins Canada C
Stablecoin legNone in flight; wallet-to-wallet N
USD legBalance, then bank partner C

Notes

The FX rate is set at the CAD → USDC conversion. Timing is dominated by the two domestic legs; the stablecoin leg settles in minutes.
