## Key Intelligence / What Changed

### 1. Fraser conceded the entity-acquisition structure — but the tax premise was not tested

Fraser answered the central ask with: *“We could do it the other way as well”* and later *“I can get this updated.”* That is the strongest movement in the call: the asset purchase is not a fixed commercial condition.

However, Alek argued that BIGG may have chosen the asset structure because it misunderstood Simon's residence and foreign ownership. The LOI itself says the asset structure addresses deficient corporate records and historic tax/cross-border liability, not Colombia alone. **Inference, high confidence:** Fraser accepted the requested outcome without engaging the real legal rationale. The revision must be reviewed on mechanics, not trusted from the verbal concession.

### 2. The founders' role remains unresolved despite the call ending “on the same page”

Asked twice whether Simon and Alek would run RebelFi, Fraser said it was *“something for all of us to figure out together”* and that they would be *“part of visioning and designing.”* He did not assign P&L ownership, product authority, hiring authority, roadmap control or a reporting line.

**Fact:** collaboration was confirmed. **Inferred, low confidence:** founder control or even day-to-day leadership. Cheapest test at the demo: *“Who is the accountable operator of RebelFi after close, and what decisions do Simon and Alek own without approval?”*

### 3. The product thesis is converging around payments infrastructure; yield is a feature

Fraser described a standalone RebelFi stablecoin/payments company serving cross-border B2B, merchant payments, gaming/gambling and possibly agentic x402/L402 use cases. Netcoins would plug in liquidity, identity and services. Yield sits on idle merchant/customer balances as a differentiator, not as the core product.

This validates the July read, but the build is not ready to scope: BIGG is still evaluating an unnamed merchant-payments code asset and a Canadian custodian/trust company with US MTLs. **So what:** the demo should reveal capability and force a scope decision; it should not volunteer a build commitment before those dependencies and the budget are known.

### 4. Utila is the likely payments wallet/orchestration layer; Fireblocks is considered too heavy

Fraser said Netcoins uses Fireblocks but finds it poor for instant-settlement payment flows and expects to start the NewCo on Utila because it is cheaper and easier to build with. This is the first concrete infrastructure preference for the post-acquisition build.

### 5. The earn-out is intended as two time/product tranches, not revenue performance

Fraser's current model is a first tranche at closing and a second when the technology is *“stood up,” “functioning,”* and mutually agreed to be live, with full consideration targeted by year-end. October 1 and December 1 were illustrative dates.

The removal of revenue milestones is favourable. The remaining risk is objective acceptance: licences, counterparties, budget and deployment are controlled substantially by BIGG. Without an outside date and acceleration, the “simple” second tranche can still become a buyer-controlled earn-out.

### 6. BIGG is not offering transaction-expense coverage and suggested its own CFO can explain the definitive agreement

Fraser preserved the LOI's each-side-bears-own-costs position. He said Kim Evans could walk the founders through the definitive agreement and that independent review might only take “a couple” of legal hours. BIGG's explanation is useful but is not independent advice on tax, representations, warranties, indemnities or personal exposure. This is a substantive negotiation and governance risk, not an administrative detail.
