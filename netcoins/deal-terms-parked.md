## 0. Negotiation Thesis

### 0.1 Why Surge is buying rather than building — and what that says about price

Fraser can build this. He said so without meaning to: *"we think we could do that in the first, I don't know, 120 days — Kim, I think we've got all the components."* That sentence describes **Surge's** assets, not RebelFi's: a CTO-led dev team, a second technology asset for swaps and merchant payments, Netcoins' rails and liquidity, a dedicated OTC team, an existing client base and a lined-up LatAm client.

So the build option is real and permanent. What it does **not** give him:

| What the acquisition buys that a build does not | Why it matters to him |
|---|---|
| **A transaction to announce** | You cannot press-release an incorporation. Rename → consolidation → **acquisition** → financing → first customer is a news-flow sequence, and Canaccord raises against an acquired asset, not an intention |
| **Better accounting** | A build is an **expense line paid in cash**. An acquisition is a **capitalised intangible paid in shares**. Kim, unprompted: *"there's a lot of pressure to be profitable at all times, so you can't load a whole bunch of costs into the business."* Buying is cheaper than building in the only two scarce currencies — cash and reported profit |
| **Product and business capability** | His team is *pure engineering*. Hiring a stablecoin-payments product lead and a BD lead is 3–6 months and C$300–400K/yr **in cash**. Acquiring two is a share issuance |
| **Two-week diligence** | No MTLs to transfer, no contracts to novate, no employees, no cash to true up, no litigation. For a COO whose world is regulatory friction, zero regulatory surface is unusually cheap to absorb |
| **A cap table that can accept his currency** | See 0.2 — the strongest item on the list |

**What the code actually buys: months, not quarters**, and specifically the yield/orchestration middle layer — not the ramps, not the payments. Fraser called the platform *"stagnant"* and said *"we can take it on together and work on it with our other teams."* **Do not price the code as the asset.**

### 0.2 Where the leverage is — and where it is not

**Not leverage, despite feeling like it:**

- **The code.** He has a dev team and a second technology asset. It is an input.
- **The customer.** Disclosed as zero, twice. He did not blink.
- **Indispensability.** He can build. That option never disappears — and it **strengthens every week diligence runs**, because the dev team is building throughout. Leverage of the form "we have code you don't" decays. Move efficiently; do not slow-play.

**Real leverage, ranked:**

1. **The clean shell.** ~8 conversations since March, **zero closed.** The named failures explain why: HashRails *"too big"*; Lumx raised US$3.4M from BTG Pactual, CMT Digital, Bitso, Nomad and Antler — **investors who cannot swap into a C$0.06 microcap trading C$1,560/day.** His currency is illiquid paper and almost no cap table can accept it. RebelFi is two people, no SAFEs, no notes, no board, no consents, no ROFRs, nobody to refuse the stock. **This is plausibly the entire reason RebelFi is the one getting an LOI.** *Inference, confidence medium-high. Free test: "what was missing in the ones you passed on?"*
2. **Two operators who cost more to hire than to acquire.** Shares are his cheapest currency — he owns none, it dilutes shareholders he isn't one of, and it buys the announcement his own underwater options depend on. Cash is his most expensive.
3. **Genuine willingness to walk.** 4–6 months runway, Quintel moving. The only leverage that does not depend on a read of his situation, therefore the most reliable.
4. **Simon specifically.** He requested the three-way call to meet him, twice raised unprompted whether he is in or out, noted *"he's a dev too."* Alek alone is a business co-founder with no engineering. If the deal needs Simon and Simon is the ambivalent one, **Simon is the constraint** — but only if that is true.
5. **The recap clock, if it exists.** Unasked after four calls. Caveat honestly: the 2026-07-09 AGM approved directors, KPMG and an equity plan — **no consolidation, no name change** — so the reset may be board-level (short window, real), may need a special meeting (2–3 months), or may wait a year. Test it; do not design around it.

### 0.3 The governing conclusion

> **The leverage is not on the price of RebelFi. It is on the terms of engagement.**
>
> He needs a **transaction** more than he needs the **technology**. Let the headline be easy — shares he does not own, in a stock that barely trades, are cheap for him to give and near-worthless as runway anyway. Spend the leverage where his resistance is real, because **resistance is the signal that something has value**: cash comp, a ≥24-month term, severance funded from parent treasury rather than from the financing, a ring-fenced NewCo budget, and an option grant under the **Omnibus Equity Incentive Plan ratified by shareholders on 2026-07-09** — which exists, is fresh, and nobody has mentioned.

## 0b. What Fraser Is Probably Modelling on Compensation

**Read: low-to-zero fixed cash, hourly or day-rate contracting, equity as the primary compensation, real money contingent on a financing that has not happened.** That is rational for him, consistent with everything he has said, and it is **precisely the structure that fails to deliver the one thing this deal was supposed to provide.**

### The evidence

| Signal | Source | What he infers from it |
|---|---|---|
| *"A bit of a contract agreement... **how many hours** do you want to do on this, how many hours on that, what the pay would be"* | 2026-07-29, unprompted | Hourly/day-rate. The cheapest structure available, and the one **he** named |
| *"We'd be looking at making an **equity-based** offer"* · *"we do shares"* | 07-29, 07-24 | Consideration is paper, not cash |
| *"We don't technically have employee agreements"* | Alek, 07-29, under diligence | **The most damaging answer of the call.** Two founders worked a year on this without salaries. Revealed preference: they will work for equity |
| Cost basis *"$50 to 70 grand"* | Alek, 07-24, unprompted | The valuation floor is anchored at spend, not at value |
| Revenue *"for all intents and purposes, zero"* | Both, 07-29, twice | A fairness opinion on zero revenue and two people does not produce a large number |
| Tolerance for the second business volunteered **four separate times** | 07-24, 07-29 | **Not a concession — a preference.** Two part-time contractors is the cheapest operating model for a NewCo inside a group that "can't load costs" |
| *"We would raise funds to scale it up"* | 07-29 | Marketing, sales and technology budget lives **inside a raise that has not closed** |
| *"You can't load a whole bunch of costs into the business"* | Kim, 07-29 | Group-wide profitability pressure. Cash compensation is the expensive ask |

### The likely package he has in his head

1. **Acquisition consideration** — 100% parent shares, modest, justified as *"many multiples of what you put in."* The cost-basis disclosure is doing real work against RebelFi here and cannot be undone.
2. **Ongoing** — a modest retainer or hourly draw against logged time, scaled to "part-time."
3. **Options** under the Omnibus Plan, presented as the upside.
4. **Real budget** — after the financing closes.

He will present this as generous, and by microcap convention it is. **The defect is that two of the three components are paper in a stock with ≈C$390K of annual dollar volume**, so a "generous" package can be near-zero as income for years.

### The collision nobody has named

Simon's stated reason for engaging: *drawing a salary would relieve the runway pressure.* **The structure Fraser named on the call is the one structure that does not do that.** These are on a collision course and it has not been said out loud by anyone.

### Tactical consequences

1. **Decouple the two negotiations explicitly, and early.** *"Two separate conversations — what the entity is worth, and what it costs to have us run it. I don't want to conflate them."* Bundled, he can point at the share package and pay nothing in cash.
2. **Kill the hours frame before it becomes the pricing frame.** "How many hours" → a rate → a small number. "What outcomes do you need in the first two quarters" → a scope → a retainer. Same work, different price — and it is the position Simon and Alek already agreed to hold.
3. **State the cash requirement without apology, with the affordability math.** Two modest bases ≈ 3% of Netcoins' operating base and a rounding error against a C$25M treasury. Small for him, decisive for us, true.
4. **Insist cash is parent-treasury funded, explicitly not contingent on the concurrent financing.** Otherwise the floor is only as durable as a raise at 52-week lows against 26,000 shares/day.
5. **Ask for both share pools** — acquisition consideration *and* an executive option grant struck at today's price. Low resistance, and the only line item with a 10x tail.
6. **Pre-empt the anchor.** If *"you've been doing this for free anyway"* appears: **"which is exactly why it stopped. We're not doing that again."**

## 1. The Governing Fact: The Equity Cannot Be Sold

| Metric | Value (2026-07-28) |
|---|---|
| Share price (TSXV: BIGG) | **C$0.06** · 52-wk range C$0.055–0.135 |
| Market cap | C$23.1M · 355.8M shares |
| **Average daily volume** | **26,007 shares ≈ C$1,560/day** |
| US OTC (OTCQX: BBKCF) | US$0.0421 · volume *n/a* |
| FY2025 | Rev C$12.86M (+3.5%) · net loss C$1.27M · GM 97.6% |
| **TTM revenue** | **C$10.78M — down 15.9%** |
| Treasury (last disclosed 2025-07-28) | C$24.9M = C$6.3M cash + C$18.6M crypto (65% BTC) |
| **CRA liability** | **C$3.23M owing** |
| Last known financing | C$5M brokered LIFE @ C$0.24 (Mar 2024, A.G.P. Canada) |

**The arithmetic.** Total annual dollar volume is roughly **C$390K**. A C$300K package is ~77% of a full year of all trading in the company. Post-consolidation ADV would be ~3,700 shares/day; exiting that block at a non-destructive 20% of volume takes **about three years**, and sustained selling moves the price the whole way down. The 7:1 consolidation changes price per share, not dollar volume.

**Two legal constraints beyond "a little bit of a holding period":**

- **Rule 144 is probably unavailable.** No SEC filings surfaced; Canadian issuers on OTCQX typically rely on the 12g3-2(b) exemption, so the "current public information" condition can't be met by a US holder of restricted stock. Realistic path is Reg S — sell on the TSX-V via a **Canadian** broker after the NI 45-102 four-month-and-a-day hold. A US-resident individual often can't open that account.
- **TSXV Policy 5.4 escrow is open.** Tier 2 escrow runs 36 months (10% at bulletin date, 15% every six months) and attaches to new listings including a **Change of Business**. A tuck-in likely doesn't trigger it; a tuck-in executed alongside a rename, a divestiture and a wholesale strategic repointing plausibly could. **Counsel question before signature.**

**What this does not mean.** The shares are illiquid, not worthless. The company trades at or below its last-reported treasury, owns a licensed exchange and a real forensics business, and is near operating breakeven. The posture is not contempt for the paper — it's refusing to treat it as runway.

## 4b. Role Definition — a Term of the LOI, Not an Afterthought

**Why this is now a terms item.** Across four calls and ~2.5 hours, Fraser has never defined a role, a title, a mandate, a reporting line, a deliverable or a measure. He asked Simon **no questions about capability** while running full asset diligence — cap table, IP, domicile, revenue, headcount, trademarks, litigation. **That is a title check, not an interview.** See [[deal/calls/netcoins-call-prep-2026-07-29]] §3c.

**The consequence for terms:** effort level is not a posture to adopt in advance. **It is a function of what the deal contains** — and today the deal contains nothing definable. Signing an operator-shaped obligation into a story-shaped deal is the failure mode to avoid. So the definition goes *into* the LOI.

### The checklist — put these in writing before signature

| # | Item | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The NewCo's mandate**, in one sentence, and who owns it | Determines whether this is a business or a wrapper |
| 2 | **Simon's role and scope of responsibility**, named | Currently undefined after four calls |
| 3 | **The authority attached to it** — what he decides without asking, and who can overrule | A product owner without authority is a contractor with a title |
| 4 | **Reporting line**, and who approves the roadmap | Fraser? Kim? The CTO group? Never established |
| 5 | **First two quarters' deliverables**, and who agrees them | Converts "how many hours" into a scope |
| 6 | **How success is measured** — revenue, product shipped, customers, announcements | Fraser has never said |
| 7 | **The NewCo budget**, pre-raise and post-raise, and whether it is board-approved or intent | *"We would raise funds to scale it up"* — the real budget is post-raise |
| 8 | **What happens if the concurrent financing does not close** | The floor is only as durable as an unclosed raise at 52-week lows |
| 9 | **Relationship to the existing dev team and the second technology asset** | Product owner, or manager of someone else's roadmap? |
| 10 | **Scope boundary protecting Quintel** — explicit, bounded commitment, not open-ended | The trap is operator obligations at story-level pay |

### How to read the response — this is the decision test

| Fraser's response | Reading | Correct posture |
|---|---|---|
| Engages seriously — mandate, budget, authority, measurement | **JTBD #2 is live.** They need operators | Go hard. Negotiate cash, term and budget accordingly, and execute |
| Stays vague; steers back to *"how many hours"* | **JTBD #1 confirmed.** Buying a transaction | **Do not "milk it" — price it as what it is:** a shell-and-code sale with a small, bounded consulting tail. Quintel unambiguously primary |

> **Kim Dwyer is the instrument, not Fraser.** Fraser sells the story; Kim does the work, and she was already scoping integration unprompted (*"maybe put something on paper... we'd need some work up front to get it up and running with the licensed entities"*). **A role definition that comes back from Kim with dates and integration scope is real. One that comes back from Fraser as vibes is not.**

### The posture that survives either reading

**Conviction about a scope defined before signature.** Simon's own definition of "all in" — milestone-driven, extremely efficient, not forty hours a week — is fully compatible with a story deal. What is not survivable is *"show 100% conviction and figure out the scope later."*

**Why milking it is not the alternative:** if the cash is small, the paper is the deal, and the paper only appreciates if the NewCo works. Minimum effort optimises the small component and destroys the large one — and manufactures the outcome it predicts. Illiquid ≠ worthless; **volume follows story**, and a C$23M shell that becomes a C$150M payments business trades nothing like the current chart.
