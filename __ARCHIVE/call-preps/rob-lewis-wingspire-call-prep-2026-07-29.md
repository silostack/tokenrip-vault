# Call Sheet — Rob Lewis, Wingspire Equipment Finance

## 0. The one-paragraph read

Wingspire is the **largest and best-capitalised lender Quintel has ever had on a call** — $701.3M funded in 2025, up 66%, backed by Blue Owl through Wingspire Capital, with its own ABS shelf. Rob Lewis's title (*SVP, Strategy **and** Originations*) is an ICP title and the "strategy" half means he is likelier than any buyer in the log to buy an infrastructure story rather than a lead list. **But the standard pitch does not fit this firm and will fall flat if run as written.** Their tickets are $5M–$100M+, their borrowers are PE-sponsor-owned middle-market companies, and their growth is coming from sponsor relationships plus a capital-markets desk. A ranked cold-call list of companies that filed a permit is not their problem. **The angle that fits is sponsor-side intelligence and their own back book — see §4.** Run this as discovery, not as a demo.

⚠️ **Two flags before you dial** — details in §7: Wingspire is on our own dial list's **parked** list (A17, Blue Owl-owned, longer cycle), and **Stauss names Wingspire as VFI's direct competitor.** Do not mention VFI on this call under any circumstances.

## 4. The angle — do not run the standard pitch

The standard demo shows companies that filed a permit or won a contract award, ranked for a rep making calls. Wingspire's originators are not making those calls, and the Tesla→landscaper story — our best 40 seconds — points at exactly the wrong end of the market for them. On the Capteris call four weeks ago, that story was told to a $1M+-ticket buyer and it undersold us.

**Three angles that do fit. Lead with the first.**

### 4a. Sponsor-side intelligence (the primary angle)

Their thesis is PE sponsors. So point the inference layer at sponsors rather than borrowers:

> *"You cover sponsors, not companies. So the signal that matters to you isn't a permit — it's a sponsor closing an add-on in a portfolio company that's about to carry a capex programme, or a fund coming up on hold-period pressure with an asset that needs re-equipping before exit. That's assemblable from the public record: fund filings, portfolio-company announcements, contract awards, permits at the portfolio-company level. We can watch the sponsors you cover and tell your team which portfolio companies are heating up, before the sponsor's CFO starts calling banks."*

**Why this is credible and not a stretch:** the strongest published backtest case is exactly this shape — Revere←Eos, where the lead lived in the *customer's own SEC filings* ("resin named, molder missing"). That's a third-order inference from a counterparty disclosure, which is the same mechanic a sponsor-portfolio watch requires. Lead the case studies with that one if proof comes up.

### 4b. Their own back book (Capability 1, and the exclusivity answer)

450+ obligors, $3B+ funded, and every secured deal leaves a UCC-1 naming them. Equipment has known useful lives.

> *"You've financed 450-plus obligors. Every one of those filings is public, dated, and tied to an asset with a replacement cycle. Nobody else can be sold which of **your** deals are coming up for replacement — that's not a promise we're making, it's a fact about where the data comes from."*

This is the dial, not the switch. It has a positive floor, it's provable, and it doubles as the exclusivity answer (§6).

### 4c. The direct platform as the beachhead

They invested in a "national direct lending platform" and credited it for 66% growth. Whoever staffs that platform is doing exactly what the product is for. **Find out who they are and how many.** If the answer is "we're hiring into it," that's the headcount frame landing on a receptive ear:

> *"You're building the direct platform out. The research half of an entry-level BDO, per rep, that never sleeps — that's the budget line this sits in, not a data subscription."*

## 5. The three things to LEARN (70% extract / 30% explain)

1. **"Roughly how does the $700M split between sponsor relationships, the direct platform, and capital markets — and who works the direct side day to day?"**
   This decides whether there is a desk to sell to at all. If direct is a rounding error and everything comes through sponsors and Tom Murray's programmatic partnerships, the product as built has no user. Ask it in the first five minutes, before anything is shown.

2. **"When your team decides which sponsors and which portfolio companies to go after this quarter, what does that process actually look like today?"**
   Open, specific, past-tense-able. This is where the pain is if pain exists. Follow with the one question that has produced every real pain quote in the log: ***"Walk me through the last deal you found out about too late."***

3. **"Who signs for something like this, and what's the process — is it you, Ryan, or does it go up through Wingspire Capital?"**
   Not "is this your decision." Eleven of our eighteen EF calls were run with a non-decision-maker; at a Blue Owl portfolio company there may also be procurement, InfoSec and vendor review sitting behind the yes. Better to know on call one.

**Bonus, if it fits naturally:** *"You came from Dext — very different end of the market. What surprised you most moving up-ticket?"* Real question, gets him talking, and tells you how he thinks about sourcing at both scales.

## 6. Objections most likely on THIS call

### 🔴 The coverage question, inverted — expect it, and it's the one we've failed before
Not *"do you reach small companies"* but **"do you have anything at my scale?"** Paul Gentile asked exactly this four weeks ago ($100M+ revenue, $1M+ tickets), the filter never loaded, and the most important question of that call went unanswered.

> *"Fair question and I'd rather show you than argue it. Give me your box — sponsor-backed, $5M ticket floor, your sectors — and I'll come back with the actual named companies it surfaces. If it's thin at your end of the market, you'll hear that from me first."*

**If you demo at all, pre-filter to their band before you share the screen.** Do not let him watch a spinner, and do not show a landscaper.

### 🔴 Exclusivity / "aren't you selling this to every lender?" — handle with extreme care here
The house answer only. **Never answer with who our other customers are** — the segment-separation answer given at Ameris last week is falsified by two live deals, and here the exposure is worse, because our design partner competes with this firm.

> *"Two lenders pointed at the same public record don't get the same list — it ranks against your box, your history, your funded book. And your back book is yours alone: nobody else can be sold which of your 450 obligors are coming up for replacement. If category exclusivity in a defined segment matters, that's a premium tier we can price."*

### 🟡 "How many customers do you have?"
Answer honestly — two live, ten onboarding, founding terms — **and do not name the live one.** If he presses for a reference, offer to arrange one *after* checking with the customer. That is both true and the correct hygiene here.

### 🟡 "How many deals would this realistically surface for us?"
This is the big-ticket version of the hit-rate question and it's the second question after it, every time.

> *"At your ticket size I'm not going to pretend it's a volume play. One net-new funded deal in your band pays for this for years — the honest framing is asymmetric: cheap misses, very large hits. What I'd want to measure is how many of the companies we surface in your sectors you'd have gotten to at all this quarter."*

### 🟡 "We have a capital markets team and plenty of data"
This is the make-vs-buy objection wearing an institutional suit — 4th occurrence in the log, and now from firms with real resources. Diagnose before you argue:

> *"What are they using today, and where does it break? The scrape is the easy 20%. Matching the same company across a permit, a UCC filing and a portfolio-company announcement when the name's spelled three different ways is the part that eats months, and it's most of what we've spent six months on."*

### 🟢 "How is the intel sourced?"
Be precise and volunteer it — SEC filings and the 8-Ks that name private counterparties, USAspending contract awards, UCC lien maturities for refi timing, permits, utility interconnection queues. **Never confirm his guess about a source.** ("Hoover's" was accepted on the Capteris call and became that buyer's stated top reason for interest; it's wrong.)

## 8. The close — what to ask for

**Do not ask for a quote, a deck, or a follow-up "when you've had a chance to think."** Every flat deal in the log has a next step that costs the prospect nothing.

**Ask for the sponsor list:**

> *"Here's what I'd like to do rather than send you a brochure. Give me the ten or fifteen PE sponsors you most want to be in front of, plus your ticket floor and sectors. I'll come back with what's actually moving inside those portfolios — named companies, dated public evidence, and why each one is likely carrying capex. If it's thin, I'll tell you it's thin. Does [specific day, 9 days out] work to walk through it?"*

Why this one:
- It costs him five minutes and real thought, which is the qualification test
- It is the only artifact we can produce that no list vendor can, and it's built entirely from public data we already ingest
- It converts the coverage objection into a delivered commitment before he raises it
- It puts a **date** on the calendar, which the majority of first calls in the log failed to do

**Fallback if he won't give a sponsor list:** the buy box alone — ticket floor, sectors, geography. That's what Kevin/Civista handed over in thirty seconds after being asked, and nobody ran it.
